Judah al-Ḥarizi, Taḥkemoni

The Hebrew maqamat, with an English translation line by line · ספר תחכמוני

Judah al-Ḥarizi (about 1165 – 1225) wrote the Taḥkemoni, fifty gates of Hebrew rhymed prose with poems set into it. The narrator, Heman the Ezrahite, meets again and again the wandering poet Ḥever the Kenite. The book was the model for Immanuel of Rome's Maḥberot. This site shows the Hebrew and the English side by side, one row for each line.

Method

  • Lines. The Hebrew edition ends each rhyme unit of the rhymed prose with a full stop. Each unit is one row. Each line of verse is one row, with its two halves set apart.
  • Prose. The English is cadenced prose. Rhyme is kept only where English gives it without added words.
  • Poems. The English is literal, line for line. It does not keep the meter or the rhyme.
  • Allusions. Al-Ḥarizi builds his sentences from biblical phrases. References in gray brackets identify them. They are the translator's identifications and are not yet checked against a concordance.
  • Uncertain renderings have the mark ⚠ and a note at the end of the page.
  • Translator. The translation is by Claude (Anthropic), directed and reviewed by Ezra Brand.
