שַׁעַר אַרְבָּעִים וְתִשְׁעָה

בְּשֶּׁבַח פֵּרוֹת אִילָנֵי הַגָּן

The Forty-Ninth Gate

A spring wind leads Heman into a garden, where he praises its fruits one by one in short poems, then finds Ḥever the Kenite among a company of young poets

Gate 49 on Project Ben-Yehuda ↗

About this text

203 lines. One row is one rhyme unit of the rhymed prose, or one line of verse (its two halves set apart). References in [gray brackets] are the translator's identifications of biblical phrases. ⚠ marks an uncertain rendering. Each line number is a link to that line. Hebrew text: Project Ben-Yehuda.

1

Thus says Heman the Ezrahite: My soul longed, yes, it fainted [Ps 84:3],

נְאֻם הֵימָן הָאֶזְרָחִי: נִכְסְפָה וְגַם כָּלְתָה נַפְשִּׁי.

2

in the days of my delight and my ease⚠1,

בִּימֵי תַעֲנוּגִי וְנַפְשִׁי.

3

when I was like a wild ass running free [Job 39:5],

וּבִהְיוֹתִי כְפֶרֶא חָפְשִׁי.

4

to take my pleasure in the shade of the gardens,

לְהִתְעַלֵּס בְּצִלְלֵי הַגַּנִּים.

5

and to feed among the lilies [Song 2:16].

וְלִרְעוֹת בַּשּׁוֹשַׁנִּים.

6

And it came to pass one day that I rose early, before the lights of heaven rose,

וַיְהִי בִקְצָת הַיָּמִים הִשְׁכַּמְתִּי בְּטֶרֶם יַשְׁכִּימוּ הַמְּאוֹרִים.

7

and I awoke the dawn [Ps 57:9],

וְעוֹרַרְתִּי שְׁחָרִים.

8

and went out into the villages [Song 7:12],

וְיָצָאתִי בַכְּפָרִים.

9

to take my delight in love [Prov 7:18]

לְהִתְעַלֵּס בַּאֲהָבִים.

10

beneath the shade of the willows.

תַּחַת צִלְלֵי הָעֲרָבִים.

11

And behold, a wind of the dawn sought out my face,

וְהִנֵּה רוּחַ שְׁחָרִים פָּנַי מְשַׁחֶרֶת.

12

revealing to me a hidden love [Prov 27:5],

תְּגַלֶּה לִי אַהֲבָה מְסֻתֶּרֶת.

13

and speaking without a tongue.

וּבְלִי לָשׁוֹן מְדַבֶּרֶת.

14

It called me to peace,

קָרְאָה לִי לְשָׁלוֹם.

15

and said to me: "Come near, hither.

וְאָמְרָה לִי: גְּשָׁה נָא עַד הֲלֹם.

16

I will pour out secrets to you,

אַבִּיעַ סוֹדוֹת אֵלֶיךָ.

17

and lay the spirit of my love upon you [Num 11:17].

וְאָצַלְתִּי רוּחַ אַהֲבָתִי עָלֶיךָ.

18

Behold, I have brought you of the fruit of my ground [Deut 26:10],

וְהִנִּה הֵבֵאתִי לְךָ מִפְּרִי אַדְמָתִי.

19

and of the first of my produce,

וּמֵרֵאשִׁית תְּבוּאָתִי.

20

the scent of my garden bed,

רֵיחַ עֲרוּגָתִי.

21

and the spice of my powders [Song 3:6].

וְרֶקַח אַבְקָתִי.

22

Take, I pray you, my blessing [Gen 33:11].

קַח נָא אֶת בִּרְכָתִי.

23

And if you turn aside to me, to take delight at the windows of my shade,

וְאִם תָּסוּר אֵלַי לְהִתְעַדֵּן בְּאֶשְׁנַבֵּי צְלָלַי.

24

and by the wells of my streams,

וּבְמַשְאַבֵּי נְחָלַי.

25

you will drink your fill of my flowing dews,

תִּרְוֶה בְנוֹזְלֵי טְלָלַי.

26

and you shall be near me [Gen 45:10].

וְהָיִּיתָ קָרוֹב אֵלַי.

27

And like a handmaid I will be betrothed to you [Lev 19:20],

וְכַשִּׁפְחָה אֵחָרֵף אֵלֶיךָ.

28

and I will send portions from before me to you [Gen 43:34]."

וְאֶשָּׂא מַשְׂאוֹת מֵאֵת פָּנַי אֵלֶיךָ.

29

When I heard her word,

וּכְשָׁמְעִי אִמְרָתָהּ.

30

I rejoiced in her joy and in her tidings,

שָׂמַחְתִּי בְשִׂמְחָתָהּ וּבְשׂוֹרָתָהּ.

31

and I said to my heart: "Now run, I pray you, to meet her [2 Kgs 4:26],

וְאָמַרְתִּי אֶל לִבִּי עַתָּה רוּץ נָא לִקְרָאתָהּ.

32

to see whether her words prove true [Gen 42:20],

לִרְאוֹת הֲיֵאָמְנוּ דְבָרֶיהָ.

33

and her sayings are right."

וְיִצְדְּקוּ אֲמָרֶיהָ.

34

And as she had vowed, so she did,

וְכַאֲשֶׁר נָדְרָה כֵּן עָשְׂתָה.

35

and as I had imagined, so it was [Isa 14:24].

וְכַאֲשֶׁר דִּמִּיתִי כֵּן הָיְתָה.

36

For she brought me to a garden,

כִּי הֱבִיאַתְנִי לְגִנָּה.

37

fat and fresh [Ps 92:15].

דְּשֵׁנָה וְרַעֲנַנָּה.

38

Its shrubs were intertwined,

הִסְתַּבְּכוּ שִׂיחֶיהָ.

39

and its plants clung together,

וְהִתְלַכְּדוּ צְמָחֶיהָ.

40

with all kinds of the yield of its months [Deut 33:14],

וּמִינֵי גֶרֶשׁ יְרָחֶיהָ.

41

and its knobs and its flowers [Exod 25:31].

וְכַפְתּוֹרֶיהָ וּפְרָחֶיהָ.

42

And the cheek of its blossoms

וּלְחִי נִצָּנֶיהָ.

43

was like the cheek of its youth,

כִּלְחִי עֲלוּמֶיהָ.

44

set in settings [Exod 28:20] of embroidered work, and the garden bed was wrapped in the garment of its delights,

מְשֻׁבָּצִים מַעֲשֵׂה רִקְמָה וְהָעֲרוּגָה מִתְעַטֶּפֶת בְּבֶגֶד עֲדָנֶיהָ.

45

and in settings of its scarlet⚠2.

וּמִשְׁבְּצוֹת שָׁנֶיהָ.

46

And he took up his parable and said:

וַיִּשָּׂא מְשָׁלוֹ וַיֹּאמַר:

47

Its flowers shine, until I thought them like lamps on the tops of the highest boughs [Isa 17:6].

פְּרָחֶיהָ יְאִירוּן עַד חֲשַׁבְתִּים כְּמוֹ נֵרוֹת בְּרָאשֵׁי הָאֲמִירִים.

48

And when the cloud comes down and presses to put them out, they shine all the more.

וְעַל שַׁחַק אֲשֶׁר יֵרֵד וְיִפְצַר לְכַבּוֹתָם וְהֵם יוֹתֵר מְאִירִים.

49

And in the midst of the garden were streams of water [Isa 44:4],

וּבְתוֹךְ הַגִּנָּה יִבְלֵי מַיִם.

50

and a place of rivers, of streams broad on both hands [Isa 33:21],

וּמְקוֹם נְהָרִים יְאוֹרִים רַחֲבֵי יָדַיִם.

51

and their waters poured down the slope [Mic 1:4]

וּמֵימֵיהֶם נִגָּרִים בַּמּוֹרָד.

52

like a necklace of moist crystal, scattered and dispersed.

כַּעֲנַק בְּדֹלַח לַח מְפֻזָּר וּמְפֹרָד.

53

And the poet answered and said:

וַיַּעַן הַמְשׁוֹרֵר וַיֹּאמַר:

54

Channels come down over a pavement of fire, and pour like my tears down the slope.

פְּלָגִים יוֹרְדִים עַל רִצֲפַת אֵשׁ וְנִגָּרִים כְּמוֹ דִמְעִי בְּמוֹרָד.

55

And the drops of the streams, as they come down, are like moist crystal, scattered and dispersed.

וְנִטְפֵי נוֹזְלִים יִדְמוּ בְרִדְתָּם בְּדֹלַח לַח מְפֻזָּר וּמְפֹרָד.

56

And over the wells

וְעַל הַמַּשְׁאַבִּים.

57

were basins and lattices,

גֻּלּוֹת וְאֶשְנַבִּים.

58

and around the channels

וּסְבִיב הַתְּעָלוֹת.

59

tents of aloes wood⚠3,

אֹהָלִים מֵעֲצֵי אֲהָלוֹת.

60

and between the basins, upper chambers,

וּבֵין גֻּלּוֹת עִלִּיּוֹת.

61

castles most fair [Jer 46:20],

טִירוֹת יְפֵה-פִיּוֹת.

62

and chambers of imagery [Ezek 8:12].

וְחַדְרֵי מַשְׂכִּיּוֹת.

63

And around the well-ordered garden beds

וּסְבִיב הָעֲרוּגוֹת הָעֲרוּכוֹת.

64

were troughs of water and pools,

שִׁקֲתוֹת מַיִם וּבְרֵכוֹת.

65

and around them buds and flowers,

סְבִיבָם נִצָּנִים וּפְרָחִים.

66

and towers of perfumes [Song 5:13],

וּמִגְדְּלוֹת מֶרְקָחִים.

67

and all kinds of plants,

וְכָל מִינֵי צְמָחִים.

68

and a forest growing over the shrubs.

וְיַעַר צוֹמֵחַ עֲלֵי שִׂיחִים.

69

Among them were apples

מֵהֶם תַּפּוּחִים.

70

blended in the oil of grace,

בְּשֶׁמֶן הַחֵן רְקוּחִים.

71

as if plucked from a maiden's breasts,

כְּאִלּוּ מִשְּׁדֵי עַלְמָה קְטוִּפים.

72

or gathered from the cheeks of a graceful gazelle.

אוֹ מִלְּחִי צְבִי חֵן אֲסוּפִים.

73

And he took up his parable and said:

וַיִּשָּׂא מְשָׁלוֹ וַיֹּאמַר:

74

And apples, red as blood, perfumed with all the chief spices [Song 4:14].

וְתַפּוּחִים כְּמוֹ דָמִים אֲדֻמִּים מְקֻטָּרִים בְּכָל רָאשֵׁי בְשָׂמִים.

75

When a man takes them and eats them, then the lines have fallen for him in pleasant places [Ps 16:6].

בְּעֵת אִישׁ יֹאחֲזֵם וַיֹּאכְלֵם אָז חֲבָלִים נָפְלוּ לוֹ בַנְּעִימִים.

76

And beside them pomegranates,

וְאֶצְלָם רִמּוֹנִים.

77

white and ruddy [Song 5:10].

צַחִים וְאַדְמוֹנִים.

78

In them, for aching pangs,

בָּם לְצִירִים נִכְאָבִים.

79

are cures for the grieving,

רְפוּאוֹת נֶעֱצָבִים.

80

hewn from the quarry of sweetness,

מִמֶּחְצַב הַנֹּעַם נֶחְצָבִים.

81

and cut from the trees of Eden.

וּמֵעֲצֵי עֵדֶן נִקְצָבִים.

82

And every pomegranate,

וְכָל רִמּוֹן.

83

white and ruddy,

צַח וְאַדְמוֹן.

84

holds within it a throng of the daughters of beauty,

וּבְתוֹכוֹ מִבְּנוֹת הַיֳּפִי הָמוֹן.

85

whose pearls have a bell for a house⚠4,

לָהֶם מִפְּנִינֵיהֶם פַּעֲמוֹן.

86

and outside, upon them, is the dew of Hermon [Ps 133:3],

וּמִחוּץ עֲלֵיהֶם טַל חֶרְמוֹן.

87

and inside, their waters are like the waters of Dimon [Isa 15:9]⚠5,

וּמִבַּיִת מֵימֵיהֶם כְּמֵי דִימוֹן.

88

as if they were ashamed before us⚠6,

כְּאִלּוּ מִפָּנֵינוּ הָיוּ נִכְלָמִים.

89

and so they turned red,

וְעַל כֵּן שָׁבוּ אֲדֻמִּים.

90

rolled in blood [2 Sam 20:12].

מְגוֹלָלִים בְּדָמִים.

91

And he took up his parable and said:

וַיִּשָּׂא מְשָׁלוֹ וַיֹּאמַר:

92

And the pomegranate tree, fair to look on and ruddy, which sways in every wind like a reed [Isa 58:5],

וּבֶן-רִמּוֹן יְפֵה מַרְאֶה וְאַדְמוֹן אֲשֶׁר יָנוּד לְכָל-רוּחַ כְּאַגְמוֹן.

93

its pomegranates like bells around it, at every turn a golden bell and a pomegranate [Exod 28:34].

וְרִמּוֹנָיו כְּפַעְמוֹנִים סְבִיבָיו בְּכָל-עֵת פַּעֲמֹן זָהָב וְרִמּוֹן.

94

And opposite them walnuts,

וְנְגָּדם אֱגוֹזִים.

95

hidden in treasuries of sweetness,

בְּגִנְזֵי נֹעַם גְּנוּזִים.

96

wearing green garments,

לוֹבְשִׁים בְּגָדִים יְרֻקִּים.

97

and in their taste sweet to every mouth.

וּבְטַעְמָם לְכָל פֶּה מְתוּקִים.

98

And every walnut is comely in its form,

וְכָל אֱגוֹז נָאֶה בְּתַבְנִיתוֹ.

99

and lovely in its splendor,

וְנֶחְמָד בְּהַדְרָתוֹ.

100

but it is a churl [Isa 32:5] to all who seek its good,

אַךְ הוּא כִילַי לְכָל מְבַקֵּשׁ טוֹבָתוֹ.

101

and will not give you its blessing

וְלֹא יִתֵּן לְךָ בִרְכָתוֹ.

102

until you strike it again and again,

עַד תּוֹסִיף לְהַכּוֹתוֹ.

103

and drive the peg into its temple [Judg 4:21].

וְתִתְקַע אֶת הַיָּתֵד בְּרַקָּתוֹ.

104

And I took up my parable:

וָאֶשָּׂא מְשָׁלִי:

105

Walnuts wear garments of pleasantness, and on them, for beauty, a robe of embroidery.

אֱגוֹזִים לוֹבְשִׁים בִּגְדֵי נְעִימוֹת עֲלֵיהֶם לַיֳּפִי מַלְבּוּשׁ רְקָמוֹת.

106

But they stiffen their necks hard against those who seek them, until he strikes them, and then he has his revenge⚠7.

אֲבָל יַקְשׁוּ מְאֹד עָרְפָּם לְדוֹרְשָׁם עֲדֵי יַכֵּם וְאָז יִמְצָא נְקָמוֹת.

107

And before them palm trees,

וְלִפְנֵיהֶם עֲצֵי תְמָרִים.

108

with the stature of young harts,

בְּקוֹמַת עֳפָרִים.

109

set straight upon a straight line,

עַל קַו ישֶׁר מְיֻשָּׁרִים.

110

as if they had stolen from the maidens

כְּאִלּוּ גָזְלוּ מִן הָעֲלָמוֹת.

111

the beauty of their stature.

יְפִי הַקּוֹמוֹת.

112

And I took up my parable:

וָאֶשָּׂא מְשָׁלִי:

113

I said: I will climb up the palm tree [Song 7:9], and glory in the honey of its delicacies.

אָמַרְתִּי אֶעְלֶה עַל תָּמָר וּבְצוּף מַעְדַנִּיו אֶתְיַמָּר.

114

When I taste it, I take my delight, until the dripping honeycomb is bitter beside it.

עֵת אֶטְעַם אֹתוֹ אֶתְעַדֵּן עַד נֹפֶת צוּף נֶגְדּוֹ יֵמַר.

115

And when I meet a graceful doe [Prov 5:19], I say:

וּבְפִגְעִי יַעְלַת חֵן אֹמַר:

116

"Your stature is like a palm tree [Song 7:8]."⚠8

קוֹמָתֵךְ דָּמְתָה לַתָּמָר.

117

And facing them, pleasant grapes,

וְנִכְחָם עֲנָבִים עֲרֵבִים.

118

to satisfy the hungry,

לְהַשְבִּיעַ רְעֵבִים.

119

and their taste sweeter than honey and the honeycomb [Ps 19:11],

וּמִצּוּף וְנֹפֶת מָתוֹק טַעְמָם.

120

and beside them all sweet things are forgotten, and are no more remembered by their name [Hos 2:19].

וְנֶגְדָּם נִשְׁכְּחוּ כַל הַמַּמְתַּקִּים וְלֹא יִזָּכְרוּ עוֹד בִּשְׁמָם.

121

And I took up my parable:

Uncertain renderings

  • ⚠1 בִּימֵי תַעֲנוּגִי וְנַפְשִׁי — The line ends again with נַפְשִׁי ("my soul"), as the line before. Read here from the verb נָפַשׁ, "to rest, be refreshed" (Exod 31:17), for the rhyme with חָפְשִׁי. "In the days of my delight and of my soul" is also possible.
  • ⚠2 וּמִשְׁבְּצוֹת שָׁנֶיהָ — Uncertain. Read שָׁנֶיהָ as "its scarlet" (שָׁנִי, as in Prov 31:21 לְבֻשָׁה שָׁנִים), the red flowers set in the green. The pointing also allows "its years".
  • ⚠3 אֹהָלִים מֵעֲצֵי אֲהָלוֹת — A play on אֹהָלִים "tents" and אֲהָלוֹת "aloes" (Song 4:14; Num 24:5-6 has both words close together): arbors made of aloes wood.
  • ⚠4 לָהֶם מִפְּנִינֵיהֶם פַּעֲמוֹן — Literally "they have, of their pearls, a bell": the seeds are the pearls, and the bell-shaped rind holds them. Bells and pomegranates alternate on the hem of the high priest's robe (Exod 28:33-34), as in the poem below.
  • ⚠5 כְּמֵי דִימוֹן — Isa 15:9: "the waters of Dimon are full of blood". The juice is red as blood.
  • ⚠6 מִפָּנֵינוּ — Pointed מִפָּנֵינוּ, "before us". Unpointed, the same letters can be read מִפְּנִינוּ, "of our pearls", a play on the pearls (seeds) two lines before.
  • ⚠7 וְאָז יִמְצָא נְקָמוֹת — Literally "and then he finds vengeances": the one who cracks the stubborn nut is paid back for its hardness. The subject may also be the nut ("it meets its punishment").
  • ⚠8 קוֹמָתֵךְ דָּמְתָה לַתָּמָר — From Song 7:8 זֹאת קוֹמָתֵךְ דָּמְתָה לְתָמָר. Kaminka's edition prints lines 115-116 as one verse line, "וכפגעי יעלת חן אומר / זאת קומתך דמתה תמר", which continues the rhyme in -מר of the poem. Our text has two prose lines, without זאת; it is kept as it is.
  • ⚠9 לְזֹאת תָּלָה מְכוֹן עוֹלָם בְּאֶשְׁכּוֹל — Uncertain. The line rhymes אֶשְׁכּוֹל with itself. מְכוֹן עוֹלָם recalls the Temple (1 Kgs 8:13 מְכוֹן לְשִׁבְתְּךָ עוֹלָמִים), at whose entrance a golden vine with clusters was hung (m. Middot 3:8). The exact sense is not clear.
  • ⚠10 וּמִשְּׁבָרִים יִתְחַטָּאוּ — Job 41:17, of Leviathan, an obscure phrase ("by reason of breakings they purify themselves" or "they cringe at the crashing"). Here the kernels come out of the broken shell clean.
  • ⚠11 שְׁחָרִים וַעֲרָבִים — Her cheek is dawn (שְׁחָרִים) and her hair is evening (עֲרָבִים, "evenings"; compare the poem below, "her hair is night"). שחרים also suggests "black", and עֲרָבִים recalls עָרֵב, "sweet".
