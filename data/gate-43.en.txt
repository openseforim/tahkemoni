Thus says Heman the Ezrahite: I went from Ekron
to Samaria.
And there I came upon men setting in array a battle of reproofs,
and fighting a war of upright sayings.
Their words were softer than oil, yet they were drawn swords [Ps 55:22].
Among them was a man from the sparks of whose sayings the fire blazed,
and he shattered the rock with the hammer of his words [Jer 23:29].
He answered and said: "Men of understanding, hearken to me [Job 34:10],
and give ear, you princes of reason ⚠1.
Let me now put forth to you a lovely riddle [Judg 14:12],
that shall be to you for instruction and for testimony [Isa 8:20]."
And all the people gathered around him,
and fell silent for his words.
He said to them: "Know that God, the Creator of the heavens,
when He created the earth a ball, divided it in two:
half of it dry land, and half of it water.
And the sea went around the whole world,
and around all the creatures of the earth.
So the land was shut up in his prison,
and beneath his rim ⚠2,
and she was held fast within him,
as if the sea were a king and she a captive handmaid,
with no escape for her this way or that,
for on every side he subdues her and surrounds her.
And when the sea saw that he subdued the land,
and went around her like a wall,
he said in his heart: My hand is high [Deut 32:27].
The whole world was despised in his eyes,
and his thoughts rose proudly over the earth.
He answered and said: 'To me the kingship is fitting,
and my high rank is established.
All the earth is bound in the bond of my covenant [Ezek 20:37],
and all who dwell in her are in my power.
When my wrath rises against her,
the waters of my fury shall sweep her away.
She has no escape from my fetters,
and no freedom from my yoke.'
And when the sea grew great in speaking in his pride,
and lengthened his tongue,
the land clothed herself in majesty and pride [Ps 93:1],
and wrapped herself in zeal as in a cloak [Isa 59:17].
She almost rose to the height of terror,
and the earth shook and trembled [Ps 18:8].

She answered and said: 'Who is he, and where is he, whose heart has filled him [Esth 7:5] to lift himself up over me,
and to touch my hems?
He is but a treading place for my feet [Deut 2:5],
and a thong for my shoes [Gen 14:23].
I am like a king, and the seas are my chariots;
I am like a lord, and the rivers are slaves around me.
All the sons of men were created within me,
and the prophets prophesied on me.
The Torah was given on me,
and the Shekhinah dwelt on me.
But on the sea let a cloud dwell [Job 3:5],
and let no joyful shout come into it [Job 3:7].
For my sake God opens the heights,
to bring down rains of mercy,
and to pour down showers,
and to water with them the furrows [Ps 65:11],
and to make the plants spring up,
and to make the flowers bloom,
with the precious yield of the months [Deut 33:14].
On me the lights give their light,
and by me princes rule [Prov 8:16].
So why do you open wide your mouth [Isa 57:4], when you are despised and contemptible [Ps 15:4]
to all who hear and see?
Everyone who goes from me to you goes out of a house of delights,
and he is going to die [Gen 25:32].
All the days that he is tossed upon you ⚠3,
and dwells within you,
his life hangs in doubt before him [Deut 28:66],
and death stands facing him at every hour.
Even at night his heart takes no rest [Eccl 2:23],
and all the day his grief is not still.
He is not sure of his life [Deut 28:66] until he goes out from you,
and escapes from you.
And when he comes to me, then he finds rest,
and knows that there is relief [Exod 8:11].'

When the sea heard her words,
his waves tossed themselves [Jer 5:22],
and his floods stood upright as a heap [Exod 15:8].
The roar of his breakers grew strong with the voice of a storm,
and he almost swept away the land and all that fills it [Jer 47:2].
He said to the dry land: 'O earth, earth, earth [Jer 22:29], will you liken yourself to me,
and compare yourself with me [Isa 40:18]?
First of all, who am I? Am I not he who makes the shades writhe [Job 26:5],
and makes all creatures tremble?
Everyone who sees the roar of my waves
is afraid to come near me.
And had not my Maker, who brought me forth,
set the sand for my bound [Jer 5:22],
and said to me: You shall not change your place;
hitherto shall you come, and no further [Job 38:11],
my waves would have gone out over you,
and I would have swept away all your borders,
and taken away all your tin [Isa 1:25],
and broken all your ships,
and sunk your vessels in the great deep [Gen 7:11].
You would hide in my depths,
and be sought, and not be found [Ezek 26:21].
Do you not know the wonders that God did in me,
when the holy people came into my midst,
and the enemy drew near to fight them,
and Pharaoh drew near [Exod 14:10]?
When I saw their distress from their enemies,
my compassion was stirred for them.
And at the time when their foes said, "Where are they?" ⚠4,
Moses stretched out his hand over the sea [Exod 14:21];
my breakers lifted up their roaring [Ps 93:3],
my waves were split for them,
and my floods stood up like a wall [Exod 14:22].
I brought them into my midst,
and hid them among my waves,
and from the enemies I covered them in my shadow [Isa 51:16].
And when the great dragon [Ezek 29:3] came to tear their flocks,
and the Egyptians pursued after them [Exod 14:23],
I repaid him according to their deeds [Ps 28:4],
and judged him by the measure that he measured,
and swept him away in the breakers of my waves.
I brought on him a day of anguish [Ezek 7:25],
and made of him a meal for the fish of the sea.
Had the holy people stayed on the dry land,
the end would have been more bitter and harder than the beginning;
the sword of the foe would have devoured them,
and the fire of his anger would have made an end of them,
and your mountains and your deserts could not have saved them.'

Then the earth opened her mouth [Num 16:32] and said to the sea: 'Can you find an answer in your mouth?
How long will you repeat your folly [Prov 26:11]?
Is not your evil great, without a bound?
Are you not the remnant of the waters of the Flood,
the bitter waters that bring the curse [Num 5:18],
in which the creatures drowned?
Your waters are not pure and not clear,
not bright and not cold,
but mire and dregs,
salty and bitter.
They do not water the ground,
nor do they quench thirst [Ps 104:11],
and the foot of a beast does not come near you.
Whoever drinks your waters, his bowels turn over moment by moment,
until he dies of sicknesses of his bowels.
Besides this, you are cruel and violent,
treacherous and lawless.
You kill the innocent,
and put to death the needy and the poor.
How many ships you have sunk,
and how many pious men you have killed!
How many sucklings you have weaned from their mothers' breasts,
and slaughtered, and had no pity [Lam 2:21]!
If a man comes into you, he stands in danger of death,
and sees the gates of death open before him [Job 38:17].
When he sees your waters standing over him like walls,
terrors surround him,
and his thoughts mount up to the heavens and go down to the depths [Ps 107:26].
And if the winds are roused against you,
sighs are roused in his heart.
If your waves toss themselves over him,
and your floods stand upright as a heap [Exod 15:8],
woe to him who lies upon you [Prov 23:34]!
For he has his fill of great torments,
and tastes death many times.
And when he goes out from you, he sets out praise and blessing to God,
who saved him from the overthrow,
and from the wrath poured out [Ezek 20:33].'

The sea said to the dry land: 'The great number of your words
is a sign of your brazen face [Prov 7:13].
If you would only keep silent [Job 13:5], you would hide your shame,
but with your many words you lay bare your sin [Prov 10:19].
Do you not know that you are the abode of transgressions,
and the house of ugly deeds,
and the seat of the cruel and the violent,
and a den of lawless men?
All who dwell in you are men of wickedness and guilt,
and all who live in you are masters of sin and lewdness.
But he who goes out from you and comes into me
turns pious, for I am pious [Jer 3:12].
As long as he dwells in me, he multiplies his prayers to God,
and does not cease, evening and morning, from his supplications.
The wicked forsakes his way, and the man of iniquity his thoughts [Isa 55:7].
But when a man escapes from my net,
and the one goes out from me [Gen 44:28],
he quickly turns back to his wickedness,
forsakes all, and returns to his former state [Ezek 16:55].
He forgets his bonds,
and breaks his vows,
and forsakes prayer,
and does abominable deeds [Ps 14:1],
and turns again to do every vile thing,
as at the first.'

And when they had spoken many words,
their elders ⚠5 made peace between them,
and both of them returned to their former state."

Said the teller: When I heard his eloquent sayings,
and his lovely parables,
I looked at him closely, and behold, he was the mightiest of the shepherds [1 Sam 21:8],
and the lord of the nobles,
our chief and our teacher,
Ḥever the Kenite, our friend.
And I took up my parable and said [Num 23:7]:

Whoever seeks wisdom in riddles and devices, / and asks who is one, with understanding, without a second [Eccl 4:8],
will weary of every sage and every keen man of learning, / and from among them all will run to Ḥever the Kenite.
