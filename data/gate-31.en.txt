Thus says Heman the Ezrahite: I set my aim,
when I went out from my land,
to see the wonders of the world,
so that no secret should be hidden from me.
I went round the ends of the earth,
and took wandering for my inheritance and my portion.
And it came to pass at the end of some days, as I was journeying from Syria to Antioch,
that with us was the glory of the great,
the crown of companions,
Ḥever the Kenite.
He would tell us parables and riddles,
and delightful words.
He said to us: "Let me tell you a wondrous thing,
the like of which never came up on any man's heart."
We said: "Tell your wonder, and let us know it;
put forth your riddle, and let us hear it [Judg 14:13]."
He said: "I was journeying from Dibon
as far as the land of Heshbon,
with companions shining like the stars of night,
mighty men of valor,
men of high arm,
all holding swords, expert in war [Song 3:8].
And while we were driving our chariots
along our paths,
we saw a man riding on his horse, and he was in haste,
running like lightning,
looking out from the top of the hill.
When he saw us, he hurried down into the valley.
There was a veil on his face,
and he showed nothing but his eyes.
When we saw him, we ran toward him with bared arms,
and drawn swords,
and battles of brandishing [Isa 30:32].
And when the points of our spears had reached him,
he threw himself to the ground before us,
and kissed the steps of our chariots,
and said to us: 'Listen to me,
my heroes and my nobles,
and then decree upon me what you will.'
We said to him: 'Speak your words,
and uncover your secrets.'
And he uncovered the hiding of his face,
and took the veil away from between his eyes,
and behold, she was a maiden!
The light of the sun was not like her beauty;
before her the dawn put on dread,
and wrapped itself in shame.
Her lips were like the jar of manna [Exod 16:33],
her teeth plants of pleasantness [Isa 17:10],
her hair blue and purple,
the work of the hands of a craftsman [Song 7:2].
And her brows ⚠1 were like two rows written by the finger of God [Exod 31:18],
cherubim, the work of a skilled designer [Exod 26:1].
We said to her: 'Tell us what ails you,
and declare what has befallen you.'
She said: 'Let it not come to you, all you who pass by the way: look and see if there is any pain like my pain [Lam 1:12],
and have pity on me, for the hand of God has touched me [Job 19:21].
I was a maiden hidden away ⚠2 in the chambers of kings,
and the palaces of princes,
from the sun and the moon,
and from the shining light.
Not even the winds could steal a scent from my spices.
The ruin of the wicked did not come upon me [Prov 3:25],
and their hand did not drive me away [Ps 36:12],
until one of the violent men heard of me,
a man of the ruthless,
and came at midnight and broke through the walls of my house,
and carried me off from my mother's house and from the chamber of her who conceived me [Song 3:4].
To this wilderness he brought me,
inside the cave he laid me down,
in dark places he made me dwell [Lam 3:6],
he abused me and grieved me,
and set me up as a mark for the arrow [Lam 3:12].
It is now a full year that I sit in this wilderness alone,
and no soul of all the world's creatures at my side,
until my soul grew short, and I was weary of my life [Gen 27:46],
and I struck my cheeks ⚠3,
and said: "Better my death than my life [Jonah 4:3];
better for me to put myself in danger,
and give up my soul and my blood to death,
than to sit in this wilderness until the day of my death."
So I rose up, and took my life in my hand [Judg 12:3], and said: "I will trust in the mercies of the Lord, for they are not ended [Lam 3:22];
if they let us live, we shall live, and if they kill us, we shall die [2 Kgs 7:4]."
And it came to pass at midnight, when he was drinking himself drunk on his bed [1 Kgs 16:9],
as his custom and his manner was,
that I came to him softly [Ruth 3:7],
and drew near to him a little,
and drew out his sword,
and thrust it into his heart,
and it came out at his back.
He rose against me, and I caught him by his beard [1 Sam 17:35],
and threw him to the ground on his belly,
and drew the sword out of his body,
and cut through his neck ⚠4,
and the cords of his throat.
After that I made haste,
and rode on his horse,
and made my way through the wildernesses,
and quickly fled to a hill,
and I had no quiet and no rest [Job 3:26],
until I came upon you.
And now, here I am in your hand:
do with me as is good in your eyes [Jer 26:14].'
When we heard her words,
we had pity on her pangs,
and our compassion stirred at her speech.
We said: 'May the fire of the Lord come down on that wicked man and on his carcass,
and blessed be He who felled him and uprooted him!
And now, do not fear, for no harm shall befall you;
come with us, and we will do you good [Num 10:29].'
She said: 'Here I am, your handmaid,
to serve you
in all your desires.'
And as we went along together,
and her heart was at peace from fear,
she said to us: 'Know that behind this mountain there are springs of water,
sweet as honey to the lips.
If you set your way from there,
and take water for your need,
you will act wisely in what you do, for the way is long,
and the sun is strong,
and the soul is thirsty [Isa 29:8].'
The thing was good in our eyes, and we came to the spring in the heat of noon,
and behold, all the land was streams and watercourses [Isa 30:25].
Our heart rejoiced greatly,
and our grief was stilled.
She said to us: 'You are weary from walking and from toil,
and from the force of the heat and the journey.
If you lie down for the noonday rest,
and give the eyes a share of repose,
until the day breathes [Song 2:17],
and the wind blows,
then you will go on in joy,
when you see that there is relief [Exod 8:11].'

The thing was good in the eyes of our company.
We got down from our chariots,
and set our seats around the spring.
She too got down from her horse and took off her robes,
her headbands and her earrings,
and tied up her horse and our horses,
and made places ready for us as we wished,
and made us lie down in green pastures [Ps 23:2].
We said to her: 'Happy are you, graceful doe [Prov 5:19], for you have gathered, together with a splendid form,
a precious soul,
and joined to innocence
quickness and charm.'
She said: 'You wonder at my charm,
and you have not yet seen my quickness!
For I have a number of mighty and lovely skills,
and a number of honorable qualities,
and now I will reveal some of them to you.'
And she rose to show us her might and her power,
and bent her bow,
and shot an arrow up to the heart of heaven [Deut 4:11],
and before it fell, she shot a second arrow after it and split it in two.
We were amazed at her might
and at her quickness.
She said to us: 'I will show you yet another skill,
which will be a memorial for the last generation,
and kept in your hands.'
And she took her quiver in haste,
and rode on her horse,
and bent her bow,
and shot an arrow into the heart of one of our companions.
He fell to the ground dead, and I cried out to her and said: 'Woe to you, what is this you have done?'
She said to me: 'Be silent, son of a whore!
Your heart was beguiled by the form,
the white one,
and by the lip,
the lovely one,
and by the sight,
the comely one,
and by the temple,
the desired one;
the eyes ravished your heart [Song 4:9],
and the twin breasts,
and the slender waist,
and the curves of the thighs [Song 7:2],
and the whiteness of the hands and the legs;
and you thought to embrace and to kiss,
and to lay breast on breast and leg on leg.
As the Lord lives, you have erred in your reckoning,
and your heart and your thoughts were beguiled.
And if each man of you does not bind his fellow,
I will tear open his breast [Hos 13:8],
and give his flesh to the birds of the sky [1 Sam 17:44].'

Said the teller: When the people heard these evil tidings, they mourned [Exod 33:4],
and fell on their faces to the ground, and were dismayed.
The joy was turned
to sighing,
and the song
to lament,
and the love turned back
to enmity,
and the desire was turned
to war and weapons.
And we knew from all that was mentioned
that he was a male ⚠5.
We did not know in whose shade to take refuge,
nor what counsel to take.
For there was no strength in us,
neither to fight nor to flee,
for our horses were tied up,
and our weapons bound,
and each of us was lying down,
while he was riding on his horse,
with his bow drawn and ready at hand,
and no man could stand before him.
We found no counsel but to do his word:
each one of us bound his fellow,
until I was left alone,
and there was no second at my side [Eccl 4:8].
He came down from his horse and seized my hand,
and struck me on the back of my neck,
and said to me: 'Seed of an adulterer [Isa 57:3], you thought to drink your fill of the honeycomb of the lips,
and to embrace the arm and the cheeks!
Take your shoes off your feet [Exod 3:5].'
I said to him: 'This shoe was new when I put it on,
and now its moisture is gone [Deut 34:7], and it has dried up,
and I cannot pull it off.'
He said to me: 'I will draw off your shoe ⚠6 today,
for this honor has fallen to your lot.'
And when he stood beside me to pull off my shoe, I had inside the shoe a knife,
like a flash of lightning to the eye.
I drew it out before he could see it with his eye,
and thrust it into his belly,
and he fell to the ground on his belly.
I stood over him and killed him [2 Sam 1:10],
and took hold of his head and cut it off.
Then I hastened to loose my companions from their bonds,
and tore away the cords that were on their hands.
We buried our companion in the dust,
and went on, grieving in our calamity and our pangs,
and set out praise to our Rock,
who had loosed our bonds.
And I answered and said:

Had not the Lord helped us, / we had all but passed through the roar of death [Ps 94:17].
We came into a sea of troubles, and had it not been for God, / the roar of its waters would suddenly have swallowed us.
But after we had come into the mouth of death, / God commanded, and it spewed us out [Jonah 2:11].
We give thanks to the name of God, Lord of the world, / for the great kindness He has shown us."
