Thus says Heman the Ezrahite: I was journeying from Mahanaim
to Mount Ephraim.
And while I was still in the city, going round its roads
and roaming its ways,
I saw a man standing at the gate of his house, sullen and vexed [1 Kgs 21:4],
weary and faint.
I drew near to him and said to him: "What ails you, that you are grieved,
and why do you stand here alone?"
He said to me: "Cursed be appetite, which brings its owners back to sorrow."
I said: "Tell me what has found you,
and the chance that has befallen you."
He said: "Today a man of the merchants called me to his house,
and brought me to his meal.
And while I was still walking with him,
he began to tell me of the greatness of his wealth and his splendor,
and of the beauty of his wife.
He said to me: 'Know, my lord, that my fathers were of the nobles of the land, men marked out by name,
and heads of the princes.
And God granted my father wealth and honor,
greatness and majesty.
I was born to him under a fair star,
and I was pleasant to look upon.
And when I grew up,
I learned the way of understanding,
and grew wiser than all my teachers,
until I made the wise foolish [Isa 44:25]. And my father gave me a wife from the daughters of the nobles,
and of the well-known princes.
And the girl is fair in her form and her face,
and in all her other ways;
like a lamp she lights her dwellings,
and she is the joy of her husband and her neighbors.
By your life, my lord, if you could see her busy at the business of her house,
hurrying and never holding back,
going round the castle and the dwelling,
and from the dwelling to the palace,
sitting between oven and stove [Lev 11:35],
and lying among the hearthstones [Ps 68:14] ⚠1,
with the blackness of the smoke on the whiteness of her face
and on the beauty of her eyes,
you would say: Happy the man beside whom she sits,
and happy the people that is in such a case [Ps 144:15].'
And he did not stop speaking words like these until my ears rang with his voice.
When we came to the gate of his house he said to me: 'See, my lord, this gate and its frames,
and the carvings of its doors,
and its bars and its rings,
and its boards of cypress,
and the hinges of its planks.
How much of my wealth I spent on it,
and spent on it my strength and my vigor,
until I made it fair,
as the sight that you see!'
After that we came into the courtyard, and he said to me: 'See, my lord, this courtyard and its walls:
how lovely are its turrets [Ps 84:2],
and how goodly its dwellings [Num 24:5]!
For it is the choicest of courtyards,
and round about it are the houses of the merchants,
but there is none among them all as fair as it,
and no courtyard to compare with it.
Set your heart, my lord, on this hall, how good it is,
and see the windows that are in it,
and set your eyes on the beauty of the walls and the beams,
and the halls and the turrets,
and the figured stones and the upper chambers,
and the carvings of the images,
made in all kinds of colors,
on all four sides,
like eyes widened with kohl [Jer 4:30];
beauty has carved carvings upon them [1 Kgs 6:29],
and a carved work of gourds [1 Kgs 6:18].
See, my lord, the basins of the lovely water,
and see the lions standing on their rims,
and from their mouths jets of water rise and fall;
in them is the life of souls,
and the joy of God and men [Judg 9:13].'
After that we came into the house, and he said to me: 'See, my lord, these fair rooms,
made with rows of refined gold,
and paved on a pavement of porphyry [Esth 1:6].
There is my bed,
and the chamber of my beloved.
And if you could see me lying alone,
and my wife at my side,
her cheeks a lamp to my eyes,
her arms the girdle of my loins,
and her lips my delights,
as she kisses and embraces,
and leans upon me [Song 8:5],
and does not hold back,
until the neck is almost broken [1 Sam 4:18],
your thoughts would be amazed at this fair union,
and you would be driven mad by the sight of your eyes which you would see [Deut 28:34].'
After all this torment,
he called to the lad
to bring the table.
And when he had brought it,
and set it before him,
he said to me: 'See, my lord, this table and its make,
and see its stand and its base,
and its poles and its frames,
and its four feet [Exod 25:12].
I made it of all kinds of spice-wood,
for it will never rot.'
And he said to his lad: 'Bring the water,
to wash the hands.'
And when he brought the water vessel,
he said to me: 'See, my lord, this basin,
as if poured from gold,
or cast from the fine gold of Ophir,
great and wide [Ps 104:25];
it holds of water
as much as two seahs of seed [1 Kgs 18:32].
Set your heart, my lord, on this water,
for it is a delight to the eyes [Gen 3:6],
as if it were tears poured out
from the eyes of lovers;
pure and clear,
like fresh crystals,
flowing like threads of silver,
and cold as snow.
They drew it from the river Euphrates,
and left it all night in a vessel of onyx,
and in the morning it was like the layer of dew [Exod 16:13]
when it drips on the cheek of a lily.'
And he said to the lad: 'Take the basin in your right hand,
to pour water on the hands of your master [2 Kgs 3:11].'
And when the lad came he said to me: 'Set your heart, my lord, on this lad and his form,
and the grace of his stature,
and the blackness of his locks [Song 5:11],
and the redness of his lips,
and the radiance of his beauty and the lily of his cheeks,
and the embroidery of his face,
and the whiteness of his teeth,
and the blackness of his eyes,
as if his cheek were a garden bed [Song 5:13],
marked out with a compass [Isa 44:13],
hedged about with lilies [Song 7:3],
and woven with threads of scarlet [Song 4:3];
it takes sorrow from your heart.
I roamed after him to the ends of the world,
and bought him in the land of Elam.
I found him after my own heart [1 Sam 13:14],
and met him as my soul desired.
He prospers in all to which he turns [1 Kgs 2:3],
and before I call him he answers [Isa 65:24].
He walks in my ways,
and understands all my needs;
and all that I asked of my God,
for this lad I prayed [1 Sam 1:27].'
And when my ears were ringing with his many words, he said to his lad: 'Go and bring the pleasant savory dishes,
and the cooked foods,
and the roasted lambs,
and the fat things full of marrow [Isa 25:6],
for great is the hunger and the torment,
and the belly's weapons of war are set in array at the gate [Isa 22:7] ⚠2.'
Said the teller: I said in my heart: See, he has spoken of every thing, and still the food has not come.
It remains for him to tell of the bread and its sweetness,
and the flour and its grain,
and the millstones that ground it,
and with what sieve they sifted it,
and the oven in which they baked it,
and the wood, in what forest it was cut,
and the axe with which it was hewn,
and the meat, where it was taken from,
and the spices with which it was seasoned,
and the savory dishes and their taste,
and the stews and their sweetness,
and the condiments and their smell,
and the spices and their blending.
And it still remains for him to tell of the fire-pans and the basins,
and the vessels and the spoons,
and the shovels and the pots [Exod 27:3],
and the cauldrons and the bowls,
and the vessels of copper,
and the griddle and the stewpan [Lev 7:9],
and the pot and the kettle,
and the flask and the dish.
And all this he would not finish in many years and days,
nor until everlasting generations.
So I slipped from his hand with great force,
and hurried to flee,
and said to him: 'I have a need at my house; I will go and do it ⚠3,
and come back when I have finished it.'
But he held me fast and dragged me,
and brought me to the privy,
and said: 'You cannot go out
until you see the privy,
for in it the work is made complete.'
And he said to me: 'See, my lord, how fair it is, how good,
and see the water basin that is in it,
and the pavement of porphyry and marble [Esth 1:6] within it.
If a fly walked on it,
its foot would slip on its paths.
Your soul would long to eat in it,
for its great beauty and goodness.'
I said to him: 'Eat in joy, my lord [Eccl 9:7],
for everything came into my reckoning,
but the privy never came into my mind.'
And I hurried and did not hold back,
and escaped at once like a gazelle from the hand [Prov 6:5].
And he ran after me to bring me back,
and cried out with a trembling voice:
'Remember, do not forget the meal [Deut 9:7]!'
When the boys heard his words, they thought he was taunting me over food,
and they cried out after me: 'Remember, do not forget the meal!'
And when I saw that they surrounded me like bees [Ps 118:12],
I threw a stone at one of them, and it sank into his forehead [1 Sam 17:49],
and his spirit almost went out of him.
Then the neighbors came running after me,
great and small,
and they pursued me and overtook me,
and gathered against me and beat me,
and I escaped from them with my spirit between my teeth.
And this was the merchant's meal: may the iniquity of his fathers be remembered before the Lord [Ps 109:14] ⚠4."
Said the teller: When I heard his words I laughed,
and fell my full length to the ground [1 Sam 28:20].
And I said to him: "By your life, my lord,
are you Ḥever the Kenite?"
And he answered and said:

I am Ḥever, who captivates the hearts / with the goodness of my words, and dazzles thoughts.
I compose compositions of lovely riddles ⚠5, / sweeter than honeycomb [Ps 19:11] to the palate of the hearers.
From the fire of my sweetness I hew out flames [Ps 29:7], / and from the rock of my speech I draw swords.

When I heard his poem,
I marveled at his lies,
and he drew me on with the sweetness of his speech,
and I was bound many days with the cords of his love [Hos 11:4],
until wandering carried him away on its pinion [Deut 32:11],
and struck me with the rod of its wrath [Lam 3:1].
