Then came the ninth century, and in it was born Rabbi Isaac ibn Khalfun,
who filled with his poems the western side and the north,
and he was the first to pass over into the ranks of poetry.
God gave him, in some of his poems, the answer of the tongue [Prov 16:1].
He began to be a mighty one [Gen 10:8] in poems,
and he opened its closed doors
to the children of the Hebrews;
then they went down to the gates [Judg 5:11].
Yet only a few of his poems are goodly as the fruit of a goodly tree [Lev 23:40],
and most of them are thorns and thistles [Gen 3:18].
There were many poets in his generation,
who growled like bears [Isa 59:11],
but his poetry rose above them all,
and he was anointed king over their assembly.
In his days there lived the great Nagid, Rabbi Samuel ha-Levi, of blessed memory, who bared a high arm [Job 38:15] in the craft of poetry,
and brought hidden things out to light [Job 28:11].
At the end of his days was born Rabbi Solomon the Small ⚠1, who waved the spices of poetry, and there came no more such spice [1 Kgs 10:10];
and after him there arose none like him [2 Kgs 23:25].
For all the poets who came after him
ran to learn from his poems,
and did not reach the dust of his feet [Nah 1:3],
for the force of his verses
and the strength of his words.
Had he lived long,
he would have composed mighty wonders in the secrets of poetry;
but his crown was plucked while still in its bloom [Job 8:12],
and he was snatched away while the freshness of youth was in him [Deut 34:7].
At twenty-nine his lamp went out,
and he did not reach thirty.
In the course of the ninth century those poets perished; only their delights remained.
They were gathered to their people [Gen 25:8], and their praises lived on.
And all that generation too were gathered to their fathers [Judg 2:10].
After them arose the generation of the lovely poets,
and their time was called the time of the blossoms [Song 2:12],
for the generation was like thorns and they like lilies [Song 2:2].
That is the generation of the wondrous poets,
who are called a band of prophets [1 Sam 10:5],
among whom was Rabbi Judah ha-Levi,
the turtledove of the Torah ⚠2 and the ornament of the Testimony,
and the other poets of his generation, peace be upon them; and though none of them reached in his poems the rank of Rabbi Solomon,
nor attained the force of his speech
and the sweetness of his taste,
yet they had wondrous words
and awesome rhymes,
and through them were seen the awesome works of the sweet poems,
as if they were taken from the mouth of cherubim,
or stolen from the tongue of seers.
The spirit rested upon them, and they were of those that were written [Num 11:26],
and they prophesied, and did not cease [Num 11:25];
and to hear the wonders of their song the princes of the peoples gathered [Ps 47:10]."

Nobles who put on garments of delight, / they shone like the sun in the heavens of the Testimony.
At their song men of heart were dismayed, / and Time wrapped itself in garments of trembling [Ezek 26:16].
They set up boards of understanding for their song, / and in their hearts sockets for it, and tenons [Exod 26:17–19].
They were unique among the people of God, / and so too their eloquence was unique.
Prophecy spoke with them mouth to mouth, / in the vision of song, plainly and not in riddles [Num 12:8].

"After that generation the springs of song were stopped up
in the Hebrew tongue,
and among the fat ones of the eloquence of poetry leanness was sent [Isa 10:16];
her prophets, too, found no vision [Lam 2:9].
All who came after that generation toiled to search out the spring of song,
and hewed out broken cisterns [Jer 2:13],
and their waters were bitter [Exod 15:23].
And since Solomon, who sat on his throne, was gathered in,
and Abraham his Nasi breathed his last and died [Gen 25:8],
and Judah the captain of his host,
and Moses his prophet ⚠3,
the vision of poetry and its source were stopped up,
and that glory and its awe departed,
and the angel of the Lord appeared no more [Judg 13:21].
For none arose after them
who could compose like their poems.
And here we are, all of us here this day [Deut 5:3], gleaning the forgotten sheaves of their ears [Deut 24:19],
and cutting off the stragglers behind them [Deut 25:18].
Day and night we run in their tracks,
and toil, and do not touch them ⚠4.
For each one of us meditates a vain thing [Ps 2:1],
and draws out the sword of folly [Lev 26:33].
The first ones ate the fine flour,
and left us the bran.
And indeed I have seen the communities of the East,
where the glory of the Lord has shone [Isa 60:1],
and the communities of France,

and Ashkenaz and Riphath [Gen 10:3],
and the communities of Persia,
and Meshech and Tiras [Gen 10:2],
and the communities of Greece,
who dwell between Dishon and Alvan [Gen 36:21–23] ⚠5:
there is no wisdom that they have not inherited,
and no good quality that they have not conquered;
only the land of poetry alone they have seen and not entered.
They thought they had learned the craft of rhymes,
and supposed they had understood it;
but poetry is far from them and will not come near them,
as far as the east is from the west [Ps 103:12].
Their soul goes round about its land and its dwelling,
but it shall not pass through it;
for you shall see the land from afar, but you shall not go there [Deut 32:52].

If you ask them, they will say that there are no poets like them,
and no poems like their poems.
But no man sees his own blemish,
and no man finds himself guilty.
For poetry has seven conditions, and by them it is sweet and pleasant,
and its ways are ways of pleasantness [Prov 3:17];
and whoever does not keep these conditions, his poem shall be trampled,
and shall be like the layer of dew [Exod 16:13]: when the sun grows hot, it melts [Exod 16:21].
