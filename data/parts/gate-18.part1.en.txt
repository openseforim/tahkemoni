Thus says Heman the Ezrahite: I journeyed while I was still a tender youth
from the land of Hadrach [Zech 9:1]
to the Holy City, where the lamp of the kingship is set in order,
and the light of the Presence has trodden.
There I joined company with the chosen of God,
and there I met with the children of Israel.
And it came to pass after some days,
as I sat in an assembly of sages,
honored and high,
who raised poetry up to the heavens
and poured rains from the streams of its themes,
that they were speaking of the matter of poetry and its secrets:
to know how its foundations were first built,
and who dug its springs for the Jews,
and made its lights shine for them.
Some of them answered and said: "Behold, we all know,
and our fathers have told us [Ps 44:2],
that poetry is the inheritance of the Arabs,
from the day they were upon their land.
But they did not know when the spirit of song came mightily [1 Sam 10:10]
upon the Hebrews,
and who first opened its gates to them,
and went into its chambers
to gather its bdellium and its pearls;
and who split its streams and their pools,
and who measured their waters in the hollow of his hand,
and meted out their heavens with the span [Isa 40:12]."
And they spoke at length, each man after his own way,
one saying this and another saying that [1 Kgs 22:20].
And there was found an old man and wise,
and he sat among them,
listening to their words,
and it could be seen from the look of his eyes that he laughed and mocked at them.
And when they had given and taken in their words,
and roamed about to seek and did not find,
and toiled to come out of the sea of folly, and sank,
and the men rowed hard to bring it back to the dry land, and could not [Jonah 1:13],
he said to them: "Woe to you who have come into a river that has risen and swelled,
and sweeps on and passes over [Isa 8:8]!
You are entangled in the land, with no crossing;
the wilderness has shut you in [Exod 14:3].
The question you have asked is farther than the sky,
and deeper than the sea,

and there is no man but me who knows its secret
and reaches to its foundation."

Says the narrator: When the people heard his speech,
they all gathered around him,
for him to kindle his spark for them
and drop his showers upon them.
He said to them: "Know that the wondrous poetry,
which is filled with pearls,
was at first the inheritance of the Arabs.
They repaired every breach in it [2 Kgs 12:6],
and weighed it in the balances of justice [Job 31:6],
and set it up on its base,
and raised its tabernacle,
and set its table in order,
and fed others its portion.
They built its upper chambers,
and walked about its palaces [Ps 48:14];
they laid its beams and set up its doors [Neh 3:3].
Theirs is the highest rank over the poets of the world,
and when the men of poetry are called upward [Hos 11:7], they answer and go up first.
And though there are poets in every nation too,
who busy themselves with the craft of song,
all their songs, beside the songs of the Ishmaelites, are neither a help nor a profit [Isa 30:5],
and all their words are vanity, with nothing in them that profits [Jer 16:19].
For poetry pleasant in its utterance,
sweet in its reading,
like the sun at its height,
and sure as the dawn in its going forth [Hos 6:3],
belongs to the Arabs alone,
and all the nations are as nothing beside them [Isa 40:17].
Only, the children of our people, after their exile from their land,
many of them dwelt with the Arabs in their countries,
and grew used to speaking their tongue
and thinking their thoughts;
and when they mingled with them,
they learned the craft of poetry from them,
as it is said: They mingled among the nations and learned their works [Ps 106:35].
For while our fathers still dwelt in the Holy City,
no metered poem in the holy tongue was known to them,
only the books of Job, Proverbs, and Psalms,
which are short and light verses;
they look like the lines of a poem, but they have no rhyme and are not metered.
And because a remnant of the tribe of Judah escaped to Spain,
as it is said: And the exiles of Jerusalem who are in Sepharad [Obad 1:20];
and the land of Spain lies over against the middle of the sky, straight and level,
under the equal and straight line ⚠1;
for the people of Babylon are on the equal line in the east,
and the people of Spain at the end of the equal line in the west;
therefore the sciences spread at these two ends, each after its kind,
for they are on the two sides of the sphere, and its pillars,
on which the house stands [Judg 16:26].
In these two places the world took its glory and its strength:
it shall have two shoulder-pieces joined at its two ends [Exod 28:7].
For of old there were in Babylon
the sages of the world,
and likewise in Spain there were
masters of the sciences in every science, and in the secret of poetry, joined and parted ⚠2.
Therefore their tongue was kept in its purity;
its taste remained in it, and its scent was not changed [Jer 48:11].
Poetry had a camp among the men of Spain;
before they call it, it answers [Isa 65:24];
from their heart it journeys and at their mouth it encamps [Num 9:18]."
And he took up his parable and said:

When the sages hid from singing a song, / the sages of Spain mustered to sing it.
And when the people of the East found no vision [Lam 2:9] in song, / the sons of the West prophesied.

"It came to pass in the year four thousand seven hundred of the Creation ⚠3,
in the seventh century, that the spirit of counsel and might [Isa 11:2] came mightily upon the people of Spain,
and a pure language [Zeph 3:9].
Then the Hebrews began
to train their tongue in the ways of song,
and the coals of eloquence began to set their hearts alight,
and the signal-smoke began to go up out of the city [Judg 20:40].
But their eloquence at that time was poor,
and their tongue limped on the path of poetry,
until the eighth century came;
and then they roused themselves to play upon the Sheminith [Ps 6:1] ⚠4,
and prepared the house of poetry according to its rule and its pattern [Exod 25:9],
and measured its plan [Ezek 43:10].
In those days there shone in Spain the sun of praise
in the firmament of greatness,
the great Nasi,
Rabbi Isaac the Sephardi ben Ḥasdai ⚠5,

may he rest in the shadow of the Almighty [Ps 91:1];
for he poured out to all who asked of him a blessing past all measure [Mal 3:10].
In his days the waves of the sciences roared,
and their waters cast up crystal and coral [Job 28:18],
and their streams stood up like walls [Exod 15:8].
Every soul that thirsted for the waters of wisdom quenched its thirst in him;
and God opened her eyes,
and she saw a well of water, sweeter than honeycomb to the lips,
and she went and filled the skin with water [Gen 21:19].
For that Nasi revived with the dews of his kindness the dead of folly,
and drew with the cords [Hos 11:4] of his bounty the hearts of the children of the exile,
and he made a proclamation [Exod 36:6]: 'Whoever is for the Lord, to me [Exod 32:26]!
All his wants are upon me [Judg 19:20].'
And there gathered to him every Gaon and every master,

from the lands of Edom and Arabia,
from east and from west;
and he set the table of his kindness before them,
and the cloud of his majesty stood over them,
and the pillar of fire of his glory lit their eyes [Num 14:14].
The sages of the generation gathered to him, shining with lights of brightness,
to set in array the order of wisdom before those who seek God;
and he filled them with the spirit of God,
in wisdom, in understanding, and in knowledge poured out,
and in all manner of workmanship,
to devise devices [Exod 31:3–4],
and to hew from the hearts
blazing flames [Ps 29:7].
From that time the sciences broke forth in Spain,
and from these the whole earth was overspread [Gen 9:19].

From then on the murmurers learned instruction [Isa 29:24],
and the singers went before, the players after [Ps 68:26].
For his kindness made the tongue of the dumb sing [Isa 35:6],
and opened the hearts that were stopped up,
until they composed pleasant poems in his honor,
that shine like the stars on high.
Then the eyes were opened in the craft of poetry,
and the heavens were opened,
and visions of God were seen [Ezek 1:1].
In his days wisdom spread in Israel,
for he was to learning a kinsman and a redeemer [Ruth 2:1, 2:20] ⚠6.

the lights that had shone grew dim,
