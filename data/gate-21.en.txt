Thus says Heman the Ezrahite: I set my heart [Ps 119:112],
while I was still fresh in my bloom,
to roam over the pleasant lands,
and to learn understanding from the mouths of the wise,
and to gather portions from the fruit of their tongues.
One day I came to the city of Rehoboth ⚠1,
and while I was going about in the markets and the broad streets [Song 3:2],
behold, Ḥever the Kenite came toward me; and when he saw me he ran to me, his mouth full of laughter,
and his voice was heard far off.
I turned aside to him,
to greet him with peace.
I said: "Why did you laugh,
and what ails you that you cry out so?"
He said: "Hold on, and I will tell you a thing that will fill your mouth with laughter [Job 8:21],
beyond all measure [Isa 5:14]."
I said to him: "Tell me, please, your nonsense,
and your words of jest."
He said: "I rose early today, and my soul was longing [Isa 29:8],
and my hand was empty.
I craved to eat meat,
and my heart was lashed with the whip of hunger,
and I had not a piece of silver [1 Sam 2:36] in my hand,
to get what I longed for.
And when the blaze of burning hunger was kindled in my heart,
I said: 'I will rise now and go about the city [Song 3:2];
perhaps God will stir up His mercy.'
So I rose and went about on every side;
perhaps there may be hope [Lam 3:29] and expectation.
But there was no food and no grain,
only grief and breaking ⚠2,
that brings down to the grave.
And when I despaired
of what I had sought,
and was at a loss
in what I had hoped for,
I turned, after I had gone out,
to go back to my house,
seeing that my hope was lost,
and my toil in vain.
Before I had finished speaking in my heart [Gen 24:45],
I lifted up my eyes and looked, and behold, an Arab,
coming from his town and his village,
and he was riding upon his ass.
And when I saw him,
I said in my heart: 'I have found what I craved;
this is the day I hoped for [Lam 2:16].'
I hurried to him,
to greet him with peace,
and I said to him:
'Peace be your coming [1 Sam 16:4], my lord,
Avidan son of Gideoni [Num 1:11]!
Where do you come from,
and where are you going?
Blessed be He who granted me to see an angel!
Therefore I came out to meet you,
to seek your face, and I have found you [Prov 7:15] ⚠3.
For all my days I have yearned for you,
and asked about you of all who passed to and fro.'
The villager said: 'Peace be upon you, my lord;
but I am not Avidan son of Gideoni,
only Avida the Gibeonite ⚠4.'
I said to him: 'The two names are like each other,
and all goes to one place [Eccl 3:20].
And now tell me how you are,
and where you live,
and tell me how your father and your mother are:
are they as strong as when I left them,
or is their eye dim and their vigor gone [Deut 34:7]?'
The villager sighed
when he heard my words,
and said: 'They were gathered to their people in great poverty,
and have journeyed to their eternal home [Eccl 12:5].
They have set out to their rest,
and left me to my sighs.'
When I heard his word, I put out my hand
to tear my garment.
The villager took hold of my hand and said to me: 'By the life of your parents,
do not tear your garments,
for grief will not help,
nor will it save from death.'
I said to him: 'Had I done as is fitting, I would have torn my liver
instead of my garment,

and poured out the blood of my ribs
instead of tears,
for a faithful brother,
who was to me a tower of strength [Ps 61:4] against the blows of time.
How many good things he did for me,
and how many kindnesses he gave me!
But it is fitting for every man of understanding
to accept the judgment as just.
And now, my friend, take comfort,
and come into the house to eat a morsel of bread [Gen 18:5];
or let us go to the market,
and I will buy you meat roasted on the fire, pleasant and desirable.'
When the villager heard meat named, his soul was crushed with longing [Ps 119:20],
and he ran after me with love,
and hurried along with me,
and did not know that he had fallen into my net.
When we came to the market, we found meat roasted on the fire, a delight to the eyes [Gen 3:6],
set between oven and range [Lev 11:35].
Its juices dripped in their heat,
and from the blaze of the fire their flesh almost melted off their bones.
The tongue of the flame had cooked its pieces,
and seethed its seething [Ezek 24:5],
and made its blossoms bloom.
In its heat it made them languish ⚠5,
and laid them down in sorrow [Isa 50:11] ⚠6,
until it brought them back
pure and bright,
soft and moist,
like cut pieces of crystal ⚠7.
Above, they were more ruddy in body than pearls [Lam 4:7],
and below, white as snow;
like the whiteness of the frost was their whiteness,
their cut was sapphire [Lam 4:7].
And on them was fat, pure and moist,
and its look was like the look of bdellium [Num 11:7],
pure and holy, seasoned with salt [Exod 30:35].
It would split the heart of those who saw it,
and send fire into their ribs.
And when we came near to the owner of the meat,
I said to him: 'Weigh out some of the meat
for Avidan our friend,
for he is our brother, our flesh [Gen 37:27].
And choose for us the good pieces,
the fat and the sweet,
the soft and the rich,
the red and the white,
fair to the sight of the eyes,
sweet to the lips,
honeyed to the palate and the teeth.'

The man put out his hand and took the knife [Gen 22:10],
to cut from the beast that may be eaten [Lev 11:47].
And behold, it was red as a coal,
and cooked like the heart of the jealous,
and in its taste like the honeycomb,
and its smell like cinnamon and onycha [Exod 30:34].
He brought us bread of fine flour,
and set it before us,
and we ate, both of us,
until our teeth were set on edge [Jer 31:29] ⚠8,
and our belly cleaved to the ground [Ps 44:26].
And when we had finished eating,
and licking everything up,

I said to the owner of the meat: 'Give Avidan our friend some of the almonds cooked in honey,
whose taste binds up every pang ⚠9.
And let what you give be new, the child of a night [Jonah 4:10],
like the moon when it shines:
its taste sweet and rich,
it will wake the sleeper to it,
and lie in the mouth upon beds of ivory [Amos 6:4] ⚠10.
It will melt at the sight of the eyes,
and run between the hands;
and before the teeth can pound it,
the jaws [Ps 22:16] will draw it in
without tongs ⚠11,
and hide it away in the chambers of the bowels.'
The man, when he heard the word, hurried
to give and to weigh,
and to set it all to the villager's account.
And we ate it all up [Gen 31:15],
and then we turned, both of us,
to bite with our teeth
and to draw with our tongues.
And when we had finished the work,
and set out to God our thanks,
I said to the villager: 'We need cold water,
to put out these burning foods.
You stand here, while I go over to this gate
and call to the lad,
and he will bring us good water,
sweet as blocks of snow,
hewn from Mount Senir,
to put out in our insides
the blazing flames.'
The villager said: 'Do as you have said;
you have decided well.'
So I went down from the post ⚠12,
and stood hidden behind the wall,
to know what would be done to him [Exod 2:4],
and what would come up in his lot.
And when he saw that I delayed to come [Exod 32:1],
he was grieved in his heart [Gen 6:6],
and rose to tie his girdle,
and to ride upon his ass.
The owner of the meat hurried to him,
and took hold of his skirts,
and said to him: 'Where are you going, when you have not paid your debt?
Give the price of what you ate.'
The villager said: 'Get away from me,
and do not come near me,
for I bought nothing;
I was a guest.'
The man seized him by the neck,
and struck him with his palm,
and said to him: 'Son of a whore, when did we invite you,
or where do we know you from?

Give three pieces of silver, and if not, we will drag you off,
until you vomit up all you have swallowed,
and go twice as hungry as you were full.'
The villager wept and said: 'Cursed be that scoundrel ⚠13,
who mourned for my father!
For I told him my name was Avida the Gibeonite,
and he insisted on calling me Avidan son of Gideoni,
until he bit me like an adder,
and the days of poverty have scattered me.'
And he could not get away until he had weighed out the coins,
and he went off with streams of tears on his cheeks.
And as he went off to his town,
I heard him weeping in his pain,
and saying in his song:

A scoundrel who set my heart on fire: / who will give him to be food for my sword?
For in all my days I never saw him, / until he came to cheat me and mocked me [Gen 31:7].

He laughed at my distress ⚠14, who at the first / had wept and mourned over my father."

Said the teller: When I heard the words of Ḥever the Kenite, I knew that he was one alone in villainy, and there is no second [Eccl 4:8].
And I said to him: "Truly, there is none to compare with you [Ps 40:6] in the sweetness of your sayings, and in the evil of your deeds.
With your tongue you capture mighty men, and in your insolence you are a charmer of charms [Ps 58:6] ⚠15.
You betray all who pass to and fro [Zech 7:14], and like a lion you tear the native and the sojourner."
He laughed at my words, and said to me:

The sons of men are all traitors, and therefore / I betray all who come and go.
I hunt down the lionesses like gazelles, / and I tear the lions like lambs.
I make the wise like fools, / and turn the understanding into simpletons.

He turned from me and went his way, and left me amazed at his wickedness and his villainy, his cunning and his wit.
