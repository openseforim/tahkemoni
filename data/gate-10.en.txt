Thus says Heman the Ezrahite: I journeyed from the Valley of Siddim [Gen 14:3]
to the land of the Chaldeans.
And when I had shone forth on its heights,
and trodden its low ground ⚠1,
I went out to walk about the land, to see those who come and go,
the citizens and the settlers,
the men of the east and the men of the west.
I lifted up my eyes and saw a man sitting alone on a pillar of stone,
and I sat down at his side,
as is the way of travellers and strangers,
who join company each with his brother,
and tell the happenings of their time.
I said to him: "What is the matter of your sitting here,
and what is your work?"
He said: "I am a foreign man, going from strength to strength [Ps 84:8];
once I hunt a fox, and once I tear a ram."
I said to him: "Tell me some of your matters,
and let me hear of what your eyes have seen in the world."

He said: "I have seen, on the roads I have walked,
and on the paths I have trodden,
how many awesome things,
and how many wonders;
but my eyes never saw a thing so wondrous to those who hear it,
and so delightful to those who know it,
as what I saw last night,
before the sun went down.""
And I asked him about the awesome wonder
that he had seen.

He said: "I was with pleasant companions,
of the royal seed and of the nobles [Esth 1:3]:
their gifts were rains,
their blessings granaries,
their pillars of smoke were spices [Song 3:6],
and their sayings reached the heavens.
Their faces were lights,
their looks were dawns,
their hands were rivers.
By their light they put the sun and the moon to shame,
and hide the stars of the night,
and turn the night into day [Job 17:12].
And when the dawn came up yesterday, we rose,
we rose early and we agreed
to go out to the villages,
and take our delight among the nard and the henna [Song 4:13] ⚠2,
or in one of the forests.
We told the lads to harness our chariots [Gen 46:29],
and we went out with our lads around us,
we on horses in full gear,
ready to overtake the lightning.
And there said to us
the head of our company:
'Will you not go with me to the choicest of the villages,
on one of the hills?
For I have a delightful village there,
whose beauty cannot be measured,
and every pleasure is joined to it.
And all its men are my servants,
and have been repaid by my kindness.'
We said to him: 'All that you decree for us we will do,
and in your shade we will take refuge.'
We went all day until noon had passed,
and reached the village at dusk.
The sun had far declined,
and turned towards evening,
and the night was near.
And when the sun was hidden in her dwelling,
and had covered her face
in her settings of scarlet ⚠3,
we came to the village, and behold, the villagers, the tillers of the soil, came out in joy to meet us,
and bowed down before us.
The greatest of them came and helped us down from our chariots,
and took the beasts,
and brought them to the choicest land,
to fill them and feed them;
he makes them lie down in green pastures and leads them beside still waters [Ps 23:2].
Then he came back to us in his joy,
and brought us to his house.
And behold, a wide dwelling,
like the wilderness and the plain,
and within it straw, inside and out,
and the dung of cattle [Ezek 4:15] and sheep.
He hurried to clear the house,
and to take away the briers and thorns [Isa 5:6];
he had the house scraped within [Lev 14:41],
and cleansed it of its stench and its filth,
and took away its crop [Lev 1:16] ⚠4.
He took his wives and his daughters and hid them in the inner house;
he brought them into an inner chamber [1 Kgs 22:25].
And he came before us,
and said to us: 'If it is good in your eyes,
I will set before you
curdled cheese,
and milk and butter.'
We said: 'Where is it?
For there is none like it.'
He hurried to the flocks,
to fill a lordly bowl [Judg 5:25] with the milk of the goats,
and in a moment he curdled it,
and brought it before us.
He said to us: 'Eat of the fat things [Neh 8:10],
and drink your fill of dainties.
For we who dwell in the hills,
and live in the villages,
our soul is generous,
and our hand is open wide
to receive every guest with love.
Our eye is not evil,
and our giving is not scanty like that of the city-dwellers,
for their gift is wanting,
their hand is short,
and their eye is narrow.
But all our joy is to receive the guests,
and bring them into spacious upper rooms [Jer 22:14],
and fill them with offerings of fatlings [Ps 66:15],
and set before them the heart's blood and the choice cuts ⚠5.'
And he took up his parable and said:

To bring in guests is sweet to my palate, / like the churning of milk [Prov 30:33] in the butter of the herds;
and like morsels soaked in butter / when they are piled up in heaps [Exod 8:10].
When I hear the voice of guests at midnight, / I call to my sons: Open the gates [Isa 26:2]!
My joy in a guest is like the joy / of a wolf that has stolen a kid from the flocks.
Every day I plough the soil of generosity, / as one ploughs the ground with oxen ⚠6.
To do kindness is as good to me as to a hungry man / who finds a dry crust with leeks [Num 11:5].
My eye hopes to see guests / as the eyes of the donkeys hope for barley.

After all this he took a lamb of its first year,
and hurried to prepare it [Gen 18:7],
and set it before us,
and he stood over us [Gen 18:8].
And when we had finished eating, he made ready for us
fresh beds,
and resting places
undisturbed [Isa 32:18].
We lay down and rested
from the weariness with which we had wearied ourselves.

And in the morning, when the sun looked out from the windows of her circuits,
and laid bare her robes,
and took off her veil from upon her [Gen 38:19],
we rose from our beds
to set out our prayer to God.
We rode on our horses,
and went out from our dwellings,
and set our faces towards the open country,
to sharpen our eyes on the sight of the blossoms and the plants,
and to wake our sleeping thoughts.
We walked among the shades of gardens,
full of sap and green [Ps 92:15],
full of delights.
And when the time to eat came, behold, the villager came to meet us in his joy,
and brought us to his house.
We found places made ready,
and savoury dishes prepared.
The pots were boiling,
and gave their smell from afar.
The greens filled the pot,
and all the flesh was grass [Isa 40:6] ⚠7.
He set his bowls before us,
full of his savoury dishes.

Said the teller: In the villager's house I saw a cock,
long of pinion [Ezek 17:3] and big,
tender and good,
fat and juicy.
I longed to eat him,
for all night long he had woken me from my sleep with his voice.
I said to the villager: 'You have made your goodness to us great,
and it is our duty to give you thanks.
But I am sick, and my heart is sullen and displeased [1 Kgs 20:43],
and the doctors have ordered me not to eat meat.
Yet I have a craving to eat
of the flesh of this cock.'
The villager said to me: 'If you had craved the flesh of my son,
my eye would not have spared him.'
And the villager hurried, he and his wife,
after the cock, to take him.
The cock fled in grief and anguish,
from roof to roof and from corner to corner.
And when the villager had toiled and found no rest,
chasing after his cock,
and saw that he could not prevail against him [Gen 32:26],
he called the people of his house to help him.
The lads ran after him
with joy and with songs,
and one lad caught him
and held him by his foot.
In the bitterness of death the cock bit him,
until he let him go.
The cock fled and escaped from the hand of them all,
and went up to the top of the ladder,
and gave praise and laud to the Master of the world,
on the day that the Lord delivered him from the hand of all his enemies [Ps 18:1].

After that he went up on the roof of the house of prayer,
and there was a great congregation there,
and the sound of a tumult.
He lifted up his voice in song,
and said: 'Blessed are You, Lord, who gives the cock understanding [Job 38:36] ⚠8.'
And all the people gathered around him
to hear his speech.
He answered and said to them: 'Listen to me, you who pursue righteousness [Isa 51:1], men of praises,
who come to set out prayers
to Him who is terrible in His deeds,
and to pour out hearts
to Him who dwells above the skies,
and to lift up hands
to God in heaven [Lam 3:41]:
what good are the prayers,
when your souls are defiled with blood [Isa 59:3]?
And why do you make many supplications,
when you shed the blood of the innocent and the needy?
How do you think to pray,
when every day you lie in wait to kill the innocent?
Bring no more vain offerings [Isa 1:13], and do not come to the house of God.
Your hands are full of blood [Isa 1:15].
Your prayer is mockery,
and not for favour;
by it no sin is atoned,
for it is an offering of remembrance, bringing iniquity to remembrance [Num 5:15].'
The people were amazed at his complaint,
and asked one another about what had happened to him.

He answered and said to them: 'To you, O men, I call [Prov 8:4],
who turn back the battle at the gate [Isa 28:6]!
Is there no fear of the Lord upon your faces,
and is there no just judgement among you?
If you betray all who come under the shadow of your roof [Gen 19:8],
who will trust in your love?
And if you do evil to your allies,
who will believe in you any more?
Is it a light thing in your eyes [1 Sam 18:23] to betray the men of love,
and to repay evil for good?
Since the day I came under the shadow of your roof,
have I not served you with all my strength?
All night I wake your sleepers
to pray to your God,
and all day I sing before you
to gladden your hearts.
I have multiplied seed in your shade,
and made sons and daughters to sweeten your palate with their taste.
And your thoughts are to do me harm,
and all your craving is to kill me.
You have been rebellious against the Lord since the day I knew you [Deut 9:24],
and all your thoughts are to slaughter me and eat me.
Behold, while I am yet alive with you, you have been rebellious; how much more after my death [Deut 31:27].
I adjure you: what wrong have you found in me [Jer 2:5] in all my days and years?
Here I am: witness against me before the Lord [1 Sam 12:3].
Behold, all of you have seen,
and have looked upon my deeds,
that I have no work in my house
but my supplication and my song,
and my only business is to seek my living,
and to sing to God in my sitting and my rising [Ps 139:2],
and to chant in my night and my day.
By day the Lord commands His kindness, and at night His song is with me [Ps 42:9].
And now, after I have grown old,
and have grown worn out in your service,
and my days and years have passed,
and the days of my old age have come,
you pay me evil for good [Ps 109:5],
and reward me with hatred for love.
You want to part me from my sons,
and to drive me from the bosom of my wives,
to leave the daughters orphans,
and my wives deserted widows.
Had you found me of any use, I would have kept silent,
and not sought redress for my wrong from you.
But my flesh is harder than flint;
it would not be accepted as an offering by fire [Lev 22:27].
My eye is dim and my moisture is gone [Deut 34:7],
my strength is dried up like a potsherd [Ps 22:16],
and my bones have become like iron
through the length of my years and the number of my days.
My scent is changed [Jer 48:11],
my flesh bristles [Ps 119:120],
and my taste is bitter.
My cuts are hard for those who taste them,
for they are bitter,
and broth of foul things is in their vessels [Isa 65:4].
My grinders have ceased,
and my eyes have grown dark [Eccl 12:3].
No flesh is left on my ribs,
and no fat on my kidneys.
I am poured out like water, and all my bones are out of joint [Ps 22:15].
If a sick man eats me, he will die that day,
and bear his sin on his shoulder,
and his guilt will not be atoned.
And if a healthy man eats me, he will gain no strength from me,
and will find no rest [Lam 1:3].
So why should you repay me with such a reward [2 Sam 19:37],
and leave to your children such an evil report?
Have you not in my sons an exchange,
and a delightful gift?
For they are pleasant and pure,
good and tender.
Each one of them has many praises,
and tender cuts,
and spices like perfume,
and his ribs are full of fat,
and the marrow of his bones [Job 21:24].
And my daughters are all fat and sweet,
pleasing to every tongue and desired by the heart.
Their taste sweetens the mouth,
and if the sick man eats them he will be healed,
and will need no doctor.
Of me the Torah spoke, in time long past and gone:
"You shall surely let the mother go, and take the young for yourself [Deut 22:7]" ⚠9.'
And he took up his parable and said:

My acquaintances and my friends, have pity on me, / and comfort me in the greatness of my grief.
Is this to be the reward of faithfulness, / that they repay my good with evil?
Friends have forgotten the kindness I did them, / and they always seek to kill me.
I pursue their peace and their good, / and in return for my love they are my adversaries [Ps 109:4].
Always I hear their counsel against me, / and all day long, as one who fights, they oppress me [Ps 56:2].
I came before them with sons and with daughters, / and they came at me with the knife ⚠10.
I fed them the flesh of my sons, and they / seek an occasion [Judg 14:4] to eat me.
I am old, and there is no flesh on my body; / why then do they long to eat me?
They pursued me in their wickedness, and had / they caught me, they would have destroyed me in a moment.
But God helped me in His kindness, / and I escaped when they sought me.
And had the Rock not written me for life, / they would have gathered to kill me and slaughtered me.
My wives they drove from between my arms, / and from my hens they kept me far.

Said the teller: When the people heard his complaint,

I have seen him fear the Lord,

But tonight a company of nobles of the children of Israel came here,

Were it not for them, my haters would have ruled over me, / and crushed my bone and broken my bones ⚠12."

Said the teller: When the man had finished his words and his speech,

and he answered and said:

I am Ḥever, who joins together songs of love ⚠13, / filled with the pearls of testimony.
