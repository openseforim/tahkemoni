(The poem of the ant and the poem of the flea)

Thus says Heman the Ezrahite: My eye saw, in the days of my youth
and the time of my boyhood,
two Hebrew men striving [Exod 2:13],
like warriors armed for war,
with forceful matters in their mouths,
and on their tongues spear and arrows.
To their quarrel the sons of men were gathering,
running from every corner,
and each man pressing against his brother.
One of them was old; his strength had left him [Judg 16:19].
The other was a young man; his eye was not dim, nor his natural force abated [Deut 34:7].
The two of them were like two lions fighting with strong hands,
or like two eagles raging at each other in the midst of the heavens.
This one would say: "I am the Lord's [Isa 44:5];
from the womb He called me [Isa 49:1],
and created me to compose eloquence."
And that one exalted himself, and walked on the circle of the heavens [Job 22:14],
and lifted himself up, saying: "I will be king" [1 Kgs 1:5].
And when the bonfires of the quarrels blazed between them,
and the torches and the voices [Exod 20:15] went up to the heart of the heavens [Deut 4:11] between the two,
they came near to the judge, their faces like faces of flame [Isa 13:8],
and sparks of fire leapt from their eyes,
and the case of the two of them came before God [Exod 22:8] ⚠1.

When they stood before him, they said to him: "If it seems good to our lord, let him search out which of us has the truth,
and let him lay his hand upon us both [Job 9:33]."
The judge said: "Tell me the matter of your quarrels,
and bring forward your strong arguments [Isa 41:21];
and the righteous, his righteousness will help him,
and the wicked, his wickedness will return to his own bosom."
The elder of them said: "Oh my lord [Gen 43:20], incline your ear to my speech,
for I cry out to you of violence [Job 19:7] from my adversary,
who wants to fly through the heavens
without wings,
who seeks to come into battle with me, when he is weary and weak of hands [2 Sam 17:2]."
His adversary said to him: "Behold, pride is your necklace [Ps 73:6] over all of us,
and from on high you speak to us [Ps 73:8].
Now let us know this strength of yours,
and we shall know whether it is fat or lean [Num 13:20];
show us whether your troops are weak or strong,
and your dwellings, whether in camps or in strongholds [Num 13:19].
And when your hidden things are laid bare,
then your words will be tested [Gen 42:16],

and then your light will break forth like the dawn [Isa 58:8]."
His companion said: "And how can you set yourself against me, when on my tongue is the source of understanding and counsel,
and in my heart the spring of eloquence?
For I have the power, by the strength of my thought
and by the eloquence of my tongue,
to exalt every despised and lowly thing,
and to bring low every exalted thing.
And you, if the storms were your camps,
and the winds your chariots,
and the clouds your hosts,
and the lightnings the bearers of your weapons,
and the stars, at the light of your arrows,
went by the gleam of your flashing spear [Hab 3:11],
their heart would melt at standing before me,
and your hands would grow slack at matching my thoughts,
before you could praise an ant,
though it is despised and poor,
and make it a name and a praise."
His adversary said to him: "How you have helped him that has no power [Job 26:2]!
Will you frighten the cedars of Lebanon with nettle and thorn [Hos 9:6]? How trifling is your question,
and how light your riddle!
Now hear what you asked for."

And he answered and said of the ant: "A diligent handmaid,
formed out of the dust,
armed like a warrior,
and in haste to seek her food.
She is thin of loins,
light of foot,
sitting at the opening of the ways [Gen 38:14] ⚠2.
Black and comely,
yet not of the daughters of Eve.
She dresses in black,
and comes out of the holes,
and every day she wakes the dawn,
and rises early at the openings of the gates,
to go around the paths,
on the hills and in the plains,
now in the street, now in the squares [Prov 7:12].
And all the days of winter she hides herself, if they seek her.
And to roam after her food,
her feet do not stay in her house [Prov 7:11].
Every day she goes up hills,
and goes down valleys;
she toils
and does not rest;
she runs
and does not weary;
she gathers and is never still,
to seek her living,
and to find food for her household [Prov 31:15].
She is the poorest of the poor,
and carries great loads;
from the fields she plucks ears of grain [Deut 23:26],
and makes under the ground caves and holes [Isa 2:19],
to fill them with produce,
and ears full and good [Gen 41:22].
Every day she stands at the crossroads [Prov 8:2];
in the evening she comes, and in the morning she returns [Esth 2:14].
All the days of cold she hides in prison houses [Isa 42:22],
and comes down wonderfully low [Lam 1:9].
In the days of summer she goes out with those who go out,
to buy grain among those who came [Gen 42:5].
And when harvest time arrives,
and the messenger of the vintage comes,
she girds her loins with strength [Prov 31:17],
and sets out from her dwellings,
and sets her face toward the threshing floor,
and goes out with the laborers
to beat out the ears of grain [Ruth 2:17],
and goes beside the reapers [Ruth 2:14]
to gather among the sheaves [Ruth 2:7].
Among the threshing floors she roams until they take pity on her,
and among the sheaves she gleans, and they do not shame her [Ruth 2:15];
of their blessing they furnish her [Deut 15:14],
and from the bundles they pull out some for her,
and leave it, and she gleans, and they do not rebuke her [Ruth 2:16].
And one man says to another: Let her alone,
for her soul is bitter within her [2 Kgs 4:27],
and her house is empty, and she has no seed ⚠3.
Every day she roams like a handmaid
after the gleanings and the forgotten sheaves.
With her sense and good judgment
she brings her bread from afar [Prov 31:14],
and lays up grain and food
for a time of drought and leanness.
Every barleycorn she finds she guards like the apple of her eye,
and keeps it under the ground,
and hides it from the storm and the rain;
but the wheat and the spelt were not smitten, for they are late [Exod 9:32].
And when she sees that the days of cold have come,
and the terrible ice [Ezek 1:22],
and every man hides from the rain,
and none goes out and none comes in [Josh 6:1],
then she opens her storehouses and her treasure house [2 Kgs 20:13],
and gives food to her household [Prov 31:15]
of the fruit of her hands [Prov 31:31],
and of the toil of her steps.
By her works she bears witness to the wisdom of her Maker,
and her works praise her in the gates [Prov 31:31].
Happy the wise man who takes instruction from her works,
and woe to the sluggard who does not learn from her deeds,
for he will be left shaken out and empty [Neh 5:13],
like an empty vine [Hos 10:1],
kissing the lip of sorrow,
and embracing the bosom of want."
And he took up his parable and said:

A woman of valor, swarthy [Song 1:6], / praised among the daughters [Song 6:9];
girded with strength on her loins [Prov 31:17], / with no belt and no girdle.
Black she is, like myrrh, / yet not perfumed with myrrh [Song 3:6].
Every morning she goes out to look, / and every dawn she goes seeking.
She rises early to gather food, / and cuts the nights short.
With the laborers she stands / at dawn on the heights of the town.
She heaps up grain and plenty of food / for a time of leanness and drought;
and every grain of wheat she heaps up / she buries in the dust.
In the belly of Sheol [Jonah 2:3] she hews / caves for herself, and digs,
and among the chambers of Sheol she hides / what she gathers and heaps up.
She is no thief, / yet she is found breaking in [Exod 22:1] ⚠4.
In summer she is out in the open, / and in the cold she is hidden.
She stores up her bread for the winter, / and lays it up to be kept [Exod 16:23].
Her wheat is made ready, / and her matzah is guarded [Exod 12:17] ⚠5.
And when harvest time is past, / she is buried alive.
She locks the gates of her city, / and is shut up like Jericho [Josh 6:1].
And when harvest time comes, / she breaks open her doors,
and goes out of her dwelling into exile, only / hurrying to Bethlehem ⚠6,
for she knows that by her exile / she atones for her sins [b. Sanhedrin 37b].
At dawn men are asleep, / and she is awake in the night watch.
On every side and on every road / she roams like a merchant.
She has never learned instruction, / yet she instructs the sons of men.
The sluggard is on his bed [Prov 26:14], / and she wakes the dawn.

The teller said: And when he had finished his poem,
he said to his companion:
"These are the words of eloquence that tear every heart in two,
and draw hearts without hands,
and put to shame the face of the sun on the day it shall be sevenfold [Isa 30:26].
Before them a man like you keeps silent and does not speak.
But let him that glories glory in this [Jer 9:23].
Will you set yourself against me, when my wrath is poured out on you?
Which way did the spirit of the Lord pass from me to speak to you [1 Kgs 22:24]?
Will you set earthen pitchers [Lam 4:2] beside pearls,
and liken the light of the sun to things of darkness?
And when did the spirit of God come mightily upon you [Judg 14:6],
and wisdom, where shall it come from [Job 28:20] to make you wise? I lift up my hand to the Lord [Gen 14:22]: if you tore the dawn apart,
and spread out the mountains,
and split the rivers,
and the host of heaven were your troops,
and the Bear with her children [Job 38:32] your servants,
and you raised your banners and standards on the top of a mound,
and fire itself were burned by the fire of your brushwood [Isa 64:1] ⚠7,
your heart would tremble at standing against me,
and your spirit grow faint at the sound of my terror,
and your words would be rash [Job 6:3],
and your mighty men dismayed,

and the gallopings of your steeds [Judg 5:22] would grow weary,
and the horses of your sayings would stumble,
in reaching the dust of my chariot,
and in rising to the height of my speech,
before you could, like me, expound and explain
the matters of the flea."
His companion said to him: "The eagles do not tremble
at the voice of the birds,
nor are the wolves afraid
at the sound of the wings of flies.
Did you think you had frightened me with your riddle? Now incline your ear to your question,
and hear your answer…" And he answered, to expound and explain
the matters of the flea.

And he answered and said of the flea: "A Cushite, but not of the sons of Ham,
black as coal.
He eats the bread of wickedness [Prov 4:17],
and fights without a weapon ⚠8,
and grows hot like an oven [Hos 7:7].
He lies in wait like a thief in your house,
and lies down in your garment,
in your bedchamber and on your bed [Exod 7:28].
He takes your flesh all night to eat it;
and if all day you keep his food from him,
by the time the sun goes down you must give it back to him [Exod 22:25].
And when souls are caught in the cords of sleep,
and deep sleep falls upon men [Job 4:13],
he comes to you softly [Judg 4:21],
to roll himself upon you,
and to fall upon you [Gen 43:18],
and drains your blood, waking and dreaming,
and sheds the blood of war in peace [1 Kgs 2:5].
And if you look for him,
he passes by, and lo, he is not [Ps 37:36];
and if you say: 'I will run after him' [2 Kgs 5:20],
he flees and goes outside [Gen 39:12];
and if you catch him once or twice,
he slips from between your hands,
for he surely makes himself wings,
like an eagle he flies in the heavens [Prov 23:5].
And many times he hides with the maidens under dyed embroidery [Judg 5:30],
and goes from the loins to the thighs [Exod 28:42],
and hides with them between the breasts,

and calls the name of that place Maḥanaim [Gen 32:3] ⚠9.
And if he finds a girl, a virgin,
or a married woman,
he cleaves to her and lies beside her,
until she raises her voice at his evil deeds,
and the girl cries out, and there is none to save her [Deut 22:27].
And if they ask her: 'Why do you weep and not keep silent?'
she says: 'The Cushite slave came in to me [Gen 39:17] ⚠10,
and made his bed in my bosom:
my beloved is to me a bundle of myrrh that lies between my breasts [Song 1:13].
All night he made me lie down in sorrow [Isa 50:11],
and took for himself, for a couch and a standing place ⚠11,
the shoulder and the two cheeks and the maw [Deut 18:3].'
He is like a priest, a sprinkler son of a sprinkler [b. Berakhot 28a],
who has taken the thigh and the breast to eat,
and to his share fell the breast of the wave offering and the thigh of the heave offering [Lev 7:34],
and its fat, the whole fat tail [Lev 3:9].
All night he rakes up the fire of battle,
and does not lie down until he eats prey and drinks the blood of the slain [Num 23:24].
Without a bow he shoots at my skin,
and without a spear he pierces my flesh.
He is small, and his deeds are great;
weak, yet he lays giants low;
despised, yet mighty in deeds [2 Sam 23:20].
No net can catch him,
and no arrow makes him flee [Job 41:20].
The captains of the host he has laid low in his war,
and the mighty he has subdued in his prison,
and the sons of the giants too we saw there [Num 13:28].
If you hide from him behind bar and doors,
he comes to you between the two walls [Isa 22:11],
and comes down on you from heaven.
And when you think you have escaped him,
you find him in your bosom,
and carry him on your shoulder.
And if you kill his soldiers and cut them to pieces,
their kinsmen come to shed your blood while you feel secure:
the avenger of blood himself shall put the murderer to death [Num 35:19].
And if he finds no way into the king's palace,
he hides in his clothes, and walks about under his couches,
and comes to him at night like a guest and a traveler.
On that night the king could not sleep [Esth 6:1],
and all he rules over is despised in his eyes;
and he calls his servants in the night,
and they spread out the garment [Deut 22:17],
and the maids cry out loudly to one another,
and the lamps, five on the right and five on the left [1 Kgs 7:49];
and he hides from them, and they wait for him,
and he laughs at them, and they weep.
And if he were found,
his blood would be drained [Lev 1:15].
But everywhere he has a refuge and an escape,
and in every garment he finds a city of refuge.
And the king, if his hand reaches him,
and he seizes him by his garment [Gen 39:12],
does not rest until he has poured out his gall on the ground [Job 16:13],
and the king's wrath abated [Esth 7:10].
Lo, these are the fringes of his ways [Job 26:14],
and a whisper of his goings [Job 26:14].
For every day he delights in every food,
and when the sun is down he is clean, and afterward he may eat [Lev 22:7].
He harms the innocent without guilt,
and laps up the blood of rich and poor [1 Kgs 21:19],
and swallows them alive like Sheol [Prov 1:12].
Such is his manner all his days [1 Sam 27:11],
blood touching blood [Hos 4:2].
And it came to pass, when his days there had grown long [Gen 26:8],
and he sees that the day is short,
and the heat is wanting,
and the wind drives him,
and the cold strikes him,

and the rain sweeps him away,
he hides in holes of the dust [Isa 2:19],
and digs his dwelling beneath the earth.
All his troops flee and make ready baggage for exile [Jer 46:19],
and they and all that is theirs go down alive into Sheol [Num 16:33],
and all the days of cold they make their home in Sheol,
and their dwelling among the clods of the dust,
and they stay in their places in the camp until they recover [Josh 5:8].
And when they see the rain cease,
and the cold grow less,
and the heat grow great,
their time comes,
and they awake from the place of their dwelling [Ps 33:14],
and from the dust of the earth they bud and blossom [Isa 27:6],
and many of those who sleep in the dust of the earth awake [Dan 12:2]."
And he took up his parable and said:

Child of night, the pupil of darkness [Prov 20:20] covers him [Ps 139:11], / and the pit of gloom refined him sevenfold.
Were it not that his wings hovered over him, / the burning of his fire would have consumed him in a moment.
You would think his work is the work of fire: / so he is black, and the flame has scorched him [Song 1:6].
He flies only on the wings of the dark, / and steals away, and none knows of his flight.
He has struck me, and my healing is in killing him: / so in him I find one who strikes and heals [Isa 19:22].
The edge of his sword [Ps 89:44] is in his teeth and in his mouth, / and for my blood he draws it every moment.
His blood is like a reddened lily; / but he plucked it before it bloomed ⚠12.
He comes to my bed at midnight, / and the door a man bars [Neh 7:3] is of no use.
His way is the way of the bat, / that spreads its wing by night.
You lay him down in the dark of night / upon my back, and the dawn sends him flying.
He cuts short my slumber, and yet he length- / ens my night, and sets its head at its end ⚠13,
as though he found in me streams of honey, / when he comes to me thirsty of heart and weary.
He is small, young in days and young in body, / and the hand of the day can toss him with no wind.
I think he is a dot of ink / that escaped from the pen's tongue as it dripped.

The teller said: And when the two of them had completed their sayings,
and finished their poems,
the judge's heart trembled at the force of their words,
and he said: "I lift up my hand to God Most High [Gen 14:22],
who dwells in the height of His hiding place [Hab 3:4],
if my eyes have ever seen the like of this sight,
or my ears heard such a thing.
Behold, I know that none can be compared to you
among all the sons of your generation,
for each one of you is a hero in his battle,
and skilled in his work [Prov 22:29].
Now, the two of you, make a covenant of love,
and put away the enmity;
and as God has joined eloquence between you,
be joined, the two of you, in friendship ⚠14."
And the judge furnished them of his blessing [Deut 15:14],
and bestowed his bounty upon them,
and they went out from before him, glad in his praise,
laden with his generosity.
And when they went out from before him, I went out after them,
and asked the elder of them to tell me his name,
and to make me understand his secret.
And he answered and said:

I am Ḥever, and this is my fruit; but / he and I are both young lions of eloquence.
I build paths among the ruins, / and he raises up the broken ruins of song [Amos 9:11].

When I heard his poem, I knew that he was our friend Ḥever the Kenite,
and that the second was his son.
And I parted from them,
wondering at them,
and journeyed away from them, and left my heart with them.
