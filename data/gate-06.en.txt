Thus says Heman the Ezrahite: I encamped at Thebez [Judg 9:50],
to take my delight in pleasures at my resting place,
and to put on, from the mantle of the lilies, a checkered tunic [Exod 28:4].
And it came to pass one day, as I sat with beloved friends of the sons of the noble,
to take our fill of love [Prov 7:18],
and to delight in sweet songs,
that I lifted up my eyes and looked, and behold, a man standing before me, weary and weak of hands [2 Sam 17:2],
with a melting heart and knees knocking together [Nah 2:11].
When I saw his likeness from afar,
I looked closely at his form,
and behold, it was Ḥever the Kenite, and I hurried to meet him,
and greeted him first with peace.
I asked him about his affairs,
and where he was setting his face.
He said to me: "I have just come from the islands of the sea [Isa 11:11],
and was saved from the roar of its waves and their pounding [Ps 93:3].
My face is set eastward,
and I put my life in my hand [Judg 12:3]."
I said to him: "How long will you not be quiet and not rest,
as if your dwelling were on the wings of the wind [Ps 104:3]?
Stay with us, and we will do you good [Num 10:29];
settle among us, and you will gain your wish.
And if you like, I will seek a resting place for you [Ruth 3:1],
and give you a wife, through whom you will renew your strength [Isa 40:31],
and find rest."
He said to me: "I adjure you by the life of every prophet and seer,
speak to me no more of this matter,
not awake and not in a vision.
For I have sworn a strong and hard oath,
never to fall into the snare of a woman.
Enough for me is the first bitter trouble;
trouble shall not rise up twice [Nah 1:9]."
I laughed at his words,
and said to him: "What is the trouble that has found you,
and brought you to an oath?"
He said: "The serpent beguiled me [Gen 3:13],
and the evil impulse enticed me,
to seek a companion for myself,
and a woman fair and lovely,
to bring rest to my sick soul,
from shortness of spirit and from hard labour.
For I said: It is not good that I should be alone;
I will make me a help meet for me [Gen 2:18].
For I do not know the day of my death [Gen 27:2],
and when shall I provide for my own house also [Gen 30:30]?
And while I was still among the waves of the sea of thoughts,
my innards waging wars within me ⚠1,
and hewing flames [Ps 29:7] out of loves,
and my heart now refusing and now willing,
at times blazing and at times going out,
I was walking about the city,
from side to side and from corner to corner,
and behold, an old woman,
evil of shape,
and strange of form,
as if Time had stolen her from the demons to be his portion.
And he called her name Sitnah [Gen 26:21] ⚠2.
Her shape was like an ostrich,
and her end bitter as wormwood [Prov 5:4].
Her face was wrapped in her veil;
evil and imperious,
she made her voice gracious [Prov 26:25], and bent down on her knee,
and her palate was smoother than oil [Prov 5:3].
Honey was on her tongue,
and deadly poison in her throat.
She behaved with piety,
but she was a daughter of perverse rebellion [1 Sam 20:30].
Her paths were the paths of Sheol;
in her house her feet would not abide [Prov 7:11],
and in every place her slain were found [Prov 7:26].
When she saw my likeness from afar,
she stood against me like a satan [Num 22:22],
and greeted me first with peace.
She said to me: 'My son, my son, may God lengthen your life,
and keep the glow of your cheeks,
and may your fruit be ever fresh.
I see you are pleasant and fair,
and goodly to look on.
You ravish hearts with your beauty,
and draw souls by the radiance of your cheeks.
Beauty has clothed you in its silk,
and paid you its debt in full ⚠3,
and the tree of your youth has given its fruit.
It is not good for a man like you to sleep while his heart is awake [Song 5:2],
burning in the fire of desire,
with no companion to keep you company,
and no place of seed [Num 20:5] ⚠4.
Why should you seek broken cisterns, hewn out [Jer 2:13],
and stolen waters be sweet to you [Prov 9:17]?
So if it pleases you, from the daughters of the noble
I will give you a loving hind [Prov 5:19]:
her cheek brings on the dawns,
and her hair brings on the evenings ⚠5.
Your soul will take its delight in her,
and she will be your attendant [1 Kgs 1:2],
nesting in your bosom,
and singing at your table.
In her cheek is a lampstand, and in her mouth a jar ⚠6.
By the radiance of her form you walk through darkness [Job 29:3],
and her lamp does not go out by night [Prov 31:18].
Her merchandise is better than the merchandise of silver [Prov 3:14],
and her price is far above pearls [Prov 31:10].
She has eyes like the eyes of gazelles,
drunk with the wine of desire.
Her body is fresh and moist,
like a sceptre of crystal,
pure and holy, seasoned with salt [Exod 30:35].
She pierces the heart of those who see her,
and sends a fire into it.
Her cheeks are luminaries,
and her eyes like young lions ⚠7,
and the rows of her teeth sapphires,
and her two breasts like two fawns [Song 4:5].
If you saw her lovely beauty,
your heart would fail and be astonished,
and you would be driven mad by the sight of your eyes which you see [Deut 28:34].
Happy the man who rides in her chariot,
and between her feet sinks down, falls, lies [Judg 5:27]!
But whoever wishes to marry her,
must give two thousand pieces of silver;
and it is a light thing to give all his wealth and substance for her.
If a man gave all the substance of his house for love, he would be utterly scorned [Song 8:7].'

Said the teller: When I heard her sayings,
my heart was drawn by the smoothness of her words [Prov 7:21],
and I was bound in the cords of her knots.
I said to her: 'If only I could see this girl before she is spoken for,
then my heart would be glad when I take her.'
She said to me: 'Far be it, far be it,
that I should deceive you or make you stumble,
or entice you with my words or speak to you in riddles.
But all that I have spoken with mouth and lips,
you will find it twofold [Job 11:6].'
I said to her: 'If the woman is as you tell me,
ask of me ever so much bride-price and gift,
and I will give as you say to me [Gen 34:12].'
She said to me: 'When you see the gazelle,
who tears her prey like a lioness ⚠8,
you will know there is no deceit on my tongue.
And I will come back to you tomorrow,
after the dawn comes up,
to quiet the grief of your heart and its anguish;
and in the morning the LORD will make known who is His [Num 16:5].'
And the woman went on her way,
and my heart was caught on the hook of her palate ⚠9.
That night sleep fled from my eyes [Gen 31:40],
and I did not go up to my bed;
I only wandered in the cords of my longing,
and fretted over the length of my hope.
And in the morning my spirit was troubled [Gen 41:8],
and while I was still restless in my complaint [Ps 55:3],
with a sick heart and a broken soul,
the sun had risen upon the earth [Gen 19:23], and behold, the old woman came.
My heart rejoiced at her coming,
and I came trembling to meet her,
and greeted her first with peace.
She said: 'May God keep your peace for ever,
and may your place never be empty.
Know that the turn of joy has come,
and sorrow and sighing have fled away [Isa 35:10],
and your wanderings are far from you,
and the time of love has drawn near [Ezek 16:8].
Behold, the girl's father and her kinsmen are coming to you,
to hear your words,
and to bind their covenant upon you.'
And while it was thus and thus, behold, the girl's father,
and with him all the elders at the gate.
And when the assembly had come,
and the house was full from end to end [2 Kgs 10:21],
the girl's father rose and stood in his place,
and said to all the men of his council: 'Peace be upon you, our brothers, men of faith,
and of faithful love.
Know that this man wishes to join our fellowship,
and to come into the bond of our covenant [Ezek 20:37],
and to ride in our chariot,
and to take our daughter.
We have been told good things of his praises,
and of the worth of his deeds;
for he is of the families of the governors ⚠10,
in whose hands are portions,
and in whose presence is fullness of joys [Ps 16:11].
So I entrust my soul to him,
and I will give him my daughter,
and he shall surely pay the bride-price for her [Exod 22:15],
on condition that he writes for her two thousand pieces of silver as the bride-price of her virginity [Exod 22:16],
and gives it to her whenever she wishes.'
The elders at the gate said to me: 'Do you accept all you have heard?' And I, for my great shame before them,
did not look into their words,
but only said: 'I have heard and I accept,
and I know all their terms.'
The word went out of my mouth [Esth 7:8], and they hurried and called the scribe,
and brought the pen and the book,
and he wrote her a marriage deed
broader than the sea [Job 11:9].
And I accepted all that they said to me.
Then I said: Lo, I am come; in the roll of the book it is written of me [Ps 40:8]:

They called a scribe to confirm for her / the bride-price of her marriage deed in full detail.
They wrote against me a binding bond, / firm, with an increment and new clauses.
They blessed God's name there over a cup of wine, / yes, with canopy and betrothal.
And Time, mocking them, how suddenly / he turned my union ⚠11 into divorce!

When the deed was written, they read it before the Jews.
And I wrote in the book, and sealed it, and took witnesses [Jer 32:10].
And when the sun went down,
the wrath came up ⚠12,
and darkness and gloom covered the earth.
They brought the bride,
with the noise of a tumult [Jer 11:16],
and the people answering along the highway:
'May the bridegroom rejoice in the bride!'
And when they had sat long singing in gladness,
from evening until midnight,
the whole company went each on his way,
from the least of them to the greatest [Jonah 3:5],
and I was left alone,
and she sitting at my side.
I said in my heart: This night I will renew my youth like the eagle [Ps 103:5],
and mount up with wings as eagles [Isa 40:31],
and this is a night of watching [Exod 12:42].
So I turned aside to her [Exod 3:3],
and took her robes off her,
and removed the veil that was on her face [Exod 34:33],
and brought the lamp close to her face.
And behold, her face was a face of fury,
and her voice like the sound of thunder,
and her form like the calf of Jeroboam [1 Kgs 12:28],
and her mouth like the mouth of Balaam's ass [Num 22:28].
Her nostrils, their smell stank,
and the moisture of her cheeks had fled [Deut 34:7],
as if Satan had plastered them with blackness,
and done his work in coal,
until I took her for one of the daughters of Ham [Gen 10:6].
But though her form was darker than blackness [Lam 4:8],
her hair had turned white,
and her days had grown old,
and her lips dared to climb upward ⚠13,
and her teeth like the teeth of wolves or bears,
and her eyes the eyes of scorpions.
And I answered and said:

In her teeth she is like the tooth of bears, / that devour what they find and destroy.
Her head is full of boils, and her eyes / rob the heart of its joy and bring it to an end.
Her stature is like a wall, and her legs / like two trees of the forest, cut down.
Her cheek is like coal, only her lips / are the lips of a bony ass [Gen 49:14], twisted.
Her form is like the form of the angels of death; / all who meet her fall dead.

When I saw this sight, a trembling fell upon me [Dan 10:7],
and fear came upon me, and quaking [Job 4:14],
and I said:

'The day of reckoning has come [Hos 9:7]!'
But I held myself in [Gen 43:31],
and spoke to her softly,
and said to her: 'My daughter, since your face is like briers and thorns [Isa 5:6],
tell me, what have you in the house [2 Kgs 4:2]?
Where are your garments and your robes,
and your seals and your cords,
and your crescents [Isa 3:18] and your earrings,
and the ointments of your adornment,
and your vessels of oil?'
She said to me: 'I lack nothing,
for God has dealt graciously with me, and I have everything [Gen 33:11].
All my clothes I left bundled up in the house of my lord my father,
in the abode where I dwell.'
I said to her: 'And what is the deposit
that you left in your lord's house?'
She said to me: 'I left two worn-out sacks [Josh 9:4],
and broken vessels,
a platter and a pan,
a kerchief and a can,
a staff and a sack,
and torn garments,
and stained vessels,
and two bowls,
and three cooking pots.'
When I heard her words I fell on my face,
and my thoughts were lost,
and they found no vision from the LORD [Lam 2:9].
And I took up my parable and said:

Were you hewn out of demons and fetters ⚠14? / May God send the curse upon you [Deut 28:20]!
It is as if the angels of wrath and the Destroyer / were brothers to you, but yours is the birthright.
It is as if your form were the goat for Azazel [Lev 16:10]; / how then did you escape from the land cut off [Lev 16:22]?
It is as if Time had lusted after Lilith, / and for love sent you to her as a present [1 Sam 9:7].
Your Maker made you only so that / you would be a help to the angels of death.
Happy your father, had you died at your birth; / would that your mother had been barren!
Your lips are like the lips of oxen, / your mouth a grave [Ps 5:10], and your belly a cave.
Your teeth like the teeth of a bear, filthy, / and in them dung and spittle, a bowlful.
Your eyes like a smoking stove, / your face like the bottom of a pot,
your stature high like Haman's tree [Esth 7:9]; / would that I might see you on it soon!
The night you came I reckoned like the coming / of the angels of wrath when they come in fury ⚠15,
like Pharaoh's night, when he sank in the Red Sea; / like Haman on the day he was hanged on the beam;
like Sisera when he fled to Jael [Judg 4:17]; / like Eglon in the cool upper chamber [Judg 3:20].
You have a belly like a swollen wineskin, / and your form is black as your star.
Your hands are an ape's hands, hairy, / and your fingers like brands in a stove.
The sound of your speech is like an earthquake at midnight, / and the breath of your mouth like a stormwind.
Your mouth is a grave for your food and your drink, / and they turn to gall inside your belly [Job 20:14].
With your tooth you pound everything as in a mortar [Prov 27:22], / and from there the people journeyed to Beer [Num 21:16] ⚠16.
May God overturn your head into the pit, / like the overthrow of Sodom and Gomorrah [Deut 29:22],
and may your shroud be made of the bellies of lions ⚠17, / and may He set your grave inside them!

When I had finished my poem,
I saw that by my poem
my pangs would not be healed,
and by my musing
my grief would not depart,
and my song
would not take away my anguish of heart [Lam 3:65].
My wrath rose up in my nostrils,
and I took three rods in my hand,
and rose at midnight,
and muzzled her mouth [Deut 25:4] with a cloak,
and broke all the rods on her back,
until her heart's blood came out of her mouth,
and I tore all her flesh off her,
and brought out on her body the blood of her virginity [Deut 22:17].
Then I gathered all the clothes with me,
and loaded everything on my beast [Gen 44:13],
and before the dawn came up I was far from the city.
I left her land in desolation [Isa 24:12], utterly emptied [Isa 24:3],
and passed over the ford of the Jabbok [Gen 32:23].
All day I lay in the forests,
and by night I journeyed and rode,
until I knew that I had escaped,
and believed that I was saved.
Then I took up my parable and said:

Blessed is He who kept me in the day of distress, / and in His mercy dealt kindly with me.
My impulse sold me into the hand of my folly, / but the Rock in His compassion redeemed me.
After I had come into the chambers of Sheol [Prov 7:27], / He opened the belly of Sheol [Jonah 2:3] and brought me up."

Said the teller: When I heard the delusions of Ḥever the Kenite,
I laughed at his vanities and his lies,
and wondered at his dreams and at his words.
After that I asked after his welfare,
and journeyed away from him;
he too journeyed to his place,
and his word and his speech left me in wonder.
