Thus says Heman the Ezrahite:
I traveled with merchandise to the land of Basra ⚠1.
And it came to pass one day, as I walked about in the square of the city to see the people of the town,
the great and the small,
that I saw choice young men [Ezek 23:6] gathered to speak words of wisdom,
and in their mouths were forceful matters of speech.
I drew near to them,
to smell the blended spices of their perfumes,
and to drink my fill from the streams of their rains.
And while I was still gazing at them,
and listening to their sayings,
behold, an old man stood before them,
and said to them: "Lift up your eyes to heaven,
and remember your God,
and do not trust in your riches.
For the world will come to an end,
and wear away like a rotten thing [Job 13:28].
A man has no rest except in the work of righteousness [Isa 32:17],
and it will save him from going down to the pit [Job 33:24],
and from the blazing fire [Exod 9:24].

I am the man [Lam 3:1]
who has seen, in the land of wandering [Gen 4:16], every grief and every breaking,
and I had all but gone down to the grave.
On the journeys of the roads I have drunk
cups mixed with wormwood.
I have seen how many troubles and mishaps,
and evil happenings;
for there is no toil as evil as journeying on the roads,
on which the thoughts are confounded.
And so the proverb-makers have said: 'Going
is confusion,
and travel
is mishap,
and parting
is trembling.'
Whoever travels from his land,
and goes out from his desired haven [Ps 107:30],
his soul is sated with evils [Ps 88:4],
and he does not lift up his head.
The sun smites him [Jonah 4:8],
the storm crushes him,
the heat burns him,
the rain sweeps him away,
and the wind drives him.
He goes from dawn until darkness,
by day the heat, and by night the frost [Gen 31:40].
Now he climbs hills,
and now he goes down into valleys.
He cleaves through rivers,
and stamps over mountains.
He treads forests and deserts,
and crosses waves and breakers [Jonah 2:4].
He goes through steppes and dry lands,
among serpents and lions,
and ravenous beasts [Isa 35:9].
If rains come down upon him,
terrors frighten him,
and his eyes run with blood like the rains.
He grows weary in his going,
and suffers in his going up and his coming down.
And if he falls from his carriage,
he dips his garments in mud and water,
and his horse swims in water up to the ankles [Ezek 47:3];
and the entrails and the legs
he washes in water [Lev 1:9] ⚠2,
and his heart is between oven and stove [Lev 11:35].
Besides all this there is the danger of the road,
and much fear and faintness of heart [Lev 26:36].
For if robbers fall upon him, they bring him to the gates of death [Ps 107:18];
and if he escapes, they leave him naked;
as he came out of his mother's womb [Eccl 5:14], they let him go.
And if he goes on foot,
he meets harm and sickness:
for the stones hurt him,
and the thorns distress him,
and the pits throw him on his face,
and the fords dip him in their waters.
And if snow falls on him,
then his pains are doubled;
his garments are white,
but his heart is black with many griefs,
like the dovecote of the doves,
white outside and black inside.
And if he goes to sea,
and its waves lift up their pounding [Ps 93:3],
and bring out against him the weapons of its wrath [Jer 50:25],
then another spirit is with him [Num 14:24] ⚠3;
or he curses his day [Job 3:1],
and loathes his world,
when he sees the ship going up to the heights,
and going down to the deeps [Ps 107:26],
and his bowels within him
going up and coming down on it [Gen 28:12],
until he casts his merchandise into the sea,
to save his soul from death.
And if he escapes from the sea, he rejoices with a joy that cannot be measured [Hos 2:1],
and thinks that he was born on that day.
And though his wealth has left him in the sea,
and he returns naked [Eccl 5:14] on that day,
he is glad, poor and wretched though he is,
for his life has been given him as spoil [Jer 21:9].
And after all these troubles,
and bitter hardships,
if he does not gain what he asked by his journey,
his toil is in vain.
Behold, all this befalls everyone who travels from his dwelling,
like the bird that wanders from its nest [Prov 27:8],
until he beats his cheeks,
and chooses death rather than life [Jer 8:3]."
And he took up his parable:

He who wears out his days in travel / is a toiler and never lacks weariness.
His soul is girded with a girdle of grief, / and the pupil of his eye is widened with the kohl of night [Jer 4:30].
He waters the parched places [Jer 17:6] with his tears, / until things grow in a land not sown [Jer 2:2].
When he thinks to find rest and quiet, / Time wakes and sounds the alarm.
His feet tread day and night, as though / an oath were on them against finding quiet.
His soul flits day after day like a bird, / as though it were slung in the hollow of a sling [1 Sam 25:29].
She thinks her house a prison, and her wandering / a herald of good, announcing salvation [Isa 52:7] ⚠4.

When he had finished his words
and his song,
a man of the company said to him:
"We know that you have a clear tongue [Zeph 3:9].
But you have told the happenings of travel,
and left its evil report [Num 14:37] fixed for the last generation.
Now tell its good qualities,
as you have told its bad qualities,
and recall its delights as you have recalled its mishaps."

He answered and said: "Though travel is evil, and going is confusion,
and mixed with wormwood,
is it not by travel
that he who has stumbled finds salvation,
and by his going
that healing comes up for his wound [Jer 30:13]?
For by it a man gains his desires,
and his vanguard prevails,
and he girds his loins like a mighty man [Job 38:3],
and bends his arrows at the heart of his enemies.
Were it not for travel, his fortune would not rise in a moment,
and he would not grow rich, nor would his wealth endure [Job 15:29].
He who travels the roads increases his good in a few days,
and his gold is doubled.
If you see him today naked and a wayfarer,
tomorrow he will look like a king's son [Judg 8:18].
But he who stays in his house,
and does not go out from around his bed,
his disgrace does not leave him;
for he does not come out of his trouble
until he comes out of his land,
and his grief does not move from his heart
until he moves from his dwelling.
And as long as he stays in his land,
he will not find his desire.
And so the proverb-makers say: 'Travel
is salvation,
and going
is blessing.
Whoever travels
touches his desire,
and whoever goes
is master over his desire,
and he who roams
gathers the fruit of his desires;
but he who sits still
is reckoned as a dumb stone [Hab 2:19].'
In his going he sees new faces,
and heals from his heart grievous wounds [Mic 1:9].
His gladness is renewed,
and his lamp shines [Job 29:3],
his turmoil is stilled,
and he puts away the grief of his thoughts.
He returns to his land with a full purse,
and his star rising,
and finds souls longing for him,
and eyes looking out to the parting of the way [Ezek 21:26]."
And he took up his parable:

He whose heart is afraid of travel / is sated with calamity and never lacks weariness.
The sluggard turns round and round his house [Prov 26:14], / and he is like the peg driven fast [Isa 22:25].
His grief never leaves his heart, / and from evil he goes out to evil [Jer 9:2].
But the wise man roams the west and the east, / until he finds salvation for his trouble.
The diligent man loathes sitting still, / until by moving he gains his desires.
He sees the wonders of the world as he goes, / and heaps up wealth, and learns understanding and knowledge.
And when he returns, he is to those who wait for him / a herald of good, announcing salvation [Isa 52:7].

When he had finished his songs
and the fullness of his words,
all the people loaded him with their blessings [Deut 15:14],
and suckled him from the breasts of their gifts,
and asked him his name.
He answered and said:

I am Ḥever, who joins pearls together ⚠5, / and my sayings are thorns in the heart of my foes.
With my praises I raise up the lowly, / and with my tongue I hew off the head of the proud.
By the goodness of my speech I raise up all the despised, / and sate the men of pride with shame [Hab 2:16].
The honored I bring down to Sheol, / and the lowly I raise up to the clouds.
