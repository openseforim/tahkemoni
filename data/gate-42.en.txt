Thus says Heman the Ezrahite: One day I was sitting in a company of men of understanding,
and with generous nobles.
From the necklace of their minds they scatter rows of jacinth [Exod 28:19],
and from the cloud of their teaching, drops of rain ⚠1;
princes of the congregation, called to the assembly, men of renown [Num 16:2].
And while we were still speaking of matters of discourse,
overlaid with sapphires [Song 5:14],
more precious than pearls [Prov 3:15] and mother-of-pearl,
there passed by us one of the honored princes,
one of those who do deeds of kindness.
And when he heard the eloquence of our words,
he longed for the sweetness of our sayings,
and sat down with us,
and began to speak with us
on matters of mind and understanding,
and to uncover the hidden things of discernment.
He revealed to us wonders from his poems,
and showed us awesome things,
whose like the hearers had never heard,
and which had never reached them.
And after he had sat long,
and spent much thought,
he rose and went from us,
after he had given an allowance and a gift [Jer 40:5] to all of us,
and bade us farewell,
and returned to his place.
And when he had gone from us,
the men of the company wondered at the honor he had done us,
and said: "Look at this man!
Have you seen his like, awake or in a vision?
See how he drew our hearts with his good gifts,
and conquered our souls by his generosity."
One of them said: "By this you may know that among all the virtues there is none like generosity,
and all things desired cannot compare with it [Prov 3:15],
and all the virtues bow at its feet.
Many daughters have done valiantly, but it has risen above them all [Prov 31:29]."
And there was among them a man
who could make the mountains of wisdom quake,
and sting like an adder [Prov 23:32].
He said to them: "You have said that generosity is the highest of all the virtues,
and that it comes up first;
but I, if I wish, will make it last,
and bring it down to the lowest step."
All the people said to him: "Truly you would be a man of valor
if you could make the day into night,
and a young lion into a ram."
Then a young man from among their men of understanding came forward,
one of their nobles,
and said to him: "Here I come to you
to probe your words.
I will uphold the case of generosity and praise it,
and make it greater than every virtue;
and you, if you can, bring it low,
and throw it down from its station."
The old man said: "My ear is attentive [Neh 1:6] to your sayings;
now let me hear your words."

The young man began and said: "To you, O men, I call [Prov 8:4],
men of counsel and might.
Do you not know,
have you not heard,
since the day you were born,
that generosity is mistress over all the virtues,
and a crown on their head?
It is the glory of kings,
and the diadem of princes.
It gives spirit to the generous,
and makes their hands like clouds,
and their faces like stars.
Were it not for generosity, the hand of meanness would prevail,
and the honored would dwell in lowliness,
and the hand of the misers would be high.
The upright would be put to forced labor,
and the earth would be filled with violence [Gen 6:11].
The honored would become
slaves,
and the nobles,
churls.
The strong would be without strength,
and the lofty would dwell in the pit of sorrow and poverty.
And what could a lawgiver press for more than generosity ⚠2?
It is like a choice vine [Jer 2:21], and all besides it an empty vine [Hos 10:1]."
And he took up his parable and said:

Truly, generosity is mistress / over every virtue, and all besides it are young servants.
Were it not for it, the glory of the world would come to an end ⚠3, / and for its sake every glory is formed and created.
By it a man subdues the rage of kings, / and calms the sea of their wrath with a gift.
The virtues are low beside it, / for to it belongs an arm with might [Ps 89:14].
Every height falls short of its height, / and every tongue is too short for its praise.

The old man said: "Shall a multitude of words go unanswered [Job 11:2]?
And shall your mockeries hear no reply?
What is the merit of generosity,
when it lays its owners down in sorrow [Isa 50:11],
and strips a man's house bare of every good?
It scatters his wealth,
and breaks his pride,
and buries him while he lives,
and makes his heart drunk with the wine of his grief.
It brings low its great ones,
and casts down its distinguished ones,
and humbles its nobles
to its lowly,
and its generous to its churls.
It leaves the hands empty,
void and waste [Nah 2:11].
But whoever closes his right hand fills his treasuries with wealth,
and his good things increase,
and his riches grow great,
and his light shines,
and his fortune grows,
and his blessing is doubled.
Had the miser been generous,
grief would have made his gentle heart pine ⚠4,
and he would be left a poor man and lightly esteemed [1 Sam 18:23],
an outcast and an exile.
But by closing the hand
a man is honored,
and his vessels are filled with wealth,
and his tents with blessing,
and his lamp does not go out [Prov 31:18].
And of him the chosen of the Lord said: 'He who gathers by hand shall increase' [Prov 13:11] ⚠5."
And he took up his parable and said:

He who closes his right hand gathers wealth, / and it will be his help in the day of trouble.
His treasuries are filled with riches, / and his good things are laid out and kept.
The generous man empties his treasure-house [2 Kgs 20:13], / and leaves in his hand not even a small coin [1 Sam 2:36].
The miser has blessing in his barns [Deut 28:8], / and the generous man a curse in his treasuries.
This one's houses are full of his great wealth, / and that one's hand lacks every good thing.

The young man answered and said: "You deaf, hear wonders,
and you blind, look and see [Isa 42:18]!
Have you seen a slave boast over his master and mock him,
or a fox roar at a young lion?
You thought, by the length of your tongue
and the number of your musings,
to turn beauty
to blemish,
and kindness
to disgrace ⚠6,
and greatness
to churlishness,
and strength
to lowliness,
and generosity
to slander.
You boast before me of the churlishness of churls,
and pride yourself on the quality of the lowly.
Do you not know that because of a man's giving his blessing does not fail,
and because of his generosity
his good is not lost?
Like a spring of water: if men drink from it, its flow increases,
and its brooks grow strong,
and its currents do not dry up,
and its waters do not fail [Isa 58:11];
but if they do not draw from it, their waters at times fail,
and their pools dry up.
So too, the more giving and generosity,
the more good;
and by closing the hand,
wealth comes to an end and is lost.
And if you say that there are churls who heap up riches:
all their riches are without worth,
and in the end they fly away like an eagle [Prov 23:5].
And if there are generous men whose wealth was lost,
and whose money dwindled,
they were not made poor because of their generosity,
but God wished to test them."
And he took up his parable and said:

Generosity is a crown on the head of the generous, / but the miser, his blessing is cursed.
The good of every miserly man is as nothing, / for it is forbidden to him and to every man.
His wealth is great, yet he dies of hunger, / and his hand is held back from doing himself good.
He is like a man who stands by a river of delights [Ps 36:9], / and slakes his thirst with swamp water and steepings.
His good is a deposit in his hand, / kept by him for a stranger [Eccl 6:2].
The generous man is honored in every man's eye, / and the miser is cursed of God [Prov 22:14] in wrath.

The old man answered and said: "Is there an answer in your mouth?
And to which of the holy ones will you turn [Job 5:1]?
Do you not see that in all the lands of the world,
from Egypt to Elam,
you will not find a generous man without crookedness,
save one in a thousand [Eccl 7:28]?
The rest all close their hands,
from the least of them to the greatest [Jer 6:13];
and by this very thing the children of Elam grew rich ⚠7.
And here is the sign for you that generosity is a bad and faulty quality:
all the sons of men reject it,
and trample it under the soles of their feet.
Had it been a good quality, great and small would have learned it,
and the lowly and the lofty would have held fast to it.
But since all the people of the world reject it,
by this you may know that it is rejected,
and laden with every blemish.
I would almost say that it is forbidden,
and whoever cleaves to it is as if he cleaved to idolatry [b. Ketubot 68a] ⚠8.
And whoever loves it is counted in the eyes of men a madman and a lunatic,
stricken with the plague of folly."

The young man said: "Do you think that because of its baseness
the sons of men reject it,
and keep far from its company?
It is not so; only because of its greatness
they are too weary to reach it,
and their souls are too small to climb to its height.
I will give you proof of my words: a precious thing is not found in the hand of every man, but only in the hands of the few
who are honored;
like precious stones, which are not found in the hand of every man, but only in the hands of princes and rulers,
and kings and men of eminence;
and like wisdom,
which is not found in beast and cattle,
nor in all the peoples of the earth,
but only in the lofty men of understanding,
in whom is the spirit of God [Gen 41:38];
and like holy piety,
which was not given to everyone born of woman [Job 14:1],
but only to the holy upright in heart,
who draw near and sanctify themselves [Exod 19:22] to serve God;
and the other good virtues, such as humility and faithfulness,
and every worthy quality,
which are not found among the empty and the foolish,
nor in the despised and the lowly,
but only in him who has a pure soul
and a precious and clear spirit.
So too generosity is not found in the hands of worthless men,
who have denied God above [Job 31:28],
and the knees that bowed to Baal [1 Kgs 19:18],
but only with men of uprightness and right,
one from a city and two from a family [Jer 3:14].
Those who know its worth
walk in its way,
and those who recognize its height
take pride in its company.
For it was not created for hypocritical churls, but for the holy ones of the Most High [Dan 7:18],
who have built in their hearts a palanquin [Song 3:9] for the fear of God."
And he took up his parable and said:

If generosity could speak and make answer, / it would reveal its secret to men of answers:
So sell all but it, and then you will be a head, / and with all the treasure of your wealth buy it [Prov 4:7].

Said the teller: And when they had multiplied words,
to set in array a battle of sayings,
all the people knew that the young man's words were strong as a tower built with turrets [Song 4:4],
and that the old man's arguments were thrust aside.
They said to the old man: "Though you are forceful in your words and your parables,
has not the young man prevailed over you?"
The old man said: "You have thought falsely,
and daubed with whitewash [Ezek 13:10],
and trusted in vanity.
For with my tongue I could have broken the jaws of his arguments [Job 29:17],
and torn down the towers of his replies;
only it is not right to set churlishness above pleasant generosity,
lest the churls say, 'Our hand is high' [Deut 32:27],
and they have an opening of the mouth [Ezek 16:63] against the generous,
and no memory remain of good deeds."
They said to him: "You have done well in your wisdom and your judgment;
and now, tell us your name."
And he took up his parable and said:

In Elon-bezaanannim [Judg 4:11] is my dwelling ⚠9, / and there my land and my city are known.
I am Ḥever, and my deeds are like my name, for / I join together the scattered teachings of morals in my song ⚠10.
