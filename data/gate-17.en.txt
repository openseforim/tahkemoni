Thus says Heman the Ezrahite: In the days of my youth I used to knock at the doors of the wise and the learned,
and watch at the gates [Prov 8:34] of the discerning and the judges,
to gather the crystals ⚠1 of their sayings,
and draw from the springs of their parables,
and drink my fill of the honeycomb [Prov 16:24] from the forests of their words.
And it came to pass one day, as I sat with them in an assembly of the wise,
listening to their sayings,
that Ḥever the Kenite was at the head of the guests [1 Sam 9:22].
The people who stood before him,
and drew from his springs,
asked him about the Cutheans ⚠2,
who are cut off,
and about their evil faith,
which is worthless.

He said to them: Know that the Cutheans among living creatures
are like a creeping thing among the beasts.
They are not counted with the Jews,
nor reckoned with the uncircumcised,
nor called by the name of the Ishmaelites.
They are a creature by themselves,
whose lack and blemish are known to all the nations.
And what will you ask about a forsaken congregation [Isa 49:21] and an accursed people?
They are like a bough without a thicket,
and a tree without a root.
Their prophet is Sanballat the Horonite,
and their prince Tobiah the slave, the Ammonite [Neh 2:10].
Besides this, they hold on to our Torah,
and make it their pride over us,
like a slave who prides himself over his master with a brazen face,
and twist its meaning [m. Avot 3:11] ⚠3. They are suspected of stealing our Torah, yet they openly display their righteousness,
while the theft is in their bosom.
They hold on to our Torah,
and do the opposite of our practice.
All of them are like drunkards, bewildered,
walking in darkness.
Their Torah is worthless,
and their faith is evil.
Their congregation is small, and does not reach a thousand men,
for they are lacking and worthless,
stunted and not overgrown [Lev 22:23] ⚠4,
few and evil [Gen 47:9].

They said to him: What do you say about the Karaites, the heretics,
who do not believe in the words of our rabbis?
He said to them: They are of our kinsmen [Ruth 2:20],
and nearer to us,
only they do not believe in the words of our judges.
And although our Torah is in their hands,
they are not counted with our congregation,
for they made themselves slaves to their desire with rigor [Exod 1:13],
and turned aside quickly from the way [Exod 32:8].

They said to him: But how can the truth be hidden from them,
when anyone who has reason can tell the living from the dead?
Do they not know that the Torah
is like a closed castle,
and in its chambers is every precious stone,
and that the tongue of the sages is like a key to enter its chambers,
and to gather its crystals and its pearls?
And if the key is lost,
the door will stay shut and not be opened.

He said to them: They recognize all this and understand it,
but they will not listen and will not believe,
for their fathers wanted to make themselves a new Torah,
and they profaned the holy Law.
So the truth was missing [Isa 59:15] among them,
and their faith is sick and broken.
They said to him: What is their argument
for their faith?
He said to them: They have contemptible arguments,
built on foundations of vanity,
and all of them refuted.
They said to him: Will you not tell us some of their matters,
and let us hear their arguments?
He said to them: Know that from days of old two kinds ⚠5 have gathered among those who handle the Torah [Jer 2:8]:
some deny the sages of Israel, and some believe.
These hold to the words of our rabbis, such as Ravina and Rav Ashi;
and those do not believe in them, but believe in the LORD and in Moses [Exod 14:31].
For they take the verse naked, stripped of its garments of ornament,
and strip off from it its coat [Gen 37:23] of beauty,
like one who keeps the tree and throws away its fruit.
And the believers answered,
and said to the heretics: One must wonder at a man who has knowledge,
whose soul knows evil and good,
and yet does not submit to the truth.
For how can a man believe in the Torah of Moses and its commandments,
if he does not believe in the explanations of its laws?
Were it not for the Talmud,
the Torah would be like a tent without a pillar.
Were it not for the Mishnah,
the scent of the testimony would have changed [Jer 48:11] and altered.
Were it not for the Gemara,
its students would have journeyed from its sweetness and encamped at Marah [Num 33:8].
Were it not for the commentaries,
doubts and errors would have multiplied in the Torah.
Were it not for the sages, how would a man know from the words of the Torah
how to guard himself from transgression,
or take care with a commandment, light or grave?
The heretics said: Is it because there is no God in Israel [2 Kgs 1:3]
that we should seek from your sages a helper and redeemer?
Far be it from us to profane, for the sake of your sages, the Torah of the Rock who created us,
and his Law that he gave us as an inheritance.
Rather we keep his word and his utterance,
as we received them from Moses his prophet,
and we will not change it nor exchange it,
nor add to it nor take away from it [Deut 4:2].
But your sages
changed the Torah by the explanation they explained to you,
for they went out from the plain sense to the homily,
and from the homily they sank in the troubled sea [Isa 57:20],
and explained the verse in a way it was never meant.
The believers said to them: You are idle, you are idle [Exod 5:17]!
How long will you hop between two opinions [1 Kgs 18:21],
after the capital has been struck and the thresholds shake [Amos 9:1]?
And how long will he entice your heart with his lies ⚠6?

If the LORD is God, follow him;
and if Baal, follow him [1 Kgs 18:21].
Now let two men come out, one of you and one of us,
and let them argue between us both [Gen 31:37],
and then the truth will be known among us.
And all the people said: The thing that you have spoken is good [1 Kgs 18:24],
and well have you decided.
So two men came out, one a heretic,
and one a believer.

And the two of them strove together [Exod 2:13],
and the two ranks listened to their words.
And the believer answered and said:

Every man who believes in the Law of Moses alone, / and in his anger despises the word of the sages,
is like a man who says: I will do the word / of my king, and transgress the word of his second ⚠7.

The heretic said:

I believe in the Law of Moses alone, / and do not call false the word of the seers and watchmen.
But I will not listen to your sages, because / they change the word of God.
I lean on the Law of God, which lifts up / the one who holds it fast, while those who forsake it are bowed down,
like a man who leans on a tree, mighty and strong, / and pays no heed to the branches.

The believer said:

Ask the men of the times of old: / is there any wisdom that has no interpreters?
Were not the books of medicine sealed, until / the wise craftsmen [Isa 3:3] explained them by their intellect?
And had they left them sealed, / men would die of the remedies.
So too the Law was sealed of old, / and holy fathers explained it,
because within it are general rules, / and under them the details of the commandments, set in array ⚠8.
Their secrets, in the explanation of the sages, / are made plain, sought out for all who delight in them [Ps 111:2].
And were they done according to their plain sense, / we would be sinners, and separated from the Law.
Without the sages we would be in it / like the blind, abandoned without a guide.

The heretic said:

The word of Moses is clear and open, / and his commandments are known to every man of sense.
He who says they are sealed lays bare / his folly, and becomes nothing but a horror.
There is no need for the explanation of the sages, / for they are like nails planted [Eccl 12:11] in the heart.
Therefore the sages explained in vain, / and their souls weary themselves with words of vanity.

The believer said:

My son, if your soul leans on the Law, / and it is kept in order [2 Sam 23:5] within your heart,
take a verse and come out against me like a warrior, / until we set the battle in array.
I will ask you about its rulings and its statutes, / its judgments and its drawn sword ⚠9.
And if you can reveal its hidden things / without Mishnah and Talmud and halakha,
I will come over to your argument and admit it, / and say that the desire of kingship is yours.

The heretic said:

While I live I hold fast to the Law of God, / and at my death I will leave it to my sons as an inheritance.
I will not turn to the words of the sages, / nor look at the explanation of the discerning,
but I will do what my fathers did, / who dwelt in Zion of old,
who lived before the sages, / when there were no Geonim in their generations.
They did not sin nor rebel against the word of God, / and their deeds were right before God.

The believer said:

Your fathers, who lived of old, / kept the word of God, fathers and sons.
And if no Geonim were found among them, / were there not Levites and priests there?
And the prophets were then among the people of God, / to teach the right judgments of God.
Now these and those are lost, / and the Rock has raised up the Geonim in their place.

The heretic said:

If the sages did not change the Law / of God, I would act by their command.
But they changed the matters of the Torah; / therefore I depart from their paths.
The Law said: You shall kindle no fire on the day / of the Sabbath [Exod 35:3]; but they pursued their own reasonings,
and kindled the fire to light a lamp, / and walked through darkness by their lamps [Job 29:3] ⚠10.
Therefore I do not sin against God / whenever I depart from their faith.

The believer said:

He who has a clear and shining mind / sees the word of righteousness with the eye of the heart.
And when the sages find one saying that cancels / another, they understand how it is to be answered ⚠11.
Were they to sit in the dark on the Sabbath eve, / it would be a sin, and God would require it.
We did not light the lamp except / on the sixth day, when its work was finished,
like a man who did a work on a weekday and rested / when the Sabbath came, and his work remained.
And did not the fire of the sacrifice burn on the Sabbath? / If one asks you about this, what will you answer him?
So turn back, open the eyes of your heart, / and then you will see the light of what is right.

The heretic said:

Truly, all things are full of weariness [Eccl 1:8], / and God's statute is full of reason and knowledge.
The words of God are perfect / for every man of sense, and lacking for the fool.
The sayings of the Geonim are the fruit of understanding, / and its roots are planted in the heart of the man of sense.
The Torah gives light like the lampstand, / and the sages are its cups [Exod 25:31].
And I know that whoever changes their word, / his schemes go backward and astray.
But how can I change the custom of my fathers, / when they understood the Law and knew it?
If I forsake their faith and listen / to your speech, I make them wicked.

The believer said:

Your fathers, who went astray at the first: / will you too go astray like them on the highway?
Keep far from them and do not come near them; / clear your road of stones, and turn back again ⚠12.
Set your heart to the statutes of God: / were they written for you lightly?
Consider the commandment of God, such as the sukkah, / which he gave as an inheritance to the people he made his treasure.
Without the sages, how would you understand it, / and know whether it is fit or unfit,
how long its length and its breadth must be, / its height, its sunlight and its shade [m. Sukkah 1:1],
whether it may be deep or leaning, / whether it may be square or round ⚠13?
And how would you know the carryings out on the Sabbath [m. Shabbat 1:1], / and its eruv, and the measure of the way ⚠14?
But the sages made all this plain, / the Lesser Sanhedrin with the Great.
So turn back to the way of light and prosper, / and save your soul from the pit of darkness.

The teller ⚠15 said: And when the words grew many,
the truth was known from the lies,
and the darkness was divided from the lights.
And the Karaites knew that the words of our Geonim are an everlasting light,
and that they are like the tree of life, and whoever tastes their fruit and eats lives for ever [Gen 3:22].
And they hurried to come out of their snare,
to save their souls from death.
Each man forsook the custom of his father,
who says of his father and his mother, I have not seen him [Deut 33:9].
They ate of the fruit of the tree of knowledge to make their thoughts wise,
and their eyes were opened [Gen 3:7], and they brought their necks under the yoke of truth [Jer 27:11].
And their elders made
peace among them.
