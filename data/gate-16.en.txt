Thus says Heman the Ezrahite: Before the clods of wandering had been made to cleave to the company of friends,
and the dust of companionship had been cast into the mold of parting [Job 38:38],
and before gladness had melted in the furnace of stillness ⚠1,
and before the daughters of hope had stripped off the rings of companionship [Exod 32:3],
and before their palms were torn from the rivers of ease ⚠2,
and before they had been emptied from vessel to vessel [Jer 48:11],
I was settled on my lees [Jer 48:11],
gathering lilies of joy in gardens of delight [Song 6:2].
Until the thwartings that thwart had thwarted me,
and the troubles in their fullness had worn me out,
and scattered me to every corner [Deut 32:26]:
the calamities that scatter.
They woke me from the sleep of my rest,
and oppressed me and schooled me,
and forced me to make my bed
my journey,
and my rest
my wandering and roving [Gen 4:12].
In the darkness of parting I spread my couch [Job 17:13].
The dwellings of joy became
spewing me out [Lev 18:28]
from patched high places [Ezek 16:16]
to a land of tumults,
a lair of kites and pelicans.
The pupils of my eyes grew dark on high,
and from the deep of the heart
flowing clouds,
that never fail [Isa 58:11],
clouded the radiance of my light [Lam 2:1],
and made the fullness of my gladness pine away [Lev 26:16];
my cheeks were scorched by their sparks,
as the pieces of the hearts melted,
the frozen ones, to pour down their pearls
upon their faces.
The carvers of the eyes ⚠3,
and the daughters of thought that draw
waters of tears
from the rocks of the ribs,
turned the Negev of the cheek into streams [Ps 126:4];
they shot at the vanguard of my peace, whose shields are like channels [Job 41:7] ⚠4,
between the joints and the coat of mail [1 Kgs 22:34].
At the flood of their streams a torrent broke out away from where men dwell [Job 28:4],
running over its sides and over its banks [Josh 3:15];
and the dwelling of love was poured down into the valley [Mic 1:6].
The troop of burden and breaking became
a breaking before hope ⚠5.

The strength of gladness was swallowed up,
and the host of the afflicted army was clad in crimson [Nah 2:4],
with the fire of steel [Nah 2:4], making my joy wander,
and hunting the gladness of my thoughts;
and they brought it into the strongholds,
to shoot at it five times over [Gen 43:34] ⚠6.
Drought and heat robbed my eyelids of their dripping streams [Job 24:19],
and put steel in the place of dripping ⚠7.
Many who lay in wait shot at me and poured me out,
and into the hands of the wicked they flung me and delivered me [Job 16:11];
the terrors of dread were arrayed against me [Job 6:4],
and the bellows of sighs was scorched from melting me [Jer 6:29].
And when the sails of troubles were rolled away from me,
those that stood by the mirrors of the women who serve [Exod 38:8] ⚠8,
and the tumults made ready vessels of exile [Jer 46:19],
those that had thickened on their lees [Zeph 1:12],
the eyes of tranquility were opened,
as the clouds of calamity went from them;
and Time hid the weapons of his wrath [Jer 50:25],
and stilled his thunder,
and made his taste sweet.
One day I was sitting with a treasured band of eloquent men,
breathing the scent of the powders of a company of vigorous sages [Song 3:6],
and drawing the abundant radiance of revered men of understanding.
By the hand of their intellect they raised up the paths of nobility [Isa 62:10],
and with their learning they took hearts and eyes and drew them out ⚠9;
at the lights of their sayings the heavens of thought were rolled open like a scroll [Isa 34:4];
at the sound of their roaring the teeth of the young lions of speech were broken [Job 4:10],
and teachers of knowledge strayed on the isles of error;
at their word the jewels that are joined and the riddles that are loose drew back,
and the gold plates of the full words were hammered out [Exod 39:3] in the chains of their intellect.
All of them lift up hands of faith and understanding,
and shut the doors of folly on their sockets,
and let their eyes fly over the matters of the heart's thoughts [Prov 23:5],
and through the windows of the turns of the words of knowledge they look out and look around.
Among them was a flash of lightning [Ezek 1:14],
the revered, the strong,
refined gold,
in whose heart every statute is engraved.
He lays out headband and necklace with his pearls,
and fells the giant, the son of Anak, with his thorns ⚠10.
He embroiders robes out of eloquence,
and the grace of vigorous words, inlaid,
set in the gold of delight [Exod 28:20].
Lamp of those who stray,
crown of the nobles,
lord of friends,
mightiest of the shepherds [1 Sam 21:8];
one alone in eloquence, and there is no second [Eccl 4:8];
he decrees with understanding, and none can thwart.
He is Ḥever the Kenite,
our friend, one alone in eloquence;
for fear of him their foundations tremble [Job 9:6],
and burst apart and crush one another.
He removes the mountains [Job 9:5] of the hills of knowledge with the winds of the speech of his reasoning,
and with the storms of the rebuke of his thought;
he binds the bands [Job 38:31] of delicate understandings
in the utterance that goes out of his mouth [Job 37:2] ⚠11; he makes rivers and springs well up,
and binds up treasures from the weeping of the rivers [Job 28:11].
When he opens his mouth, all the men of the company
and the men of counsel and might [Isa 11:2]
are like a row bound together,
standing around him,
to gather his showers and his sparks,
to learn his words and his paths,
to pick up the stragglers of his sayings [Deut 25:18],
to glean the paths of right things from his forgotten sheaves [Deut 24:19],
to breathe in the perfumes of pure speech from the breath of his lips [Isa 11:4].
They draw out a polished lightning [Ezek 21:15] from the sharpness of his sayings,
and the wings of their hearts flutter at the sound of the neighing of his stallions [Jer 8:16] and their galloping,
and they tie on their necks the bands of the gold pieces [Ps 68:31] of his words,
of the best of pure sayings [Ps 12:7], banded,
cast in the plain of the furnace of intellect,
in the clay [1 Kgs 7:46] of the plasterings, plastered and molded.
From his mouth roll up the billows of the smoke [Isa 9:17] of thoughts,
and the blowing storms of counsel cling together,
the waters of their skies pour, and the flood of their rains from the clouds.
The deeps of reasoning boil like a pot [Job 41:23] at their scorn ⚠12,
and the circle of the upper sphere shakes [Job 26:11] at their stamping.
All of them are young lions of the soul,
that put lions to shame,
armed and drawn up for the battles of thought.
All of them press toward the thick darkness [Exod 20:21] of the flame of his teaching,
and the pillars of smoke [Song 3:6] of his perfume;
they sanctify themselves to take fire from the hearth [Isa 30:14] of his speech,
which is on his altar.
They soar to the height of the supreme thoughts
on wings of song [Job 39:13] ⚠13,
and with the pinions of the yellow gold [Ps 68:14] of meanings,
to be alone and to meet in the dwelling of the souls
with beings
whose hearts are fires,
and whose rims are of beryl [Ezek 1:16, 18],
and to join, in the vision of prophetic thought,
with the sheaves of the company ⚠14,
wrapped in flame,
in the circle of the vault,
from where the radiance of delight shines, and not through a window,
and the fire of learning is kindled, never to be quenched.
From the clay of thoughts they shaped [Job 33:6] the form of the wisdoms,
in forms most fair [Jer 46:20],
built for turrets [Song 4:4],
looking out through the windows of inquiry;
in the midst of the battle of knowledge their arms are stretched out,
and the hand of their strength seizes and holds ⚠15.
Each of them called up the seers of eloquence from the chambers of his bed,
and loaded his knowledge like moisture on the cloud [Job 37:11],
and began to hang their words as bells [Zech 14:20] on the necks of the wisdoms,
and for its chambers and halls, supports and highways.
Among them were seven desirable young men [Ezek 23:6],
the choicest of the gathering and the pairing,
who still the depths [Job 38:16] of the sea of poetry and its breakers [Ps 89:10],
and gaze through the windows [Song 2:9] of its secret places.
Each prides himself over the other on his song, and is amazed at it,
and on wings of song soars out to the open spaces of eloquence;
each man calls to his brother: "Come, let us look one another in the face [2 Kgs 14:8]."
Each of them boasts that he goes up first
on the stairs [2 Kgs 9:13] of the steps of praise.
So they gathered together before their mighty one,
their champion and their teacher,
Ḥever the Kenite, to judge between them
on their words and their parables,
to know whose poem was the choice among them,
and to decide between them.
The first came forward and said:

The source of poetry is in my heart: its outgoings [Prov 4:23]; / but my thoughts are its springs.
Is not my intellect like a sphere that turns / over the world and its four corners [Exod 25:12]?
I have stored the hail of my words in the treasuries [Job 38:22] of understanding, / and the fire of my heart melts its frozen layers.
The vanguard of my knowledge turned back the horses of battle / with the thunder of its roars.
It threshes the ancient mountains and crushes them [Isa 41:15], / and the lion's heart melts [2 Sam 17:10] at its galloping.
And the tabernacle of understanding, which God's hand spread out, / has its loops among the clasps [Exod 26:6] of my thought.
I have a tongue: when its lightnings sleep, / its flames still dim the eye of the luminaries.
It roams the sea and the eastern side, as if / its chariots were on the wings of the storm.

The second answered and said:

In my palate the lightning of a tongue darts [Nah 2:5]; / like a hammer it shatters [Jer 23:29] the heart of the terrible.
With it I hammer out threads of eloquence [Exod 39:3], / and from them I cut chains of song.
I sink the tongue of men with its hooks [Job 40:25], / and crush the head of the twisting serpent [Isa 27:1].
I bring back what it swallowed out of its throat [Jer 51:44], / until it vomits up all it sucked and drained.
And if the days grow dark like nights, / does not my song shine in them like the sun?
And when it rouses a troop of words of battle, / armed and arrayed, it goes out softly in ranks [Prov 30:27].
Hill and mountain will not save those it pursues, / nor will Seneh and Bozez rise up on the crag [1 Sam 14:4].
The tree of its speech bears new fruit each month [Ezek 47:12]; / without it, all is a bare sill ⚠16 when it should blossom and bud [Num 17:23].

The third answered and said:

My thoughts roar in the battle of testimonies, / and the rock of my tongue kindles the fire of steel.
I have a thought that cleaves strongholds of might / with the edge of its sword [Ps 89:44], and conquers the fortresses.
And when the lightning of my sword spreads its light, / the lions of battle wrap themselves in terrors [Ezek 26:16].
The daughters of my knowledge, on the ladder of my thoughts, / go up and go down [Gen 28:12] to the sphere of intellect and understanding.
They are like the living creatures, their backs bound / by the hand of intellect, and their wings spread apart [Ezek 1:11].
And Wisdom, like the Queen of Sheba, / comes to me to test me with riddles [1 Kgs 10:1].
Within me she has built palaces and castles, / and set foundations in the midst of my heart,
to join the eloquence that is scattered, / and to part the jewels that are joined.

The fourth answered and said:

At the mention of me the quiet of heart tremble, / and the innards raise up the fire of battles.
Like stars they scatter fiery sparks of steel, / hewn [Ps 29:7] from mountains of flame.
And if the moths met the daughters of my song, / these would leave them there scorched in the flame of their fire.
The rock of my speech, with the blood of those who break my covenant, / drenches the swords, the wasted places ⚠17.
Men become like women before me, / and the males turn into females.
The lions become goats before me, / and the heart of the wolves of the steppe [Hab 1:8] melts,
with words that make the hearts of foolish men pine, / but restore the souls [Ps 19:8] of men of intellect.
The speech of my tongue binds the bands [Job 38:31] / of understandings that filled the world with fruit [Isa 27:6].

The fifth answered and said:

With the breath of my mouth I split the river of poetry, / I muddy the streams of its waters [Ezek 32:2] and stamp.
I sew the festive robes of the garment [Gen 49:11] of eloquence / with a thread from the chains of my hammered speech.
If my songs were mentioned over the sea, / it would dry up, and sink down upon the fire [Num 11:2] ⚠18.
A song not mine is like a patched garment, / and its sayings like a wineskin tied up and split [Josh 9:4–5].
Poets desire it, and their souls / loathe the song of the children of Time and turn from it [Ezek 23:18].
The radiance of its words is like a banner on a bare hill [Isa 13:2], / and it has spread and pitched its tent like a lofty dwelling.
It has struck off the heads of the warriors of battle with its sword, / and set them up before the sun and hanged them [Num 25:4].
And it set loose the eloquence that was bound, / when it sounded abroad and blew the shofar of liberty [Lev 25:9–10].

The sixth answered and said:

Over the songs of Time it waves its hand [Isa 11:15], / and the sphere shakes at its stamping.
Like a river the waters of its streams have risen; / it divides, and reaches to the neck [Isa 30:28], and surrounds.
Without feet it measures the depths, / and to the skies it flies without wings.
It gladdens every hard and angry heart, / until he goes along tripping [Isa 3:16].
And if the moon saw its light, / it would bend its head low at its feet.
And when a man strikes it on the lyre, / it gladdens the poor man who beats in bitterness upon his heart [Nah 2:8].
To lovers it is balm, but the heart of haters / it bites in anger, like a flying serpent [Isa 30:6].
Glory covers it [Deut 33:12], but it / covers understanding and intellect.

The seventh answered and said:

The tongue of song declares my deeds, / and the mouth of the pen recounts my wonders.
My heart circles over the cylinder of the sky: / marvel how my thoughts bear me up!
I am a tabernacle, and the wisdoms are its clasps, / and the hand of intellect binds my loops.
I am a fortress and a strong tower [Ps 61:4] for every man of understanding, / and the lovely things throng to serve me [Exod 38:8].
I am fine gold, not found by every man, / but the souls of the wise find me.
My border is on the base of the mount of understanding, / and my outgoings are at the sea [Num 34:5] of eloquence.
Like an arrow I pierce [Prov 7:23] the heart of the senseless; / for this the souls of fools hate me.
I have songs that fly without wings, / and tell my awesome deeds without a mouth.

Said the narrator: When they had emptied their vessels,
and poured out their words,
Ḥever the Kenite said to them: "Know that the poetry of all of you is equal,
and your speech lovely and comely;
it would light up the darkness if it took the veil [Exod 34:33] from its face.
It is as if all of you had sucked the milk of one wisdom,
and leapt together from the mountains of prey [Ps 76:5].
The first of you curbs the trappings [Ps 32:9] of the poets ⚠19;
the second wraps himself in the mantle of glory;
the third strikes down the perfume of empty men;
the fourth turns diamonds into chalkstones [Isa 27:9];
the fifth terrifies hearts awake and in dream;
the sixth they have anointed king over the world ⚠20;
and to the seventh poetry has given its covenant of peace [Num 25:12]."

Said the narrator: My heart trembled for them,
and I did not know at which thing among them to marvel:
at the wonders of their poems,
or at the eloquence of Ḥever the Kenite, their teacher.
And I parted from them,
and my heart stayed with them.
