Thus says Heman the Ezrahite: I was in a gathering of friends,
lovely ones,
held fast in the hand of beauty,
and they became one [Ezek 37:17].
All of them were nobles and princes;
from their tongues they scattered princely words,
and their eloquence was like sparks of burning coals,
burning like the appearance of torches [Ezek 1:13].
And the time was the month of Aviv [Exod 13:4],
that is, the month of Nisan, the beloved and the dear.
From the tears of the dew there were showers on the cheek of the lily,
and the garden had a binding of embroidered lilies round about its opening [Exod 28:32].
The earth had put on her embroidered garments of blossoms,
and decked herself with her ring and her jewels [Hos 2:15].
The lily, like a lamp, shone before her eyes,

and gave light over against her face [Exod 25:37],
and upon her gardens,
her flowers and her buds,
and upon her meadows, her joys;
and greenery greened in her face [Num 12:14] ⚠1.
The spices of the garden were drawn to our noses;
wherever the wind would go, they went [Ezek 1:12].
The sun increased her light and was revealed,
and encamped in the sign of the Ram.
The plants came out from the depths of the earth,
and broke through a breach,
and appointed themselves one head, and went up out of the land [Hos 2:2],
and covered the face of the world with spreads of delight.
The cloud commanded the messengers of the thunder to bring good tidings to the earth;
she sent and called Barak the son of Abinoam [Judg 4:6] ⚠2,
and said to him: "Draw your sword and your quiver upon the earth;
arise, Barak, and lead your captives captive [Judg 5:12],
and go, bring the earth tidings that I have set my host in array against her."
And Barak said to her: "If you will go with me, I will go [Judg 4:8]."
She said to him: "I will not let my hand go from you;
I will surely go with you [Judg 4:9]."
The thunders lifted up their voice, and the earth answered,
and the troop of the rain encamped for her battle,
and Barak pursued after the chariots and after the host [Judg 4:16].
The garden put on her garments of beauty,
and the lily, for joy at the layer of dew [Exod 16:13] that had revived her,
weeps sore in the night, and her tear is on her cheek [Lam 1:2].
The garden looked out like a maiden from the windows of her palaces,
and put her veil off from upon her [Gen 38:19];
her robes were of chequered gold [Ps 45:14],

the perfumes of myrrh were on her hems,
and the tongues of the myrtle were her talebearers,
for with every mouth they went about telling of her.
And the wine revived the bones [Isa 58:11]
after death [Lev 16:1],
and gladdened the souls that were desolate ⚠3.
And with us was the head of the sages and the geonim,
who takes away the speech of the trusted [Job 12:20],
and with his mind takes away the discernment of the elders [Job 12:20],
of the seed of the Kenites:
he was our friend, Ḥever the Kenite,
who strings rows of sapphires
on the necks of books,
and draws out pearls
from the deeps of thought.

He answered and said to them: "You are like a tree of life, and how good is your shade!
At the throne of glory you are standing this day, all of you [Deut 29:9].
The days shine by the light of your faces,
and gleam like the brightness of the firmament [Dan 12:3].
The times have made their faces shine today,
and their eyes are opened [Gen 3:7].
Now let men of valor be armed from among you [Num 31:3],
like the stars of the night,
masters of mind and understanding,
as many as the months of the year,
and let each of them praise his month,
and set a crown of praises on its head.
Let the first begin by praising the glorious month of Nisan,
and let the last complete it with the month of Adar.
By this the power of your poetry will be known,
and your words will be proved,
whether there is truth in you [Gen 42:16]."
All the people said to him: "You have begun to show your servant your deep wisdom
and your strong hand [Deut 3:24].
Now begin you with the eloquence of your sayings,
and we will come after you, and fill out your words."
And Ḥever the Kenite came near and said:

"The month of Nisan is the head of heads,
and king over the months;
for in it the time is hallowed and the world made new,
and there arose a new king [Exod 1:8].
In it the swallows chirp,
and the dewdrops drip [Song 5:2],
and the myrtles blossom.
Time's dawns gleam,
and its lights shine,
and it renews its youth like the eagle [Ps 103:5].
It puts off from itself the black sackcloth,
and takes off its garments and puts on other garments [Lev 6:4],
and bathes in pure water.
After its old age and length of days,
it returns to the days of its youth [Job 33:25].
Then the gardens blossom,
and the nights shine,
and the sorrows flee,
and the joys spring up,
and the lilies rejoice.
The earth brings forth her growth [Isa 61:11],
and uncovers her flower,
and yields her strength again [Gen 4:12].
And after the earth has been wedded to autumn,
and joined as a bride on the bed of love,
she divorces her husband, who is old and stale [Lev 26:10],
and longs for the month of Nisan,
who is the delight of every eye,
and says: 'I will go and return to my first husband [Hos 2:9].'
And after she has been divorced from the season of cold that was with her,
her first husband, who sent her away, may return to take her [Deut 24:4] ⚠4.
It is the month of wonders
and of awesome miracles,
and the head of the months of the festivals;
for in it the beloved were redeemed
from the house of bondage,
and the captives went out
from the hands of the Ludim ⚠5,
and their enemies pursued after them in troops,
and the sea was split for them by an east wind [Exod 14:21],
and the Egyptians sank in the depths,
and the redeemed gave praise with a new song.
Pharaoh drowned in the Red Sea [Exod 15:4] and fell from heaven
to the depths of the waters,
and was washed of his filth without hands,
and scoured and rinsed in water [Lev 6:21],
and the entrails and the legs he shall wash in water [Lev 1:9] ⚠6.
And the seed of Jeshurun, upon the high places of the earth [Deut 32:13],
rode and went on,
for out of prison he came forth to reign [Eccl 4:14].
Then the sun draws near,
and the wine is sweet,
and the daughter of the flagon,
in her beauty a girl, and in her length of days aged,
I took her to me for a wife.
All the night she lies at my side,
and she has become my nurse [1 Kgs 1:4].
When the cup goes up in my hand,
she sends her spices to my nose,
and kisses my mouth;
and when she goes back empty from before me,
she says in my ears:
'I went out full, and my lord has brought me back empty [Ruth 1:21] ⚠7.'"
And he took up his parable and said:

Sit among the garden beds in the days of Nisan, / when the bud comes up, and its banner is raised high ⚠8,
and exult at the voice of the songbird on the treetop, / and rejoice too at the splendor of the blossom and the palm branch.
The joy of souls is in flagons [Song 2:5], for / from Time is given the portion of their cup [Ps 11:6].

One of the companions, who was second to Ḥever the Kenite, answered and said:
"The month of Iyyar is the delight of the months,
for in it is healing for men ⚠9
and strength for the weak,
which cheers God and men [Judg 9:13].
If the month of Nisan is king over the months,
is not the month of Iyyar second to the king [Esth 10:3]?
Therefore it sits and encamps at his side,
and he made it ride in the second chariot [Gen 41:43].
Then the garden blossoms,
and the day dawns,
and the heat spreads out,
and the cold flees,
and before the troops of the heat it cries out bitterly [Zeph 1:14],
and the army of the heat pursues it
until it strikes it down.
Then the hands of the rain open the hidden stores of the soil
and the treasuries of the deeps,
to bring out from beneath the earth embroidered garments,
and to cover the face of the ground with pleasant chequer-work,
hollow streaks, greenish or reddish [Lev 14:37] ⚠10,
to clothe the garden beds
in woven tunics of silk.
And after the earth has worn the garment of her widowhood,
mourning in her great affliction
for the death of the autumn season, her husband,
the season of heat takes her to wife and joins himself to her:
her husband's brother shall go in to her [Deut 25:5].
Then she puts off sackcloth in her great rejoicing,
and puts away the garments of her widowhood from upon her [Gen 38:14],
and sets her crown on her head and her earrings in her ears,
and her cords upon her arms.
And the wine calls to its drinkers: 'Prepare my booth in the garden,
and hurry to drink me, for you will find honey in my palate;
and do not hinder me, since the Lord has prospered my way [Gen 24:56].'
And he took up his parable and said:

See the garden, painted with rows of blossom, / and run to drink by the light of its beauty in Iyyar.
And look: Time has written embroideries / upon the blossom, without parchment or paper.
Then the troop of joy prevails, and taken / is the troop of grief, and it will not be set free.

The third answered and said:

"The month of Sivan is the head of the thirds ⚠11,
and the delight of the months; in it the face of the world shines,
and all that was hidden is revealed,
and the heavens give their dew [Zech 8:12],
and the buds of the garden yield their strength.
The day breathes [Song 2:17],
and carries its perfumes to our noses on the wings of the wind [Ps 18:11].
The apple is anointed with oil of myrrh,
and the lily is as if compounded in Aaron's censer,
or taken from the garden of God [Ezek 28:13].
The fruits are sweet to every mouth,
and are wet with the shower of the mountains [Job 24:8].
Time comes to meet us with its delicacies and its splendor,
with all the goods of its master in its hand [Gen 24:10].
In it is the choicest of the festivals of the Lord,
and the day which the Lord chose,
when He came down upon Mount Sinai [Exod 19:20],
and gave His law as an inheritance
to those who learn His testimony.
When they heard the majesty of His voice, their souls went out [b. Shabbat 88b],
and He brought down a dew of lights [Isa 26:19] and revived their bones,
to deliver their soul from death and to keep them alive [Ps 33:19].
Therefore in this month every heart rejoices and exults,
when the garden bed, like a bride, puts on a rich robe [Isa 3:24],
with ring and earring upon her.
Every fruit tree blossoms,
and the vines in blossom give their fragrance [Song 2:13],
and Time overflows,
and the heart is glad,
and the wine is perfumed with the chief spices [Song 4:14], the work of the perfumer [Exod 30:25].
And the cup calls, saying: 'If you wish to quench the fire in your sides,
and to heal your wounds,
set me as a seal upon your heart,
as a seal upon your arm [Song 8:6].'"
And he took up his parable and said:

From the faces of the cups, covered with pearl, / take off the veil slowly when Sivan comes ⚠12.

For at the sight of the cup souls / come to life, since in it is the balm for strife ⚠13.
If heat and thirst should burn them, / the cup's tongue ever sets them singing with its honey.

The fourth answered and said:
"The month of Tammuz is lovely, for in it grief is held in check and gladness clings close; grace stands over it, and its beauty cannot be measured;
the time when a man leaves his spacious house [Jer 22:14], and goes out from houses and courtyards to the fields,
to set his dwelling within the garden and his table in the garden bed, to sing over his basin in his garden, and gladden his thoughts with his wine.
On every branch is every winged thing,
and the voice of the turtledove [Song 2:12] over flower and bud [Exod 25:33],
and on every treetop, the voice of the songbird,
and over the blossom, the crane and the swift [Jer 8:7].
And though it is the month of the mishap that sets the heart ablaze and stirs up grief, for in it the tablets were broken and the city was breached [m. Taanit 4:6],
and in this month all the congregations have their crown shorn [Jer 7:29] and their glory plundered, and sit weeping for the Tammuz [Ezek 8:14] ⚠14,
yet with the cup a man forgets his sorrow, and puts away his grief and his trouble.
Whoever the serpent of grief has bitten need only look at his cup, and everyone who is bitten, when he sees it, shall live [Num 21:8]."
And he took up his parable and said:

Your portion, held fast from the good things of Time, / is to drink in a garden locked up [Song 4:12] in the days of Tammuz.
Spend all your wealth upon your wine, / before it becomes a plunder in the hands of events.
And rejoice while you are alive upon the dust, / before you are stored away beneath it.

The fifth answered and said:
"The month of Av is healing for all who are in pain, and joy for the heart that languishes; for its days are long ⚠15, its goodness laid out, and its fruit blessed.
All its choice things are desired, and its fruits are sweet:
the figs are rich, the grapes pleasant, the pomegranates ruddy, the apples spiced, the almonds in almond-bloom [Exod 25:33] ⚠16, the dates heady as wine.
And though it is the bitter month that has made the heart bitter,
for in it the Temple was destroyed, and briers and thorns came up [Isa 5:6], and ravens nested in it [Isa 34:11], and the sparrow too found a house [Ps 84:4];
and these thousand one hundred and fifty years we have been driven out like a bird from the nest [Prov 27:8], and masters rule over us, one sells and another buys; therefore we mourn in this month, for its years have grown long and its eyes are heavy with age [Gen 48:10], and we have an old father [Gen 44:20] ⚠17.
He is the man who consumed us and who devised against us [2 Sam 21:5], and drove us out from sharing in the inheritance of the Lord [1 Sam 26:19].
Yet in wine a man forgets his grief, and his turmoil subsides.
So if your heart languishes over this, hear, O sons, the instruction of a father [Prov 4:1].
Take yourselves a bowl, of the gold of Ophir, burnished and polished,
and put in it of the wine a blazing fire [Ps 104:4], that roams through the chambers of the heart and takes up grief ⚠18.
For the son of the vines is prudent and wise, and in driving out griefs a skilful caster of spells [Ps 58:6].
So a man forgets the grief of the month and does not languish, and a wise son makes a glad father [Prov 10:1]."
And he took up his parable and said:

Keep the cup beside you when you are in pain, / and turn the hardships of Av into the wonder of Nisan.
For while you drink your fill of the honey of its mouth, / the leanness of your body departs, and you will not languish.
You will forget the griefs of Av in the sweetness of the son / of the choice vine [Isa 5:2]: a wise son makes a glad father [Prov 10:1].

The sixth answered and said:
"The month of Elul is lovely and perfect, like a banner on a high and lofty mountain [Ezek 17:22].
For then the heart rejoices, and the mountains drip sweet wine [Amos 9:13].
The threshing floors are full of grain [Joel 2:24], and the blessing of the Lord overflows and passes through [Isa 8:8] over the face of the wilderness, the plain and the river; the vats overflow with new wine and oil [Joel 2:24], and every heart flows to the goodness of the Lord [Jer 31:12], for it comes like a river [Isa 59:19].
The gardens flow with honey, and the produce of the field flourishes.
The juices of the vine drip a dew of lights [Isa 26:19], and are sweeter than honey and the honeycomb [Ps 19:11].
Then the hand of the cold begins to blow a cool wind, to restore the souls, and to resettle the abode of joy.
Then the winds awake from the four winds [Ezek 37:9],
and the day breathes [Song 2:17], and Time says: Prophesy to the wind [Ezek 37:9].
And the cupbearer says to his cup: Awaken those dead of grief, and let them drink their fill of your honey; breathe upon these slain, that they may live [Ezek 37:9]."
And he took up his parable and said:

Set the cup as a border between you and grief, / but recline in the garden bed and drink in the days of Elul.
Bow every moment to the branches of the fruit tree, / worship the blossom of the garden and do homage to its produce [Isa 44:19] ⚠19.
And drink your fill of the water of the choice vine, whose waters, if they flowed / into dry wood, would make it yield its fruit.

The seventh answered and said:
"The head of the months is Tishri, when every good thing bears fruit, and every heart soars [Job 39:18] to the height of joy.
God created it to lay open the secret of the months, and it became their head.
In it God established the year of the Jews, and made the moon for appointed seasons [Ps 104:19]; from it the days that are unfit and fit are known ⚠20, and the rest of the fasts and feasts that are named.
In it are the reckonings of the festivals and the seasons, and they shall be for signs and for seasons, and for days and years [Gen 1:14].
Its first day is a day of visitation and remembrance, a day of rank and advantage, a day when creatures pass before God like sheep in single file [m. Rosh Hashanah 1:2]; one recounts and one sings, one finds favor and one finds wrath.
God charged His prophets in a vision that the Jews should be ready against this day [Esth 8:13].
A day when all who are written for life [Isa 4:3] are counted and numbered for peace, and those who deserve death are blotted out of the book of life [Ps 69:29].
A day when God judges His creatures, rich man and wayfarer [2 Sam 12:4], and commands to bring the book of records, the chronicles, and they are read before the King [Esth 6:1].
A day when every man moans in pangs and sorrow, and when he gives account of his deeds he becomes rot in the fire, for a fire has gone out from Heshbon [Num 21:28] ⚠21.
Happy the man whose soul on this day is clean and worthy to see the face of the King, for then it blossoms like a fruitful vine [Ps 128:3], and its table is full of fatness [Job 36:16] and its cup as it should be ⚠22; and then the drinking is sweet and good for every soul that moans.
When the days grow short and the hours grow fewer,
the wine calls to creatures: See, the days are short and the moments few;
so hurry to drink of the mixed wine, for the day is short and the work is great [m. Avot 2:15]."
And he took up his parable and said:

Set the cup before you in Tishri, / and by it you will blossom like a fresh tree and bear fruit.
And if you are faint of heart and sad, / by your wine you will be strong and sound.
Acquire joy for a handmaid, / and you shall have no power to sell her to a foreigner [Exod 21:8].

The eighth answered and said:
"The month of Marḥeshvan is fair and good, and rich and moist with all kinds of delicacies.
For then the heat slackens and turns its back to flee [Josh 7:12], and the troops of winter pursue it,
to take vengeance on the troops of the heat; they smite them and beat them down as far as Hormah [Num 14:45].
For they remember what the month of Nisan did to them, when it mustered its forces against them and subdued their forces.
Therefore they pay it back its due, for the work of a man He will repay him [Job 34:11].
And though the troops of the rain come down upon the earth, and the cold hides men away and makes them houses [Exod 1:21], and the roads are full of terrors [Eccl 12:5] from rain and snow, and Time sends a proclamation to make his servants and his cattle flee into the houses [Exod 9:20],
then the fire is pleasant to every man who draws near to it,
to warm himself in its heat and to gaze upon its loveliness,
and to roast meat; and let the mixed wine not be lacking [Song 7:3].
The roast at your right, and the vessel at your left:
boast yourself in the goodness of the one and take pride in the sweetness of the other; this one sweet and that one bitter; while this one was yet speaking, that one came and said [Job 1:16].
And you take your delight between them, standing like a king between the two.
The daughter of the vines kisses your mouth from time to time, and if your heart takes fright at the greatness of your grief, her breasts will satisfy you at all times [Prov 5:19].
Her taste is sweet and bitter, for she is both the pang and the balm ⚠23: What is sweeter than honey, and what is stronger than a lion [Judg 14:18]?"
And he took up his parable and said:

If you drink your wine in Marḥeshvan, / strengthen your bones [Isa 58:11], and sing out too.
Or if the offspring of the days have sinned against you, / with the cup forgive their transgression and pardon their iniquity [Exod 34:7].
For by it you will level for yourself the paths of joy / that were made hard by the hands of Time's griefs ⚠24.

The ninth answered and said:
"The best of the months is Kislev, for in it the heart is at ease and at rest.
Then the hands of the rain spin their threads of sapphire and jacinth [Exod 28:19], to plant the cedar and the rain to make it grow [Isa 44:14]; and the snow raises its banners on every side, and binds a turban of light on the heads of the mountains.
And before you is a Cushite woman of the daughters of Ham, clothed in the work of coal ⚠25; her heart is angry and hot against the army of the cold, and her army fights against his army,
until she drives into his heart her thorns from her sparks, and her sharp arrows [Ps 45:6].
It is the month of the wonders and miracles that God did for the seed of those He has borne [Isa 46:3],
when the Greeks came against the house of the Lord and surrounded the house [Gen 19:4]; and they carried outside the unclean thing that lay in the house, and the priest commanded, and they emptied the house [Lev 14:36]; and they sought oil to light the lamp and none was found in the house, for all the oils in the house had been defiled; until the priest called to the chamber of the house, saying: Tell me, what have you in the house? And she said: Your handmaid has nothing in the house except a jar of oil [2 Kgs 4:2]. And the joy was great, and great was the glory of the house [Hag 2:9], and they praised God and sang the song at the dedication of the house [Ps 30:1].
Therefore it is fitting for every wise man to rejoice in these days, and to bless God for the fruit of the vine, and to bless Noah, who found us rest in wine,
of whom his parents, our parents, prophesied, saying: This one shall comfort us for our work and for the toil of our hands [Gen 5:29] ⚠26."
And he took up his parable and said:

When grief kindles a burning [Isa 10:16] in your heart, / quench it with the water of the cup in Kislev ⚠27.
And if terrors multiply from evil Time, / with your cup you will be quiet and at ease.
Rejoice in cups, and count their blood / as the soul [Deut 12:23], and your spirit as a body for them ⚠28.

The tenth answered and said:
"The month of Tevet is healing for the languishing soul and for the grieving spirit that lies down in sorrow [Isa 50:11],
when the cold musters its armies and pitches its tents on the face of the ground.
It grows stronger and stronger [Exod 19:19], and before its cold who can stand [Ps 147:17]?
With its strength it shatters the rocks of the ribs, and makes the springs of tears well up [Jer 6:7] from the eyes.
The snow raises its banners on the tops of the mountains, and its troops lie in heaps [Exod 8:10] over the face of the wilderness.
And the fires stand like granaries, and flames go up from them, as though they were men that draw the sword [Judg 20:2], to set battle in array against the cold, to gladden the hearts with it, and to stir up love [Song 2:7].
And though it is the month of the dreadful hardship whose like no man has seen, when all the bright lights in heaven grew dark [Ezek 32:8], the day the king of Babylon laid siege to Jerusalem [Ezek 24:2],
and on the tenth of this month every eye sprinkles tears with blood, and every heart wastes away,
yet with the cup a man forgets his grief and thrusts it away, and wipes it off the tablet of his heart [Prov 3:3].
And if his calamity grows every day and nothing subdues it, at night he binds up his pain with wine; and grief in the morning flourishes and passes, in the evening it is cut down and withers [Ps 90:6].
Wine lulls to sleep the grief of the heart, for it lies close to the ribs and close to the heart;
in its heat it burns up the griefs until they are consumed, and fire comes out from before the Lord and devours them [Lev 10:2]."
And he took up his parable and said:

Keep the daughter of the vines standing beside you, / and rejoice in every good thing in the month of Tevet.
Think of the cup as the water of a river / whose waters are honey, and your tongue draws them up.
Its waters flow as though from a beloved's tongue, / or as though your cup were hewn from his cheeks [Song 5:13].

The eleventh answered and said:
"The month of Shevat is a stronghold and a hope for every man; it is an escape from griefs, and a city of refuge from the troops of sorrow.
Pleasures are at its right hand [Ps 16:11], and every good thing is renewed in its time; its darkness and its blackness [Prov 7:9] grow light, and its eye is opened.
Then the streams spread over the face of the earth, and the brooks run over a pavement of marble [Esth 1:6], and the plants peep out from beneath the earth, and the sleepers of joy awake [Dan 12:2].
The earth brings forth her growth [Isa 61:11], the yield of her months [Deut 33:14], her buds and her flowers; and the sun spreads her wings [Mal 3:20] to light up her twilights, and scatters her sparks to revive her stricken.
The light grows great, and the cold grows weak.
The sun draws near, and the light is pleasant.
The garden beds uncover their faces, and their eyes are opened [Gen 3:7].
And the cup with her wine gladdens those who grieve, and slays the griefs to bring her worriers back to life; for many are the slain she has cast down, and mighty are all her slain [Prov 7:26]."
And he took up his parable and said:

The cup in your hand is your rod in the days of Shevat ⚠29, / striking grief on the head until it flees and totters.
So strip off the garment of calamity and put on joy; / take off the cloak of grief and wrap yourself in ease.
Know that your days upon earth are a shadow [Job 8:9]; / so rejoice in it, for your stay is short.

The twelfth answered and said:
"The month of Adar is comely and glorious, like a necklace filled with pearls; for then the army of joys is set in array, the breach of sighs is fenced up, and the army of grief is lost and gone.
The cold's pride grows weak, and the fury of its tumult ceases.
It sends a proclamation through its camp [Exod 36:6]: Prepare provisions for the way [Josh 1:11] and an allowance, and arise, let us flee [2 Sam 15:14], for this is not the resting place [Mic 2:10].
The cloud scatters across her skies and spins the threads of her rain; the sun lengthens her course in her heavens, and the garden breathes out [Song 4:16] her spices and perfumes.
It is the month of the wonders and miracles that God did for the children He has borne [Isa 46:3],
when Haman the Agagite rose up to kill me, and God heard my cry and understood my meditation [Ps 5:2].
He repaid him as his wickedness deserved, and he was hanged, he and all his seed.
My cry went up to the throne of God; me He restored to my office, and him He hanged [Gen 41:13].
His ten sons went up on the tree like bridegrooms, and the tree called to Haman: Put griefs away from your heart, for I have borne you on the wings of the clouds; and if you and your sons are set upon me, am I not better to you than ten sons [1 Sam 1:8]?
Therefore it is fitting for every wise man to rejoice in it, and in it to forget his sorrow,
and to drink the blood of the cups until he cannot tell ‘blessed be Mordecai’ from ‘cursed be Haman’ [b. Megillah 7b].
And he calls to the daughter of the vines: Hurry and save me, satisfy my mouth with good [Ps 103:5], and pour into all these vessels, and set aside the full ones [2 Kgs 4:4]."
And he took up his parable and said:

Among the shadows of the lily and the splendid blossom, / how glorious is the wine of your cup in the days of Adar!
So drink wine that in the cup looks like / onyx set by a row of carnelian [Exod 28:17] filled with pearl.
Here is the count of your months; I have brought / them out by number for you, and not one is missing [Isa 40:26].

And month by month [Isa 66:23] they would sing the praise of the month, and put on it garments of praise [Isa 61:3] for its clothing, and a royal crown on its head [Esth 2:17].
Their covenant with it was kept and faithful [Ps 89:29], new and old [Song 7:14], from the beginning of the year to the end of the year [Deut 11:12].
Said the teller: And when this composition was heard throughout the land,
the drinkers set it on their heads for a crown, and on their hearts for safekeeping.
And when the report was heard in Damascus, the congregation learned it as they learn the reading of the Shema ⚠30.
They keep its commandments, are exact in its letters, and learn its laws by heart.
Every day they rise early to read it, as in the garden of the palace [Esth 1:5], before a man can tell blue from leek-green [b. Berakhot 9b].
They meditate on it in their morning, before their prayer, and at night their sleep is not sweet until they have read it on their beds; and they teach it from the least of them to the greatest [Jer 31:34], they and their children and their children's children for ever [Ezek 37:25].
