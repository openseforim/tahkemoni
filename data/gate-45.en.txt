Thus says Heman the Ezrahite: I journeyed from Alexandria
to Tiberias,
and there I found Ḥever ⚠1; and when I saw him, my thoughts rejoiced,
and my eyes grew bright.
I held him fast and said to him: "Come with me, please, to my house,
to the abode of my rest,
and I will set my offering before you.
Let us delight ourselves with love [Prov 7:18],
and drink our fill of the blood of grapes [Gen 49:11]."
He came with me
to my dwelling ⚠2.
I set before him a table laid out,
and mixed wine.
And when he had eaten and was full and grown fat [Deut 31:20],
and tongue and tooth were at rest,
he said to me: "It is my duty to return your kindness,
and to repay your deed [Ruth 2:12],
out of what the Creator has given me,
for your kindness to me is great [Ps 86:13].
Now I will tell you the choicest of the pleasant proverbs
that I have heard from the mouth of the wise."
I said to him: "Speak, for I am listening [1 Sam 3:10]."
He said: "We have heard it told,
and our fathers have told us [Ps 44:2],
that a man, one of the greatest of the sages,
the exalted,
loved the proverbs of moral instruction.
At all times he would turn to them,
and from them he never, never turned aside.
One day twenty-four men gathered before him, of the mightiest of the sages, who were named by name [Num 1:17].
He said to them: 'Let each of you recall
a proverb of the proverbs of wisdom,
to give prudence to the simple,
to the young knowledge and discretion [Prov 1:4],
that it may be a teacher to men of understanding,
and teach sinners in the right way [Ps 25:8].'
They said: 'The thing that you have spoken is good [Deut 1:14].'
The greatest of them answered and said: We have been told that a pious man passed by the graves of the dead and said: 'Peace be upon you, dwellers in the land of the shadow of death [Isa 9:1], who dwell in the court of death; tell me what you have found,
and how you bore the burden of the judgment.

You have eaten the fruit of your deeds,
and reaped the seed of your doings.
You set out first,
and we are setting out last.
May God forgive us and you,
and have pity on us and on you.'
Another said: A pious man: his companions were before him,
praising his deeds and his ways.
He lifted his eyes to heaven
and said: 'Master of the worlds, You know of me
what I do not know of myself;
and I know of myself
what these do not know.
I pray, my God, make me as they think me,
and do not punish me for the lies they tell of me.'
Another said: A pious man had this custom: when trouble came upon him, he would say in his prayer: 'May it be Your will that this hardship be for instruction and for remembrance,
and not for punishment and wrath.'
Another said: A pious man: one of his friends died,
and on one of the nights he saw him in a dream upon his bed,
and said to him: 'Tell me what you found in your world,
and where your place is.'
He said to him: 'Do not ask about what we found.
We found the account set out,
and the book open,
and the Judge takes no bribe [Deut 10:17].
And we forgot the ordeal of death for the ordeal of judgment, which is more bitter than death [Eccl 7:26].'
And he took up his parable and said:

Tell our beloved and our friend / how great our grief, how much our pain.
We came to the place of judgment, where a fire kindled / a flame in our souls and in our hearts.
Darkness and gloom and blackness are round / about us on our couches and our beds.
O that we had known all this while / alive, and turned back from the path of our guilt!
Therefore, our friends, take great care / lest a pain like our pain befall you [Lam 1:12].

Another said: An Arab woman's son died.
When she had buried him,
she said: 'My son, my son, you were the loan of a lender,
and the gift of a giver,
and the deposit of a depositor.
Today the lender has collected his loan,
the depositor has taken back his pledge,
and the giver has received back his gift.
May God give me in your place good patience ⚠3,
and repay me with a good reward for what He has taken from me.'
Another said: An Arab woman's son died, and she was soon comforted over him.
The neighbor women said to her: 'How can you be comforted so soon?'
She said to them: 'In being comforted there are two good things:
joy in this world and reward in the world to come.
And in much mourning there are two bad things:
loss of the reward for the hardship,
and punishment for too much grieving.'
Another said: An Arab woman's custom in her prayer was to say: 'Master of the worlds,
if I have sinned against You,
my sin does You no harm;
and if You forgive me, Your forgiveness costs You nothing.
I pray, my God, forgive me what does You no harm,
and give me what costs You nothing.'
Another said: A pious man met one of the princes as he walked about in pride.
The pious man said to him: 'Know that the Creator despises this way of walking.'
The prince said to him: 'If you knew who I am,
you would not have said such a thing to me.'
The pious man said: 'I know you: your beginning is a putrid drop [m. Avot 3:1],
your end is cast-off filth,
and while you are still alive, your carcass is like refuse [Isa 5:25].'
Another said: A man sinned against one of the kings, and they brought him before him.
When he came in, he said to the king: 'I adjure you by God: do not let an enemy whom you have grieved rejoice over me,
and do not grieve a friend whom you have gladdened,
and do not uproot a planting of kindness that you have planted,
and do not tear down a building of justice that you have founded.'
The king had pity on him and let him go.
Another said: One of the sages asked his friend, saying to him: 'How much wine do you drink with your meal?'
He said: 'Three cups.' He said to him: 'You do well, for you do not drink it as drunkards drink,
nor forbid it as Nazirites forbid it.'
Another said: One of the sages fell sick with the sickness of which he would die [2 Kgs 13:14].
He was praying to God to forgive his guilt,
and his companions were praising the virtues that were in him,
and weeping around him.
He said to them: 'Spare me the praise,
and help me with prayer.'
Another said: One of the princes lost his son, and one of the sages came to comfort him and said to him: 'So long as you are the remnant that is left,
we will not grieve over the hardship.
Death has shown us great kindness
in passing over you and not laying its hand on you.'
Another said: A wise man came to comfort one of the princes over his son. He said to him: 'May God give you lasting comfort,
and a reward laid up,
and blessed offspring,
and a steadfast mind [Isa 26:3].'
Another said: One of the poets praised one of the kings in his lifetime,
and lamented him at his death.
His friends said to him: 'Why are your praises better than your laments?'
He said: 'Because my praises were a duty ⚠4,
and my laments a freewill offering.'
Another said: They asked a man to tell the beauty of his beloved.
He said: 'Her belly is like a basin [Song 7:3],
her face a garden,
her lips manna,
her cheeks a lily,
and her stature a fresh green tree:
its dewdrops are the beads ⚠5 of her necklaces,
its blossoms her garments,
and its fruit her two breasts.'
Another said: They asked a man to tell the beauty of his beloved,
and he said: 'There is none like her in all the lands,
unique among the daughters,
and fair among the delicate.
If she stands, she is a reed of gold;
if she sits, she is a loving doe [Prov 5:19];
if she walks, she is a proud dove;
and if she speaks, she kindles a flame in the heart.'
Another said: One of the poets made a poem for one of the princes, and the prince honored him,
and lodged him in his house.
The master of the house had a beautiful daughter, and the poet made love songs and sang them,
while she was listening at the tent door [Gen 18:10].
And he said:

Gazelle of grace, awesome as bannered hosts [Song 6:4], / all glorious are you, like the king's daughter within [Ps 45:14].
I desire you, doe pure and perfect, / and you are listening, and your ear is not stopped.
Turn to me with a look of favor and pity, / and your reward shall be full from God [Ruth 2:12].

When she heard his voice,
she answered and said to him:

Your words are without knowledge and wisdom, / and your song is a song of men of flattery and lewdness.
It bears witness that your lineage is soiled, / and that no honest woman bore you.
So be silent, and go in peace; / and if not, shame shall cover you.

Another said: A pious man was passing on the road and saw one of the lawless hanging on a tree.
The pious man said to his companion: 'Whoever forsakes what is right in his wickedness,
the tree will make straight what he has made crooked [Eccl 7:13].'
Another said: They brought a guilty man before the king, and he ordered him to be flogged with whips.
The man being flogged was very tall,
and the man flogging him was short of stature.
The flogger said to the flogged: 'Lower your body so that I can flog you.'
The flogged man said to him: 'Woe to you, son of a whore! Is it to a feast you are calling me?
As the Lord lives, I would like to be as tall as Og,
and you as short as Gog ⚠6.'
The king laughed and let him go.
Another said: A wise man saw his son looking at a beautiful woman, and said to him: 'My son, my son, what do you love in women? Their bellies are pits,
their breasts are wineskins,
their beauty is nakedness,
and they are the graves of craving [Num 11:34].'
Another said: They asked a villager, saying to him: 'When you go out of your house,
who guards your wife?'
He said: 'I have set two guards over her:
I leave her naked, so that she will not go out with the women,
and hungry, so that she will not make merry with the men.'
Another said: They asked a villager, saying to him: 'Which of the sons-in-law do you want for your daughters?'
He said: 'Sons-in-law are three: the son-in-law of wealth, the son-in-law of lineage, and the son-in-law of the grave.
And the best of them all is the son-in-law of the grave ⚠7.'
Another said: They asked an old man, well on in years [Gen 24:1], about his health and his affairs.
He said: 'I am sick in my belly and in my desire.
The sickness of my belly: when I am hungry, I perish,
and when I am full, I am weary.
And the sickness of my desire: when I rise, it sleeps,
and when I sleep, it rises.'
Another said: One of the poets came to the house of one of the princes,
and knocked, and they did not open the gates.
He said to those standing there: 'He whose right hand is busy with a woman,
and his left with a flask ⚠8,
whose forehead is brass [Isa 48:4],
and whose mother is a harlot,
and whose family is driven out from among men [Job 30:5]:
for the wrong he does to men, shame does not cover him [Obad 1:10].'
Another said: They asked an Arab about a miser, and he said: 'His baseness is past searching out,
his promises are a lie,
his love is like a morning cloud [Hos 6:4], and his clouds are without rain,
and his furrows are parched,
his mother's breasts are dry [Hos 9:14],
and the vines of his goodness are empty [Hos 10:1].
He is like a beast lying down,
eating all that it has gathered,
and swallowing all that it finds.'
Another said: Two warriors boasted against each other.
One said to the other: 'You are no match for me, for I am the son of the man who closed the breaches.'
He said to him: 'What you say is true;
but your father closed them by his flight ⚠9,
and my father closed them with his spear.'"

Said the teller: When I heard the parables of Ḥever the Kenite,
I hid them away in my heart and in my thoughts.
I kept him with me many days,
taking his delight in my house and my hall.
After that he went away, and pained my heart with his parting,
and left inside me his burning coals.
