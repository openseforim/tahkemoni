Thus says Heman the Ezrahite: I was in the land of Egypt,
and at that time it was a delight to the eyes [Gen 3:6],
and comely as Jerusalem [Song 6:4].
Its pastures were full of dainties,
its choice fruits new and old [Song 7:14],
and its gardens fat and green [Ps 92:15],
planted with pleasant plants [Isa 17:10].
It was like a bride with the Nile a necklace on her neck,
as if the world were dark and she the lamp for the light [Exod 35:14].
I found there desirable young men [Ezek 23:6], wise and pleasant,
and they were exceedingly wise [Prov 30:24], expounders of what is sealed,
and revealers of hidden things.
They rend the sea of knowledge into pieces,
and crumble rocks to get at wisdom.
Their figures of speech are like lampstands, and they the cups [Exod 25:31];
or like the lights, and they the firmament.
From the necklace of their mind they scatter splendour to the sky,
and from the cloud of their teaching they drip abundance of rain.
Their speech is to the thirsty as streams of water in a dry place [Isa 32:2],
and their heart, for the prophecy of song, a valley of vision [Isa 22:1].
All of them were pure of heart and thought,
and knew the knowledge of the Most High [Num 24:16].
Among them was an old man who gathered from the testimonies their scattered parts,
and set in the figures of speech their ornaments.
At times he embroidered his writings with embroidery of fine cloth [Exod 31:10],
and spread over them the gold of his mind, in hanging work [1 Kgs 7:29].
At times he came into the sea of understanding to split its breakers with his words,
and to bring out of its chambers its pearls,
and to raise up from its depths
the pearls of its eloquence.
Those who stood before him asked him,
those who drew from his springs,
to teach them the craft of strong eloquence,
and its way, deep and far off,
which the heroes of thought are too weary,
and the young lions of the heart,
and the vanguard of the battles,
to tread in its paths,
and to walk in its tracks;
for it is a path no bird of prey knows [Job 28:7],
and its eloquence a great sea where no galley goes [Isa 33:21].
He said to them:

"I will make all my goodness pass before you [Exod 33:19],
and set the riddles of the testimonies before you.
Now incline your ears, and know that the happy poems,
and the delightful riddles,
and the precious letters,
and the forceful phrases,
have many ways,
and many courses,
in which the guides of the perplexed go astray ⚠1.
And the highest craft,
of those who sit first in the kingdom [Esth 1:14],
is the craft of the letters that turn,
whose bows are drawn backward,
and set out in two ways.
If you read them in their way,
you will find them straight [Prov 8:9];
and if you turn them backward,
you would think the streams of Shihor had compassed you [Ps 18:5],
or the Jordan was turning back [Ps 114:3]."
All the people said to him:

"This is a way no man has passed through [Jer 2:6],
and this craft is a virgin whom no man has known [Gen 24:16].
If you will betroth her to us,
ask of us ever so much dowry and gift [Gen 34:12],
and you shall have a share in our wealth and our substance."
He said to them:

"Come, children, listen to me; I will teach you the fear of the LORD [Ps 34:12].
And I will not accept an offering from your hand [Mal 1:10].
Behold, I will rain down for you bread of delights [Exod 16:4]
from the heavens of the testimonies,
and let you hear a lovely letter, fair and beautiful [Jer 46:20],
like apples of gold in settings of silver [Prov 25:11],
hewn from mountains of sound wisdom,
built for a showpiece [Song 4:4].
She has the grace of a gazelle,
and the terror of a lioness.
She turns like a deceitful bow [Ps 78:57], and has no second in the world.
She is the only one of her mother [Song 6:9],
and a wonder among the children of her people.
The daughters saw her and called her happy [Song 6:9];
noble women heard her fame and praised her.
If a man reads her from the beginning to the end,
his heart will long for her,
and he will find her a lovely praise,
like a modest and pious woman.
But if he reads her from her end to her head,
and turns her backward,
the pious one, the stork, becomes a heron ⚠2,
and the fair speech is turned
to desolation and to splinters [Joel 1:7],
and the praise becomes folly ⚠3,
and the blessing a curse.
By her light she makes the dark places shine,
and turns the sun back ten degrees backward [Isa 38:8],
to make all who see her tremble
at her wonders.
She is black and comely [Song 1:5];
with her mouth she laughs, and her soul is sick.
In her face is humility,
and in her heart perversity.
On her tongue is honeycomb,
and in her inward parts Topheth.
Though she is sweet at her beginning,
her end is more bitter than death [Eccl 7:26].
She has two faces,
like the bread of the faces ⚠4.
Whoever reads her in her way,
and tastes the sweetness of her palate [Song 5:16],
will say: 'This is the bread of the LORD,
and this is the table that is before the LORD [Ezek 41:22].'
But if he turns her round, he will find the bread of the faces taken away from before the LORD [1 Sam 21:7].
For good and evil are joined in her, and this man and that man was born in her [Ps 87:5],
and the children struggled together within her [Gen 25:22].
The one came out a plain man, dwelling in tents [Gen 25:27],
and after that his brother came out [Gen 25:26], worthless and evil in deeds.
This letter
I have set as a chain on the settings of eloquence [Exod 28:14].
In the furnace of my mind I refined her [Isa 48:10],
and paved her with the pearls of my sayings [Song 3:10],
and like a sword in the hand of Time I drew her.
Her scent is the scent of flowing myrrh [Exod 30:23],
and her spice the spice of the merchants.
And among all who tread her path,
and who hear her speech,
there is no man who could make one like her, to smell of it [Exod 30:38].
I made her to chastise the fools with the whip of my tongue [Job 5:21],
and to rouse the base from the sleep of their baseness.
I composed her about one of the princes,
the oppressors,
the quarrelsome governors,
the strange nobles,
the evil shepherds,
because I praised him at first,
and he paid the praise no regard.
Therefore I made him this letter,
to tell it to the generation to come [Ps 78:6].
When it reached him,
and he felt it with his hands,
he thought that my tongue had praised him,
and did not know that it had cursed him.
He who blesses his friend with a loud voice, rising early in the morning, it shall be counted to him a curse [Prov 27:14].
Now incline your ears to her,
and hearken to the wonders of her words."
And he answered and said:

"Our lord, our envoy ⚠5,
who makes great our honour,
who takes away our sighing,
who stirs up our joy,
who puts our grief to flight,
who renews our greatness,
who ends our reproach,
head of our congregation:
his name is known in the isles,
and heard among the nations.
His name has gone out in praise,
and he despises folly,
and wraps himself in uprightness,
and hates riches,
and pursues what is fitting,
and loathes backslidings,
and loves repentance,
and rejects slanders,
and counsels generous deeds,
and spurns oppressions,
and multiplies charities,
and abhors pride,
and weds humility,
and drives out mockeries,
and betroths praises,
and keeps sinners far,
and brings the modest near,
and abhors the arrogant,
and chooses the enlightened,
and rejects fools,
and desires the wise,
and thrusts away guilt,
and pursues integrity,
and loathes sinners,
and loves the God-fearing,
and hates transgressions,
and chooses the teachings,
and despises wilful sins,
and loves faithfulness,
and hates wickedness,
and forgives transgression;
a child of piety,
without rebellion;
beauty
without blemish;
kindness
without disgrace ⚠6:
the prince (So-and-so), may his Maker guard him ⚠7;
may his lamp not go out,
nor his light,
while he lives.
May the Rock of his glory
take away his sighing,
and increase his joy,
and destroy those who cast him out,
and give rest to his friends,
and bring low those who rise against him,
and raise up those who exalt him,
and break his oppressors,
and multiply his helpers,
and curse those who curse him,
and guard his hosts,
and destroy those who hate him,
and lift up his horn,
and cut off his sorrow,
and double his majesty,
and bring low his adversary,
and exalt those who love him,
and subdue his enemies,
and help those who know him,
yes, cut off those who do him evil.
May his Creator
grant him grace!
May our lord never perish!"
And he said to them:

"Here is a perfect praise,
and beneath it the curse lies crouching [Deut 29:19].
See, I set before you this day a blessing and a curse [Deut 11:26].
If you read it in its way and walk in its path, it is a straight way in which you will not stumble [Jer 31:8].
But if you wish to overturn its matters,
and pull down its buildings,
begin from its end and go backward [Gen 9:23]
by the second way,
and say thus: 'Our lord, forever may he perish! May He not grant him
grace, his Creator! ⚠8'
And so go on from its end
until you reach its beginning."
And he said to them:

"And here, besides, are these two lines that turn backward like the letter,
and give a stubborn shoulder [Neh 9:29]."
And he took up his parable and said:

O prince, in you is uprightness, and there is with you ⚠9 / no wrong, and all kindness is found in you.
Discipline without wickedness God has set in you, / for you are truth, a delight without taint.

And he said to them:

"And whoever wishes to read these poems turned round
will say that they are pouring out the wrath of the LORD:

Found in you is disgrace ⚠10, and all wrong / is with you, and there is no uprightness in you, O prince.
A taint without delight, truly, are you; for / God has set in you wickedness without discipline."

He was silent for a while, and said:

"I have lifted up my hand to the LORD, God Most High [Gen 14:22],
who dwells in the height of his hiding place [Hab 3:4],
and who watches every thought,
that this letter
breaks the pride of all the poets.
All the sons of east and west have wearied themselves to reach it,
of the sons of Eber and the sons of Arabia ⚠11;
for hands have not embroidered the like of its settings,
nor have ears heard the like of its eloquence.
The waters of its eloquence prevailed twice over the eloquence of Arabia [Gen 7:19],
and all the high mountains that are under the whole heaven were covered [Gen 7:19].
And whoever knows the tongue of Arabia and the holy tongue, the warp and woof of the two ⚠12,
can taste with his palate the sweet waters of my eloquence,
and divide between the waters and the waters [Gen 1:6]."

Said the teller: We heard, and our heart melted [Josh 2:11],
and the children of our thought were dismayed,
and there remained no more spirit in any man because of him [Josh 2:11].
We were in our own eyes as grasshoppers, and so we were in his eyes [Num 13:33].
We only asked him his name,
and where his place was,
and said to him: "Tell us, this fine gold, from where is it quarried?
And this bright light [Job 37:21], from which heights did its star come forth [Num 24:17]? And wisdom, from where does it come [Job 28:20]?"
He laughed at our words,
and said to us:

I am Ḥever, joiner of praises ⚠13, / and my sayings are the glory of the heads of congregations.
For princes I compose blessings from my mouth ⚠14, / and on the head of my foes they turn into curses.
My sayings give light to the eyes of the blind, / and turn to darkness for the eyes of my enemies.
With me all the daughters of song are wedded, / though to others they are virgins.
And whoever runs to reach my rank, / answer him: "Do not seek great things for yourself [Jer 45:5].
Will you toil and not touch it ⚠15? Therefore / keep your soul [Deut 4:9], and do not go up by steps [Exod 20:23].
Will you run to disgrace your honour, / and to lay bare your shame and your nakedness?"

When I heard his poem, I knew him and recognized him,
that he was Ḥever the Kenite.
I hastened to him and embraced him,
and stayed with him some days,
to spend my years with him in pleasures [Job 36:11],
until the raven of parting cried out between us ⚠16,
and I journeyed from him with a trembling soul,
and Judah went into exile because of affliction and great servitude [Lam 1:3],
and I took with me the good memory of him as provision for the way.
