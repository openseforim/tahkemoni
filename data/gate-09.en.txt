Thus says Heman the Ezrahite: I turned from all my business to sit with the men of understanding in their houses of study,
and to hear the words of the wise and their riddles [Prov 1:6].
And it came to pass one day, when I was with companions,
nobles and lords,
drawing near to the battle-line of poetry,
and their number was thirty.
One of them answered and said: "Whoever goes up the ascents of the lofty songs ⚠1,
and breaks out in brightness from his dawn ⚠2,
let him reveal today before our eyes his rank,
and bare the arm of his eloquence [Isa 52:10],
and the precious splendour of his greatness [Esth 1:4]."
The people said to him: "By what shall the poet and his strength be tested,
and how can we know the man and his talk [2 Kgs 9:11]?"
He said to them: "Know that poems have ways,
and rhymes have paths and courses,
and they have crafts that are fixed ⚠3,
ordered and kept [2 Sam 23:5].
And the choicest of the crafts
is to take a verse from the books of the prophets,
and to build on it a theme lovely and desired."
Now among the guests there was an old man, terrible of appearance [Judg 13:6],
and he sat alone, far from the company.
But he split hearts with the hammer of his words [Jer 23:29],
and put pearls to shame with the pearls of his parables.
His face was covered,
and I could see only his eyes.
He answered and said: "Hear me, men of heart [Job 34:10],
and let not your heart go astray [Isa 57:17].
If it is this way that you seek,
and to come into this gate that you ask,
take from among you the repairer of the breach [2 Kgs 12:6],
and the teacher of righteousness.
And now choose a man for yourselves, and let him come down to me [1 Sam 17:8],
until you know my parables,
and test my words."
A man of the company came near to him and said to him: "If you wish to kindle the fire of the intellect,
make a poem whose end shall be: 'But those who hope in the Lord shall renew their strength' [Isa 40:31]."
And he took up his parable and said:

Son of man, trust in your Rock on the day of your pain, / for in Him the weary find rest.
Those who trust in emptiness labour in vain [Isa 65:23], but / those who hope in the Lord shall renew their strength [Isa 40:31].

The second of the company answered and said to him: "If you wish to wake the sleepers of your thoughts,
make a poem whose end shall be: 'And in the greatness of Your majesty You overthrow those who rise against You' [Exod 15:7]."
And he took up his parable and said:

If you spend your days in the service of God, / you shall be a glory on the head of the sons of your people.
For then your haters will cringe before you [Deut 33:29], / and in the greatness of your majesty you will overthrow those who rise against you [Exod 15:7].

The third answered and said to him: "If you desire to cleanse your poem of every taint and flaw,
make a poem whose end shall be: 'And not for faithfulness have they grown strong in the land' [Jer 9:2]."
And he took up his parable and said:

I look to the right hand and turn to the left [Ps 142:5]; / I see none upright, none to fence up the breach [Ezek 22:30],
but only men of wickedness and men of falsehood, who / not for faithfulness have grown strong in the land [Jer 9:2].

The fourth answered and said to him: "If you would purify your poem of sin and iniquity,
make a poem whose end shall be: 'the putting forth of the finger, and speaking wickedness' [Isa 58:9]."
And he took up his parable and said:

Return, O man, to God before your death, / for your foot has sunk in the mire of the deep [Ps 69:3].
You will prosper when you take away from your midst / the yoke, the putting forth of the finger, and speaking wickedness [Isa 58:9].

The fifth answered and said to him: "If you long to show us the wisdom you have gained,
make a poem whose end shall be: 'What is this dream that you have dreamed?' [Gen 37:10]."
And he took up his parable and said:

Son of man, how long will you shut your eye? / Alas, you have fallen asleep in the bosom of lust.
The vanities of your dreams have ravished your heart [Song 4:9]; / what is this dream that you have dreamed [Gen 37:10]?

The sixth answered and said: "If you wish to lay the bases of your intellect ⚠4,
make a poem whose end shall be: 'but to do justice and to love mercy' [Mic 6:8]."
And he took up his parable and said:

Prepare, O man, to meet your God [Amos 4:12], who / stretched out the sky like a curtain [Isa 40:22] and founded the earth.
For what does your God seek of you / but to do justice and to love mercy [Mic 6:8]?

The seventh answered and said to him: "If your wisdom is like a bride fair of cheek,
make a poem whose end shall be: 'And they laid it on the boil, and he recovered' [Isa 38:21]."
And he took up his parable and said:

Shaddai, the boil of my sins has made my heart sick, / and Your name is my healing and the life of my spirit [Isa 38:16].
My thoughts ⚠5 took the salves of Your good remembrance, / and laid them on the boil, and it recovered [Isa 38:21].

The eighth answered and said to him: "If you choose to reveal your intellect,
make a poem whose end shall be: 'Your fullness and your outflow you shall not hold back' [Exod 22:28] ⚠6."
And he took up his parable and said:

Man, your days have passed in vanity; / how long will you not seek the face of God?
Your eye is full of tears, and therefore / do not hold back your fullness and your tears [Exod 22:28].

The ninth answered and said to him: "If you can grasp the secret that I have set in order in my heart,
make a poem whose end shall be: 'If you will go with me, I will go' [Judg 4:8]."
And he took up his parable and said:

Awake, right hand of God [Isa 51:9], for my heart / is melted like water within me, and I am poured out [Ps 22:15].
If you help me, I shall be helped; / or if you hold up my hand, I shall be held up.
And when on the way I fear the foe, / if you will go with me, I will go [Judg 4:8].

The tenth answered and said to him: "If you wish to rouse your intellect until its righteousness goes forth as brightness [Isa 62:1],
make a poem whose end shall be: 'And the Lord shall inherit Judah as His portion' [Zech 2:16]."
And he took up his parable and said:

When every man puts his fill in the world, / I put my desire in God and keep His law.
And when the sons of men inherit an inheritance of honour, / the Lord has inherited Judah as His portion [Zech 2:16] ⚠7.

The eleventh answered and said to him: "If you long to spread from your intellect the goodness of its spice,
make a poem whose end shall be: 'His eye was not dim, nor his vigour fled' [Deut 34:7]."
And he took up his parable and said:

Son of man, plant trees of uprightness in your heart; / the fruit of your righteousness will grow and give its scent.
And at your death the Rock will make your body strong [Isa 58:11]; / its eye will not grow dim, nor its vigour flee [Deut 34:7].

The twelfth answered and said to him: "If you long to clothe your poem in a robe of delight as its garment,
make a poem whose end shall be: 'It is not good that the man should be alone' [Gen 2:18]."
And he took up his parable and said:

Wisdom is a crown on the head of men of worth; / put away the honour of the world, and by her be helped.
God chose her and said: "It is not good that / man should be alone; I will make him a help" [Gen 2:18].

The thirteenth answered and said to him: "If you wish to grieve all who envy you and make them sick [Isa 53:10],
make a poem whose end shall be: 'And the Lord will take away from you all sickness' [Deut 7:15]."
And he took up his parable and said:

My heart, return to God when you are in distress, / for in Him is the balm for my pangs and the healing of my anguish.
Had you trusted in your Rock in the time of your pangs, / the Lord would have taken away from you all sickness [Deut 7:15].

The fourteenth answered and said to him: "If the rank of your poem is exalted,
make a poem whose end shall be: 'My sheaf arose and also stood upright' [Gen 37:7]."
And he took up his parable and said:

Indeed my tongue, on the day of battles, is counted / a sword, and it has never turned back.
And when the sheaves of the masters of song fall mute ⚠8, / my sheaf arose and also stood upright [Gen 37:7].

The fifteenth answered and said to him: "If you wish to humble all the helpers of Rahab [Job 9:13],
make a poem whose end shall be: 'And they made themselves gods of gold' [Exod 32:31]."
And he took up his parable and said:

Those who shut their hand [Deut 15:7] and hold back their wealth, / on the day of judgment, will they not be burned in the flame?
They forsook their God and served their silver, / and made themselves gods of gold [Exod 32:31].

The sixteenth answered and said to him: "If you wish the scent of your poem to be like the chief spices [Exod 30:23],
make a poem whose end shall be: 'The lines have fallen to me in pleasant places' [Ps 16:6]."
And he took up his parable and said:

When every man is in love with the world, / I am in love with the Rock who dwells on high.
And I say, when my portion is in His law: / the lines have fallen to me in pleasant places [Ps 16:6].

The seventeenth answered and said to him: "If you seek to compose speech after my soul and my heart [1 Sam 2:35],
make a poem whose end shall be: 'And I will rend the enclosure of their heart, and there I will devour them like a lion' [Hos 13:8]."
And he took up his parable and said:

Before my poems the streams of song stood up as a heap [Exod 15:8], / when I say to their deep: Be dry [Isa 44:27].
If I kindle the blaze of song in the east, / my flame sets ablaze those who dwell in the west.
I humble the men of intellect, and I rend / the enclosure of their heart, and there I devour them like a lion [Hos 13:8].

The eighteenth answered and said to him: "If you wish to cleanse your poem of every blemish and falsehood,
make a poem whose end shall be: 'and the rod that was turned into a serpent' [Exod 7:15]."
And he took up his parable and said:

The sweetness of my song roams about without a foot, / and pours forth without tongue or murmur;
and mine is the pen that tears like a lion, / and the rod that was turned into a serpent [Exod 7:15].

The nineteenth answered and said to him: "If you understand the secret of poetry and its mysteries,
make a poem whose end shall be: 'You pierced with his own shafts the head of his warriors' [Hab 3:14]."
And he took up his parable and said:

Stretch out the pen like a rod over the sea [Exod 14:16] / of testimony, until it lays bare its secret and its mysteries.
And strike with it the head of the envier until he is finished, / and you have pierced with his own shafts the head of his warriors [Hab 3:14] ⚠9.

The twentieth answered and said to him: "If you wish to show in your poems the beauty of their words,
make a poem whose end shall be: 'for in the very thing in which they dealt proudly against them' [Exod 18:11]."
And he took up his parable and said:

The eyes of a graceful gazelle wounded my heart / with their bow, for weapons of violence are their weapons [Gen 49:5].
And therefore my eyes wounded his cheeks ⚠10, / in the very thing in which they dealt proudly against them [Exod 18:11].

The twenty-first answered and said to him: "If you wish to spread the waters of your intellect like the river,
make a poem whose end shall be: 'Take heed to yourselves not to go up into the mountain' [Exod 19:12]."
And he took up his parable and said:

Pride is like a mountain, and he who climbs it / will fall, and suddenly crouch to the ground [1 Kgs 18:42].
Therefore, men of intellect and men of heart, / take heed to yourselves not to go up into the mountain [Exod 19:12].

The twenty-second answered and said to him: "If you wish your poem to be sweet to all ears,
make a poem whose end shall be: 'His eyes are like doves beside the channels of water' [Song 5:12]."
And he took up his parable and said:

A fawn fair of look, fair of eyes [1 Sam 16:12], / tears the heart of the lovers in two.
His cheek is channels of the streams of Eden, but / his eyes are like doves beside the channels of water [Song 5:12].

The twenty-third answered and said to him: "If you are well able [Num 13:30] for the craft of poetry,
make a poem whose end shall be: 'and bring it to your father, that he may eat' [Gen 27:10]."
And he took up his parable and said:

With all your heart fear the one who begot you and the one who taught you ⚠11, / and then you will be wise in your deeds and understand.
And gather now in the garden of your intellect the fruit of understanding, / and bring it to your father, that he may eat [Gen 27:10].

The twenty-fourth answered and said to him: "If you love to make the heart of those who hear your poems tremble,
make a poem whose end shall be: 'With fire they shall burn both him and them' [Lev 20:14]."
And he took up his parable and said:

A man caught in the net of his lusts, / for weapons of violence are their weapons [Gen 49:5]:
in the end his soul and his spirit will mourn, / and with fire they shall burn both him and them [Lev 20:14] ⚠12.

The twenty-fifth answered and said to him: "If you will clear a path [Isa 62:10] for the craft of poetry,
make a poem whose end shall be: 'of all that he does to incur guilt by it' [Lev 5:26]."
And he took up his parable and said:

Every man who forsakes the will of his Rock, / his soul will be sick for his guilt,
and he shall find recompense for any one / of all that he does to incur guilt by it [Lev 5:26].

The twenty-sixth answered and said to him: "If you long to rouse my thoughts with your poems,
make a poem whose end shall be: 'Do not take me away in the midst of my days' [Ps 102:25]."
And he took up his parable and said:

A wind of sighs blew in me, until / my skies grew black [1 Kgs 18:45] with the cloud of weeping.
My Rock, heal my pain, and for my sins / do not take me away in the midst of my days [Ps 102:25].

The twenty-seventh answered and said to him: "If you desire with your poems to weary all who would try them,
make a poem whose end shall be: 'He sends out His word and melts them' [Ps 147:18]."
And he took up his parable and said:

For my many sins my tears / grew strong, and my ruin covers them ⚠13;
they froze, but the fire of my heart ⚠14 / sends out its word and melts them [Ps 147:18].

The twenty-eighth answered and said to him: "If you wish the secret of your intellect to be made plain,
make a poem whose end shall be: 'Our God shall come and shall not keep silent' [Ps 50:3]."
And he took up his parable and said:

God, who is found by those who ask for Him [Isa 65:1], / draw up Your people, tossed in a sea of affliction [Isa 57:20] ⚠15.
To execute judgments of vengeance on the enemies, / our God shall come and shall not keep silent [Ps 50:3] ⚠16.

The twenty-ninth answered and said to him: "If you long for your rank to be established [Prov 8:23],
make a poem whose end shall be: 'And the kingdom shall be the Lord's' [Obad 1:21]."
And he took up his parable and said:

Have pity, Shaddai, on a bewildered congregation [Exod 14:3], / a mistress [Isa 47:7], yet trodden down by every foot.
Redeem Your kingdom from the hand of slaves [Lam 5:8], / and the kingdom shall be the Lord's [Obad 1:21].

The thirtieth answered and said to him: "If you wish your poem to be lovely to every heart,
make a poem, and at its end: 'Continue Your lovingkindness to those who know You, and Your righteousness to the upright in heart' [Ps 36:11]."
And he took up his parable and said:

See, Shaddai, how I am bowed down [Ps 38:7], / and forgotten like the dead, out of mind [Ps 31:13].
Continue Your lovingkindness to those who know You, / and Your righteousness to the upright in heart [Ps 36:11].

Said the teller: When my ear heard his eloquence,
my heart and my thoughts were confounded,
and I set my eyes upon him [Gen 44:21],
and behold, it was Ḥever the Kenite,
father of all who dwell in the tents [Gen 4:20] of wisdom,
and worker of wonders of judgment ⚠17.
When I saw the radiance of his face, my own face was unveiled ⚠18,
and the heavens of my grief were rolled up [Isa 34:4].
My heart rejoiced in him,
and its grief and its anger went away,
because another spirit was with him [Num 14:24].
And the two of us stayed in pleasant company,
until a spirit of jealousy passed over our time [Num 5:14],
and parted us.
