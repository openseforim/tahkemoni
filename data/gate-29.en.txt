Thus says Heman the Ezrahite:
I bought myself merchandise and goods ⚠1,
to find in them profit and hope.
And I journeyed to Nineveh,
which at that time shone like the lights of brightness,
a great city before God [Jonah 3:3].
I came to it in the month of Tevet,
when the stormy wind [Ps 148:8] blows.
The season was very cold,
and before His cold who can stand [Ps 147:17]?
The rain had mustered its hosts,
and the snow had raised its banners on the tops of the mountains.
One day I was in the house of prayer
with the congregation,
and behold, a man full of understanding and knowledge,
with torn garments upon him.
He was weeping for the hardness of his day [Job 30:25],
and a little lad was with him [1 Sam 20:35].
The cold was splitting the fountains of his eyes
and shattering the rocks of his teeth.
The wind in its storm
made his hair stand on end [Job 4:15].
The snow made his tongue cleave to his palate
and confounded his speech.
He stood with trembling limbs
among those who stood.
When we had finished the prayer,
he set out praise to God,
and first called out a greeting.
After that he said: "To you I call, who are wrapped in brocade [Ps 45:14],
and who wrap yourselves in robes of state [Isa 3:22],
who wear garments of many colors [Ezek 27:24],
and cover yourselves with rams' skins dyed red [Exod 25:5];
who mock the cold and its hosts,
who laugh at the snow when it raises its banners;
who make your garments a shield
against the chill ⚠2,
and a buckler [Ps 91:4]
against the frost
and the terrible ice [Ezek 1:22];
who dwell in a spacious house [Jer 22:14],
who lie among ladies and more ladies [Eccl 2:8] ⚠3.
You thought there were no rich men but you,
and no glory but your glory.
Fear Time, which pours away like water,
and the turning wheel.
For how often we were clothed in finery [2 Sam 1:24],
and sated with dainties,
and rode well-fed horses [Jer 5:8],
and trailed brocades of scarlet!
We were carried in the bosom and on the shoulders [Isa 49:22],
our clothing was dyed stuff, embroidered on both sides [Judg 5:30],
and our couch had a damsel, two damsels [Judg 5:30].
But when Time changed its manner
and brought out against us the weapons of its wrath [Jer 50:25],
the dainties turned to bitter herbs,
and the feast of fat things became a feast of dregs [Isa 25:6].
In a moment the robes of state became spoil stripped off,
and the brocades were rolled in the mire.
The garments of embroidery became
garments of vengeance,
and the anklets [Isa 3:18]
were trampled.
We stripped off embroidery and scarlet,
and put on the maggot and the worm ⚠4.
Now take heed while it is still in the power of your hand,
before you leave your wealth to others [Ps 49:11],
and go naked of your splendor.
For behold, your eyes see how short the bed is [Isa 28:20],
and how sackcloth and ashes are spread for many [Esth 4:3].
We have come down wondrously [Lam 1:9],
and drunk our fill of hardships.
Our eyes are like the eyes of orphans toward those who pity them,
or like the eyes of mourners toward those who comfort them.
Now, is there among you a son of nobles [Eccl 10:17],
a man of pure stock,
whose heart will soften at our poverty,
whose compassion will stir at our low estate,
who will fear lest what has found us find him,
lest Time make him drunk with the wine of staggering [Ps 60:5], as it made us drunk,
and the cup of grief pass over to him [Lam 4:21], as it passed over us?"
When he had come so far, his tongue cleaved to his palate [Lam 4:4].
He looked about: perhaps there was one who would pity him, with a heart stirred and broken;
but no one spoke a word to him [Job 2:13].
When he saw that his words had not availed,
and the people were like a deaf adder to its charmers' spells [Ps 58:5–6] ⚠5,
he sat on the ground, trembling,
his loins tottering [Ps 69:24].
He said to his lad: "Do whatever your hands find to do [1 Sam 10:7],
for there is none who pities my calamity or yours."
Then the lad rose,
his tongue sharpened like a razor [Ps 52:4].

He said: "O my lords [Gen 44:18], what shall I say?
The pious man has come to an end [Ps 12:2],
the scent of charity is changed [Jer 48:11],
the Almighty has embittered my soul [Job 27:2],
and my heart is in turmoil [Lam 1:20].
Had a man spoken the words you heard today to the sea, it would have split;
and to the rock, it would have cleft;
and to the mountain, it would have been beaten flat;
and to the fire, it would have sunk down [Num 11:2];
and to the serpents
for which there is no charm [Jer 8:17],
they would have listened to the voice of the charmers [Ps 58:6],
and their heart would have been torn in two,
and melted, and become water [Josh 7:5].
Why then is your heart like the flinty rock [Deut 8:15],
that will not move from its place [Isa 46:7]?
God save us from hard hearts,
from feeble souls,
from closed hands,
and from deaf ears;
from these hearts
hewn out of rock,
and the thoughts
harder than stones,
and the strong hand
that is empty of kindness,
whose spring has turned to dry land,
and whose owners have a brow of brass [Isa 48:4].
Behold, your ears have heard today
the like of which you have not heard in your days,
yet you hide your eyes from seeing and turn your faces to the other side,
as though this commandment were not for you,
or this rebuke were for others.
Now let every one of you remember his Maker,
and break the hardness of his inclination,
and let his heart and his inmost parts soften toward him ⚠6,
and let him not shut his hand against me [Deut 15:7];
let him do today what his child will find tomorrow,
and fear lest there befall his son tomorrow what has befallen me,
and he turn back to beg a morsel of bread, as I do,
and Time make him need hard men, as it has made me need them."

The narrator said: When the people heard the words of the son,
their heart, which had been like stone, split open.
They marveled at the force of his tongue,
and all of them gave freely to the father and to his child,
each according to the gift of his hand [Deut 16:17].
And when his sack was full,
and his need supplied,
the father took up his parable and said [Num 23:7]:

May God have mercy on the nobles who had mercy on me / with their gifts, their hearts soft toward my calamity.
At first their heart toward me was / like adamant [Zech 7:12], and none took me by the hand.
But the Rock turned their heart to me, and they pitied / my old age and the smallness of my child.

The narrator said: When he had finished his words
and completed his poems,
I looked at him closely,
and behold, it was Ḥever the Kenite himself,
and the lad was his son,
the planting of his garden.
I knew his cunning and his wiles,
and understood that the lad was his net and his snare.
But I did not make myself known to him,
lest the cunning of his deeds be revealed in the congregation;
and I left him and turned away from him.
